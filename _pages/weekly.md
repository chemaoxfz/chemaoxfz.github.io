<article class="card mb-3"><div class="card-body"><h3 class="h5 card-title"><a href="https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.01.755942" rel="noopener noreferrer">Intermediate phenotypic heterogeneity creates an optimal coexistence window in bacteria–phage communities</a></h3><p class="text-muted mb-2">Jung-Ho Kim, Mattia Mattei, David Soriano‐Paños, Àlex Arenas</p><p class="small text-muted">2026-10-02 · bioRxiv (Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory)</p><p><strong>TL;DR.</strong> A trait-based modeling study finds that phage richness in bacteria-phage communities is maximized at an intermediate level of host growth-rate heterogeneity rather than increasing monotonically.</p><p><strong>Key signal.</strong> Optimal phage diversity emerges from a trade-off between niche differentiation, which increases with host phenotypic heterogeneity, and reduced host abundance in each phage&#x27;s preferred niche, with the optimum set by the match between host phenotypic dispersion and phage niche width.</p><p><strong>Why it matters here.</strong> Connects to Fangzhou&#x27;s work on microbial population dynamics and community ecology and to growth-rate heterogeneity in bacterial physiology, providing a quantitative principle for how intraspecific phenotypic variation shapes coexistence and biodiversity.</p><div class="mt-2"><span class="badge badge-light mr-1">Microbial physiology, growth &amp; division</span><span class="badge badge-light mr-1">Microbial populations &amp; communities</span> <a class="btn btn-sm btn-outline-primary" href="https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2026/10/02/2026.10.01.755942.full.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">PDF</a></div></div></article>
