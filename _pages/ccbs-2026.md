### About the course

Three schools provide our tools: **physics** asks what life is as an object, **systems** asks how it works as a machine, and **industry** asks how to make it useful. We learn to estimate, model, simulate and reason about biological systems, from molecules to cells and populations. No prior background is required.

Lectures 1–7 build the tools. Lecture 8 develops the course's central research position: the cell as a machine composed of binding and catalysis. Lectures 9–16 apply and question that framework. Throughout, use AI to investigate and build, then verify the claims, reproduce the calculations and explain the result yourself.

### Assignments

The ordinary weights are unchanged. Full instructions and deadlines are in the [course guide]({{ '/assets/ccbs/2026fall/intro.html#s8' | relative_url }}).

| Assignment | Weight | When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Teaching:** prepare, rehearse and teach one point, then submit its exposition. One excellent point can earn full ordinary marks. | 40% | Your assigned lecture. Rehearse Friday for Tuesday, Monday for Thursday. Final materials the day before. |
| **Extension mini-essays:** one point taken beyond the week's lectures, clearly explained, illustrated and sourced. Best 6 of 8, five points each. [Short worked example]({{ '/assets/ccbs/2026fall/extension-example.html' | relative_url }}). | 30% | Mondays 23:59; see the [eight dates]({{ '/assets/ccbs/2026fall/intro.html#s9' | relative_url }}). |
| **Research essay:** a standalone HTML essay with a clear problem, challenge, approach, proof and significance. | 20% | 5 November, 23:59 |
| **Participation:** questions, discussion, exercises and peer ratings. | 10% | Throughout the course |

The optional teaching bonus is **five points on the 100-point total**, at most once, with the final total capped at 100. The entire approved contribution is graded normally, including additional points. This does not change the weekly extension mini-essay assignment.

Submit by **fork → add your file → pull request** to the [submission repository](https://github.com/chemaoxfz/ccbs-2026-submissions). Passing checks normally merge the PR automatically. A merge records receipt, not a grade, approval of teaching scope or website publication. Use this schedule for current teaching assignments if older repository records differ.
