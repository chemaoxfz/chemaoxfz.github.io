How reaction orders became a language for biological systems

The same relative derivative has served different questions: fitting occupancy, approximating rates, distributing control, and constraining dynamics.

CCBS · Lecture 11 · lineage · 19 October 2026 · Prepared with Codex assistance

A shared mathematical expression does not make its scientific uses identical. The ratio of fractional changes appears in enzyme kinetics, biochemical systems theory, metabolic control analysis and structural circuit analysis. Each programme specifies a different experiment or mathematical object. A rate response with other concentrations fixed is different from a network flux response after every internal pool has readjusted.

The historical question is how those different uses became useful. The account below follows problems and assumptions in the primary sources. The exposition supplies the full calculations. The core uses them to connect reaction orders to stability, adaptation and regime consistency.

Part I

A slope needs an observable and a mechanism

Two early binding problems show why an exponent can describe a curve without uniquely naming its molecular cause.

1Enzyme occupancy makes the apparent reaction order depend on concentration

Michaelis and Menten sought a rate equation for invertase acting on sucrose. Their 1913 paper connected cleavage to the amount of enzyme-sucrose complex. It also included competition from glucose and fructose. The mathematical starting point was allocation of a finite enzyme pool among free and bound states. Their derivation explicitly used free sugar concentrations, with the approximation that those concentrations were practically equal to the corresponding totals.1

The simple saturating law is one consequence of that allocation. Suppress product competition for the following present-day illustration. Let SS denote free substrate, qEq_E the fixed enzyme total, and KdK_d the binding dissociation constant. Fast binding equilibrium gives CES=qES/(Kd+S)C_{ES}=q_ES/(K_d+S). Let Vmax⁡V_{\max} be the maximal catalytic flux. A catalytic output proportional to the complex then has the form

v=Vmax⁡SKd+S,dlog⁡vdlog⁡S=KdKd+S.v=\frac{V_{\max}S}{K_d+S},\qquad\frac{d\log v}{d\log S}=\frac{K_d}{K_d+S}.

The apparent order falls from one to zero as substrate fills the enzyme pool. The microscopic binding stoichiometry need not change. The order changes because the same molecular mechanism allocates its enzyme differently.

The original model already makes the measured concentration consequential. Treating free substrate as total substrate is an approximation about abundance and sequestration. It is not part of the definition of a reaction order. Lecture 11 returns to that distinction when enzyme and substrate have comparable totals.

The source used here is the English translation supplied with Johnson and Goody's 2011 historical analysis. Its equations and wording belong to the translated 1913 paper. The logarithmic derivative above is calculated here from the simplified closure. It is not attributed as an equation written by Michaelis and Menten.

Read a changing reaction order as a statement about the occupied states and measured variable before interpreting it as a change in molecular stoichiometry.

2A useful fitted exponent need not identify the microscopic binding scheme

Hill's 1910 problem was the oxygen dissociation curve of haemoglobin. He considered aggregation as a possible explanation for the shapes measured in different solutions. After examining mixtures of aggregation states, he introduced the familiar two-parameter form as a convenient way to fit the observations. He explicitly declined to assign a direct physical meaning to the fitted constants at that stage.2

The dependent variable determines what the fitted exponent measures. Write fractional occupancy as θ=Sn/(Kn+Sn)\theta=S^n/(K^n+S^n). The exponent is the logarithmic slope of occupancy odds. The logarithmic slope of occupancy itself is different:

dlog⁡[θ/(1−θ)]dlog⁡S=n,dlog⁡θdlog⁡S=n(1−θ).\frac{d\log[\theta/(1-\theta)]}{d\log S}=n,\qquad\frac{d\log\theta}{d\log S}=n(1-\theta).

At half occupancy, the second slope is n/2n/2. Near saturation, it approaches zero even when the fitted exponent stays fixed. These are two exact derivatives of one chosen curve.

The historical caution remains useful in a modern circuit. A Hill fit can summarize a response well while leaving several microscopic mechanisms compatible with the data. Cooperative binding, molecular titration, hidden states and downstream processing can produce steep responses under different conditions. Distinguishing those mechanisms requires measured pools and interventions beyond a single fitted curve.

Name the transformed observable behind a fitted exponent, then ask which additional experiment could distinguish its possible mechanisms.

Part II

Move from an isolated component to a responding network

A component's local slope becomes a system response only after the other pools are allowed to move.

3Higgins made the primary perturbation part of the response coefficient

Higgins asked how sequential reaction mechanisms could be tested through relative responses. In 1963 he considered a system in a stationary state. Changing a controlled variable makes intermediate concentrations and velocities readjust. He defined a coefficient comparing relative changes and then took the infinitesimal limit to obtain a normalized derivative.3

The route of perturbation was part of the notation. Higgins distinguished the variable deliberately changed from the variables that responded. He used an extra index to identify that primary variable. The same pair of observed variables could have different response coefficients when a different primary intervention moved the system between stationary states.

This is a physical distinction. Increasing total enzyme can redistribute bound substrate. Raising a reservoir substrate concentration changes a boundary condition. Multiplying a catalytic frequency changes an activity. Even if all three interventions increase one observed rate, they need not move the rest of the system along the same path.

Normalized derivatives provide a calculus once the intervention is fixed. Along one regular response path, the ordinary chain rule becomes a product rule for relative responses. But switching the held-fixed quantities changes the derivative itself. A response coefficient cannot be interpreted from its numerator and denominator alone.

Record what was deliberately perturbed and which other variables were free to readjust when comparing response coefficients.

4Savageau used power laws to make nonlinear biochemical balances calculable

Savageau's 1969 biochemical systems analysis addressed the difficulty of solving nonlinear networks. Component enzyme rates were often rational functions of concentrations. In Part II, he examined their log-log slopes and used power-law approximations to represent an operating point or concentration range. He then grouped the positive and negative contributions to each pool.4

The approximation turns positive steady balances into linear equations in logarithmic concentrations. In modern notation, take

q˙i=αi∏jqjgij−βi∏jqjhij.\dot q_i=\alpha_i\prod_jq_j^{g_{ij}}-\beta_i\prod_jq_j^{h_{ij}}.

At a positive steady state, divide addition by removal and take logarithms:

∑j(gij−hij)log⁡(qj/qref)=log⁡βiqref∑jhijαiqref∑jgij.\sum_j(g_{ij}-h_{ij})\log(q_j/q_{\mathrm{ref}})=\log\frac{\beta_iq_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\sum_j h_{ij}}}{\alpha_iq_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\sum_j g_{ij}}}.

The reference concentration makes the logarithms dimensionless. The matrix of exponent differences is the predecessor of the balance-order matrix used in the present lecture. A nonsingular exponent-difference matrix gives one positive candidate for that monomial model.

The source does not make an arbitrary rational rate a global power law. Savageau discussed fitting over a concentration range and the dependence of approximation parameters on the operating point. The useful simplification carries a range of validity. A local tangent and a fitted finite-range approximation can also choose their parameters differently.

Aggregation is another substantive modeling decision. The order of a sum of positive fluxes is their flux-weighted average. Replacing that sum by one monomial fixes an aggregate slope over a chosen range. The resulting exponent is not automatically the stoichiometry of any individual elementary reaction.

Use logarithmic balances to solve a power-law model, then retain the operating range and aggregation assumptions that made that model possible.

5Metabolic control analysis separated local elasticity from control of the whole system

A strongly responsive enzyme need not strongly control the pathway flux. Kacser and Burns's 1973 account asked how changing an enzyme affects a system whose metabolites can readjust. It distinguished that settled response from the isolated enzyme response measured with surrounding pools fixed. The version inspected here is the 1995 annotated reprint, which updates terminology and adds commentary.5

Heinrich and Rapoport developed quantitative control and effector measures in a linear-chain setting. Their 1974 general-properties paper took enzyme velocities linear in substrate concentrations and analyzed linear enzymatic chains. They stated those simplifying assumptions explicitly. Their objective was to make the importance of enzymes and effectors quantitatively interpretable.6

A two-step example makes the distinction calculable. Hold reservoir concentrations S>PS>P fixed. Let the intermediate concentration be XX, with effective forward net rates v1=a(S−X)v_1=a(S-X) and v2=b(X−P)v_2=b(X-P). The positive coefficients a,ba,b have inverse-time units and represent independently scalable activities. Steady flux equality gives

X∗=aS+bPa+b,J=aba+b(S−P).X^*=\frac{aS+bP}{a+b},\qquad J=\frac{ab}{a+b}(S-P).

At fixed intermediate concentration, the first component rate is proportional to aa. After the intermediate readjusts, its flux control coefficient is

C1J:=∂log⁡J∂log⁡a=ba+b,C2J:=∂log⁡J∂log⁡b=aa+b.C_1^J:=\frac{\partial\log J}{\partial\log a}=\frac b{a+b},\qquad C_2^J:=\frac{\partial\log J}{\partial\log b}=\frac a{a+b}.

Equal activities share control equally. Making the first step much faster transfers most control to the second. The isolated component order remains one. These are different experiments on the same chosen model.

Distinguish a component elasticity at fixed neighboring pools from the control coefficient of the settled network.

6Reder separated stoichiometric structure from kinetic sensitivity

Reder's 1988 structural approach asked which response relations follow from the reaction scheme. Conservation laws and branches make a single unstructured Jacobian an awkward description. Her formulation retained independent concentration coordinates and separated the stoichiometric constraints from the derivatives of the rate laws.7

The resulting structure was more than one familiar summation rule. Reder characterized simple concentration and flux control matrices through kernel directions of the stoichiometric matrix and directions generated by independent concentration changes. Some identities depend only on stoichiometry. Others combine stoichiometry with kinetic derivatives. Her Theorem 5 gives both sets together.

A real external perturbation enters through its effects on the rate vector. If a parameter changes several rates, the local response can still be calculated from that known pattern of rate changes. A separate, perfectly specific enzyme parameter for every reaction is not required for every use of the structural sensitivity formulation.

The source's simple control matrices use ordinary derivatives. Normalized control coefficients require an additional scaling. Keeping that distinction prevents a statement about one matrix from being silently applied to another.

Lecture 11 uses the same discipline when it removes conservation constraints before linearization. The chosen state variables must be independent. A zero mode caused by a retained conserved total is different from a physical instability or an adaptive cancellation. The structural bookkeeping decides what can sensibly be inverted.

Separate independent state coordinates, stoichiometric constraints and kinetic derivatives before interpreting a control or stability matrix.

7Giersch asked when adding enzyme really acts as homogeneous rate scaling

A summation theorem depends on what the enzyme intervention does. Giersch's 1988 analysis related classical control-coefficient sums to homogeneity. If all independently scalable activities are multiplied by the same positive factor and the selected steady metabolite state is unchanged, flux scales by that factor. Euler's theorem then turns that scaling into a sum of normalized derivatives.8

Physical enzyme addition can change more than an activity multiplier. Giersch explicitly discussed the substrate bound by newly added enzyme and the resulting changes in free pools. When enzyme abundance is comparable to substrate abundance, sequestration can alter the rate dependence on total enzyme. This concern does not invalidate general local sensitivity analysis. It questions a particular identification of experimental enzyme addition with homogeneous rate scaling.

The one-pair binding calculation makes that concern exact. For E+S⇌k−k+CESE+S\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{ES}, define Kd=k−/k+K_d=k_-/k_+, qE=E+CESq_E=E+C_{ES} and qS=S+CESq_S=S+C_{ES}. If the catalytic flux is proportional to the complex, changing enzyme total at fixed substrate total gives

∂log⁡v∂log⁡qE∣qS,Kd=Kd+SKd+E+S.\left.\frac{\partial\log v}{\partial\log q_E}\right|_{q_S,K_d}=\frac{K_d+S}{K_d+E+S}.

The order approaches one when free enzyme is small compared with Kd+SK_d+S. It need not equal one at finite comparable pools. By contrast, varying a catalytic multiplier at fixed binding state gives order one exactly.

Saturation and sequestration answer different questions. Substrate saturation asks whether S≫KdS\gg K_d. Negligible substrate redistribution asks whether changing enzyme barely changes free substrate. Both can hold together. Zero-order substrate operation alone does not establish a failure of degree-one enzyme scaling.

State the intervention behind a control theorem, then test whether physical enzyme addition preserves its assumed free pools and scaling law.

Part III

Use regimes to connect local calculations with multiple behaviors

Approximations become more useful when their own validity conditions are part of the calculation.

8Design-space analysis makes candidate validity an explicit step

Different dominant balances can produce qualitatively different states of one system. Savageau and colleagues' 2009 design-space construction selected a dominant positive and negative term for each balance. Each selected system yields an analytically tractable candidate. The candidate must then satisfy the dominance conditions used to derive it.9

The concentration inequalities become parameter inequalities after substitution. A low-state solution obtained under weak activation is credible only if its own concentration keeps activation weak. Testing several solutions requires intersecting their conditions at one common parameter choice. Listing three solutions of three incompatible approximations does not establish tristability.

The method also identifies where the approximation becomes vulnerable. Near a boundary, two terms can be comparable. The dominant-term model then omits a contribution that matters. The 2009 paper discusses discrepancies near such boundaries. A full smooth calculation is needed when the claimed behavior depends on that region.

The autoactivator in the exposition follows this logic. It solves the basal, unsaturated and saturated balances, substitutes every candidate into its regime inequalities, and compares the common asymptotic range with exact smooth-model fold boundaries. The historical method supplies the discipline of the calculation. The particular normalized numbers in the exposition are present-day model choices.

Treat a candidate's validity inequalities as part of its result, and test coexistence by intersecting conditions at one parameter vector.

9Structural circuit analysis uses virtual targets to organize motion between regimes

Marken, Xiao and Murray's 2020 preprint asked for a geometric language suited to synthetic circuits. Their examples reduced different saturation regimes to monomial addition and removal laws. Regime fixed points could lie inside their own validity region or outside it. The outside points were used as virtual targets that help organize transitions.10

A virtual target is a property of a continued approximation. Continue one regime's leading vector field beyond the region where it accurately approximates the full model. Its fixed point can then indicate a direction of motion while remaining physically inconsistent with that regime. The actual trajectory changes its local rates as it crosses a boundary.

The preprint states the remaining mathematical problem. Its Discussion identifies the need for rigorous connections between approximate regime dynamics and the original smooth system, particularly near regime borders. This limitation matters when a diagram of target destinations is used to suggest an oscillation.

The present course keeps the geometric construction and checks the full rate law separately. In the equal-clock three-node Hill repression model, a cooperativity-two local slope remains below the oscillatory instability threshold at every finite input scale. A cooperativity-three model can cross it. Similar regime destination patterns therefore need not produce the same full-system behavior.

A chosen-clock calculation must also be separated from a structural claim. The preprint's all-high repressilator calculation on page 13 uses the spectrum of the balance-order matrix, which equals its Jacobian under common unit removal rates. That calculation does not establish instability under arbitrary positive turnover scalings. The same three-node balance-order matrix can be unstable for equal clocks and stable for unequal clocks. The 2021 diagonal-certificate formulation supplies the relevant structural test.1011

Use virtual targets to propose transitions, while keeping first boundary exits, recurrence and attraction as separate full-system questions.

Part IV

Constrain dynamic function through molecular structure

The final connection requires two calculations: what orders do to dynamics, and what orders a molecular mechanism can attain.

10The 2021 structural-control formulation separates reaction orders from turnover times

Xiao, Khammash and Doyle asked which circuit properties survive uncertain kinetic scales. Their 2021 paper expressed positive addition-removal dynamics through logarithmic rate derivatives. At a positive balanced equilibrium, the ordinary Jacobian is similar to a balance-order matrix multiplied by inverse turnover times. Here qi∗q_i^* is an independent pool, fi∗f_i^* its balanced positive flux, H+,H−H^+,H^- the addition and removal reaction-order matrices, and Λ\Lambda denotes a diagonal matrix of its subscripted entries.11

A=Λq∗Λτ∗−1HΛq∗−1,H=H+−H−,τi∗=qi∗fi∗.A=\Lambda_{q^*}\Lambda_{\tau^*}^{-1}H\Lambda_{q^*}^{-1},\qquad H=H^+-H^-,\qquad\tau_i^*=\frac{q_i^*}{f_i^*}.

The separation makes a precise robustness question possible. Holding the balance-order matrix fixed, ask whether every constant positive turnover scaling remains Hurwitz. This is multiplicative D-stability. A positive diagonal matrix PP satisfying PH+HTP≺0PH+H^{\mathsf T}P\prec0 gives a sufficient certificate. The negative quadratic form supplies the argument. A collection of stable simulations at selected clocks would supply less.

The same paper gives a sufficient test for structural instability. Theorem 7 requires a diagonal PP with at least one negative entry satisfying the strict Lyapunov inequality, with no eigenvalues of HH on the imaginary axis. The inertia argument then certifies instability for every positive turnover vector. A full symmetric matrix with negative eigenvalues can certify instability for selected clocks, but it does not supply that structural conclusion. For the all-high repression matrix, the diagonal entries of the inequality force all diagonal weights to be positive, so this sufficient structural-instability test cannot succeed.11

The input-output formulation separates stable return from steady cancellation. The paper writes four order blocks in positions corresponding to the classical state, input and readout matrices. The blocks HA,Hb,Hc,HdH^A,H^b,H^c,H^d describe state effects on balances, input effects on balances, state effects on the readout, and direct input effects on the readout. With a nonsingular state block, the normalized steady gain is

Gyw=Hd−Hc(HA)−1Hb.G_{yw}=H^d-H^c(H^A)^{-1}H^b.

For one input and output, this can be tested through the augmented determinant. The present exposition keeps stable settling explicit and distinguishes local cancellation from a constant set point over an interval. These qualifications determine what experiment the formula can predict.

The term structure has acquired a specific scope here. It includes fixed reaction-order patterns or regimes, rather than every molecular detail or every conceivable parameter change. Changing a parameter can move the state into a new binding regime and thereby change the reaction orders themselves. Such a change lies outside a fixed-order robustness statement.

Read a structural stability or adaptation claim together with the order pattern, equilibrium assumptions and class of kinetic variations it holds fixed.

11Binding-catalysis asks which reaction orders the mechanism can actually supply

A dynamical order pattern must be realizable by molecular allocation. The 2021 paper's finite binding pair already demonstrates the issue. The complex's enzyme-total and substrate-total orders lie jointly in a triangle. The common dilute-enzyme approximation covers only part of that response geometry. Free-species readouts obey different constraints.11

The course's broader binding formulation turns the question into a constraint derivative. Write constituent conservation as q=Lxq=Lx and compatible equilibrium relations as Nlog⁡x=log⁡kN\log x=\log k. At a positive state, relative differentiation gives

∂log⁡x∂log⁡(q,k)=(Λq−1LΛxN)−1.\frac{\partial\log x}{\partial\log(q,k)}=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_q^{-1}L\Lambda_x\\N\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

The exposition states the rank and compatibility assumptions and proves local invertibility. The formula gives a useful local response without solving an explicit equilibrium polynomial. The family of these responses over feasible states is the next object to characterize.

The geometric claim depends on which object is being plotted. Composition shares are fractions of conserved totals. An inverse matrix of those shares is a nonlinear response map. A single output row is a projection of that map. A triangle for one complex-output row does not prove convexity of the whole inverse-matrix image. Molecular states also correlate different entries, which matters when several outputs jointly enter a dynamical test.

The historical threads now meet in one experimental requirement. Declare the intervention as Higgins required. Keep the approximation range as the power-law programme required. Distinguish component and system response as metabolic control analysis required. Check candidate validity as design-space analysis required. Include turnover times and molecular feasibility before making a dynamic-function claim.

The next discriminating test is to measure both free and bound responses while changing the same independent totals. A mechanism that matches a total-output curve can still fail the free-output prediction. Those paired measurements connect the historic care about interventions to a present-day test of molecular structure.

Place a new reaction-order claim by its intervention, system level, validity region and feasible molecular readout before transferring it to a different question.
