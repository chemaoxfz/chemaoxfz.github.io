What would establish a switch, a memory or a clock?

Choose an observable and an intervention that distinguish the proposed behavior from its closest alternative.

CCBS · Lecture 9 audience extension
Editorial synthesis of four Week 5 student contributions · prepared with Codex assistance · 19 October 2026

A steep curve, a lasting response and a periodic trace each leave a mechanistic question open. The core shows how binding and feedback can construct switches, multistable circuits and oscillators. The audience contributions ask what would establish those functions in an experiment. A binding curve needs a signaling rule. A lasting effect needs a persistence test. A memory needs a writing protocol. An oscillator needs a separate test of synchronization.

We follow those four questions in order. Each question changes the input, the readout or the observation window while keeping the underlying model explicit. The result is a set of experiments that can challenge a proposed mechanism. The exposition develops the circuits themselves, and the lineage locates their historical evidence.

Names link to accepted, commit-pinned submissions. All numerical model parameters below are illustrative. Published biological observations are identified separately. The calculations and regenerated figures are editorial synthesis.

Part I

Specify what the experiment calls a response

Occupancy is a molecular state. Signaling is an output assigned to that state.

1Connect binding occupancy to the measured signaling output

The same binding distribution can produce different signaling curves. Fei Wang raises this question through the insulin receptor: must every binding site be occupied before signaling begins? That question identifies an assumption separate from affinity or cooperativity. Ha and Ferrell explicitly compare two such output rules in their analysis of negative cooperativity.1

Begin with a generic receptor unit that can bind two ligand molecules. Let II denote free ligand. An unbound receptor is RR, and its singly and doubly bound complexes are CRIC_{RI} and CRI2C_{RI_2}. Bare species symbols denote free concentrations. The totals count receptor units and ligand molecules:

qR=R+CRI+CRI2,qI=I+CRI+2CRI2.q_R=R+C_{RI}+C_{RI_2},\qquad q_I=I+C_{RI}+2C_{RI_2}.

Define the binding model by its statistical weights. Choose positive site dissociation constants Kd,1K_{d,1} and Kd,2K_{d,2}, with the site multiplicity included as follows:

Z=1+2IKd,1+I2Kd,1Kd,2,f0=1Z,f1=2I/Kd,1Z,f2=I2/(Kd,1Kd,2)Z.Z=1+\frac{2I}{K_{d,1}}+\frac{I^2}{K_{d,1}K_{d,2}},\qquad f_0=\frac1Z,\quad f_1=\frac{2I/K_{d,1}}Z,\quad f_2=\frac{I^2/(K_{d,1}K_{d,2})}Z.

Here fjf_j is the fraction of receptor units with jj bound ligands. Negative cooperativity means Kd,1<Kd,2K_{d,1}<K_{d,2} in this convention. If the experiment controls total ligand, solve qI=I+qR(f1+2f2)q_I=I+q_R(f_1+2f_2) for free ligand before predicting the fractions. Receptor-bound ligand can substantially deplete the free pool.

The output requires one more specification. Normalize the doubly bound receptor's signal to one, assign zero signal to the unbound receptor, and let ϵ\epsilon be the singly bound receptor's relative activity. Then

Sϵ=ϵf1+f2,0≤ϵ≤1.S_\epsilon=\epsilon f_1+f_2,\qquad 0\leq\epsilon\leq1.

The choice ϵ=0\epsilon=0 makes double occupancy necessary for activity. The choice ϵ=1/2\epsilon=1/2 makes each occupied site contribute independently. Neither choice follows from the binding weights. In particular, changing the output rule leaves every occupancy fraction unchanged.

The input coordinate also changes the apparent sharpness. For the double-occupancy output, define D10D_{10} and D90D_{90} as the doses giving 10% and 90% response. The width-based index heff=log⁡81/log⁡(D90/D10)h_\mathrm{eff}=\log81/\log(D_{90}/D_{10}) equals the exponent for an exact Hill curve. In Figure 1's model, it is about 1.00 when dose means free ligand. It is 6.73 when dose means total ligand. The latter interval is qI/qR=1.083q_I/q_R=1.083 to 2.080. This is an input-depletion effect, not a change in the microscopic binding constants.

The insulin receptor provides a biological reason to measure the state-to-output relation rather than assume it. Li and colleagues resolved partially liganded receptor structures and measured receptor autophosphorylation and downstream signaling. Site-2 insulin mutants produced weakened and delayed activation, consistent with partially active asymmetric receptor states. Their full receptor accommodates up to four insulins and has structural features absent from the two-occupancy model above. The study supports partial activation. It does not assign the illustrative value ϵ=1/2\epsilon=1/2 to the model.2

A discriminating experiment measures binding and signaling together. Change total ligand at a fixed receptor total, estimate the occupancy fractions, and ask whether the signaling curve is explained by one set of state activities. A fitted binding curve alone cannot determine those activities.

Before calling a response ultrasensitive, state the controlled dose and the activity assigned to each molecular state. Test the binding model and the signaling rule as separate hypotheses.

Part II

Establish a memory and determine how to write it

A persistent observation concerns duration. Bistability concerns distinct attracting states under the same final conditions.

2Separate slow molecular turnover from bistable persistence

A lasting effect after an input disappears need not imply bistability. Xinyu Wu brings target persistence into the discussion through a hypothetical covalent modification. A modified target can remain after the modifying compound has been washed away. The remaining molecular stock supplies a simple alternative to a feedback-maintained state.

Specify a turnover-only model. Let fmod(t)f_\mathrm{mod}(t) be the fraction of targets that remain modified after complete washout at t=0t=0. Assume no new modification. Modified targets turn over at rate constant kturn>0k_\mathrm{turn}>0, and replacement by unmodified targets maintains the total target pool. Then

f˙mod=−kturnfmod,fmod(t)=fmod(0)e−kturnt.\dot f_\mathrm{mod}=-k_\mathrm{turn}f_\mathrm{mod},\qquad f_\mathrm{mod}(t)=f_\mathrm{mod}(0)e^{-k_\mathrm{turn}t}.

This model has one attracting steady state: fmod=0f_\mathrm{mod}=0. Nevertheless, it can produce a long apparent memory. Choose an initial modified fraction of 0.9 and a hypothetical turnover half-life of 24 hours. The fraction is 0.45 after one day, 0.225 after two days and 0.1125 after three days. If a readout remains positive above a 20% modified fraction, the positive response lasts

tthreshold=log⁡(0.9/0.2)kturn=24 h log⁡4.5log⁡2≃52.1 h.t_\mathrm{threshold}=\frac{\log(0.9/0.2)}{k_\mathrm{turn}} =24\,\mathrm h\,\frac{\log4.5}{\log2}\simeq52.1\,\mathrm h.

Those numbers describe an illustrative molecular model, not a measured drug or a clinical duration. The threshold is a chosen readout rule. The calculation shows why a two-day response after washout is insufficient evidence for a second attractor.

Bistability requires two attracting states under one set of final parameters and inputs. In Figure 2's scalar example, the derivative of the drift is 1−3x21-3x^2. It is negative at both outer equilibria and positive at the middle equilibrium. A small displacement from either outer state therefore relaxes back. Starting on opposite sides of zero selects different long-time states.

The experimental distinction needs more than one washout trace. Prepare two initial states, return both populations to the same conditions, and follow the molecular modification and functional readout together. Extend observation beyond the independently measured turnover time. Small perturbations can then test whether each putative state restores itself. Finite observations cannot prove infinite persistence, but they can reject a specified slow-turnover explanation.

Synthetic toggle switches make the intervention concrete. Gardner, Cantor and Collins used transient chemical or thermal induction to switch a mutually repressive gene circuit in E. coli. Their assays followed expression after inducer removal, and their switching-time experiments varied the induction duration. Those interventions probe preparation, persistence and writing separately.3

Use an independently measured molecular lifetime as the null model for persistence. To support bistability, test restoration toward two states under identical final conditions.

3Determine whether an input pulse crosses the basin boundary

Equal integrated input does not guarantee the same memory state. Zixuan Wang asks how pulse amplitude and duration interact when writing a bistable memory. The relevant event is crossing the boundary between attraction basins before the pulse ends.

Use a dimensionless scalar model to isolate that event. Start at the lower stable state x(0)=−1x(0)=-1 and apply a nonnegative rectangular pulse:

x˙=x−x3+u(t),u(t)={A,0≤t<T,0,t≥T,A≥0, T>0.\dot x=x-x^3+u(t),\qquad u(t)=\begin{cases}A,&0\leq t<T,\\0,&t\geq T,\end{cases} \qquad A\geq0,\ T>0.

The pulse area is D=ATD=AT. After the pulse, the basin boundary is x=0x=0. If x(T)>0x(T)>0, the state tends to +1+1. If x(T)<0x(T)<0, it returns to −1-1. Exactly zero is an unstable boundary case, so a practical writing protocol needs a margin beyond it.

There is an amplitude threshold that extra duration cannot overcome. Between x=−1x=-1 and zero, the opposing drift has magnitude x3−xx^3-x. Its maximum occurs at x=−1/3x=-1/\sqrt3. Hence

Ac=max⁡−1≤x≤0(x3−x)=233≃0.385.A_\mathrm c=\max_{-1\leq x\leq0}(x^3-x)=\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\simeq0.385.

For 0<A<Ac0<A<A_\mathrm c, a stable pulse-on equilibrium remains between −1 and zero and blocks crossing. At A=AcA=A_\mathrm c, the trajectory approaches a zero-drift bottleneck and still takes infinite time to pass it. Thus no finite pulse duration writes the upper state at or below this threshold.

Above the amplitude threshold, the drift stays positive throughout the crossing interval. Separating the scalar equation gives the time needed to reach the boundary:

tcross(A)=∫−10dxA+x−x3,successful writing requires A>Ac and T>tcross(A).t_\mathrm{cross}(A)=\int_{-1}^{0}\frac{dx}{A+x-x^3},\qquad \text{successful writing requires }A>A_\mathrm c\text{ and }T>t_\mathrm{cross}(A).

This criterion separates the two requirements. Amplitude must remove the blocking equilibrium. Duration must allow the trajectory to travel across the basin boundary. At A=2A=2, the crossing time is about 0.574 dimensionless time units. At A=0.4A=0.4, it is about 17.3. The sharp increase near the threshold is a dynamical bottleneck.

Pulse area fails because the internal drift acts throughout stimulation. Integrating the equation gives

x(T)−x(0)=AT+∫0T(x(t)−x(t)3) dt.x(T)-x(0)=AT+\int_0^T\bigl(x(t)-x(t)^3\bigr)\,dt.

The second term depends on the trajectory, so equal ATAT need not produce equal displacement. A pulse experiment should therefore vary amplitude and duration separately, then score the final state after relaxation. The resulting switching boundary can distinguish a bistable mechanism from a simple integrated-dose threshold. Noise would replace this deterministic boundary with a switching probability, which needs its own observation protocol.

Design a memory-writing experiment around basin crossing. Find the minimum viable amplitude and the crossing time before comparing protocols by total dose.

Part III

Ask whether an oscillator can follow an external clock

Autonomous oscillation supplies a recurring cycle. Entrainment supplies a reproducible relation to an external signal.

4Test phase locking rather than judging periodic traces by eye

An oscillator can keep cycling while failing to synchronize. Chengwei Jin extends the clock question from a stable cycle to entrainment by a periodic environment. The useful readout is the oscillator's phase relative to the forcing signal.

A phase model is appropriate when forcing is weak and amplitude perturbations relax quickly compared with phase adjustment. Let θ(t)\theta(t) be oscillator phase, Ω\Omega the forcing angular frequency, and ω\omega the unforced angular frequency. Define relative phase ψ=θ−Ωt\psi=\theta-\Omega t and frequency mismatch Δω=ω−Ω\Delta\omega=\omega-\Omega. A simple sinusoidal phase-correction model is

ψ˙=Δω−Ksin⁡ψ,K>0.\dot\psi=\Delta\omega-K\sin\psi,\qquad K>0.

The parameter KK is the largest available phase-correction rate. It depends on how the external signal couples to the oscillator. This reduced equation is an illustrative model, not a derived or fitted equation for the core's protein circuit.

One-to-one phase locking requires a stable constant relative phase. Setting the phase derivative to zero gives sin⁡ψ∗=Δω/K\sin\psi^*=\Delta\omega/K. Linearization gives slope −Kcos⁡ψ∗-K\cos\psi^*. The stable branch therefore satisfies

∣Δω∣<K,ψ∗=arcsin⁡(Δω/K)(mod2π),tphase=1K2−Δω2.|\Delta\omega|<K,\qquad \psi^*=\arcsin(\Delta\omega/K)\pmod{2\pi},\qquad t_\mathrm{phase}=\frac1{\sqrt{K^2-\Delta\omega^2}}.

The last expression is the relaxation time of a small phase displacement. It diverges as mismatch approaches the locking boundary. Equality ∣Δω∣=K|\Delta\omega|=K is a nonhyperbolic threshold, not robust phase locking. Beyond the boundary, no constant phase can supply the required correction.

Choose an illustrative natural period of 60 minutes and K=0.010 rad min−1K=0.010\,\mathrm{rad\,min^{-1}}. The stable locking condition allows forcing periods between about 54.77 and 66.33 minutes. At mismatch 0.006 rad min−10.006\,\mathrm{rad\,min^{-1}}, the stable phase difference is 36.87 degrees. A mismatch of 0.014 rad min−10.014\,\mathrm{rad\,min^{-1}} exceeds the correction capacity and produces repeated phase slips.

Outside locking, the time between complete phase slips is another measurable prediction. For ∣Δω∣>K|\Delta\omega|>K, integrating over one phase cycle gives

tslip=∫02πdψ∣Δω−Ksin⁡ψ∣=2πΔω2−K2.t_\mathrm{slip}=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\psi}{|\Delta\omega-K\sin\psi|} =\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\Delta\omega^2-K^2}}.

The slipping example takes about 641 minutes per relative-phase cycle. Its fluorescence could still look periodic over a short window. Tracking the phase difference over multiple candidate slip times gives a much sharper test.

Synthetic genetic oscillators have been tested in this way. Mondragón-Palomino and colleagues periodically varied arabinose and followed GFP peaks in individual bacterial cells. Their circuit contained positive and negative feedback, and its natural period was about 32 minutes under the reported conditions. They mapped phase-locking regions and observed higher-order resonance. Those measurements support entrainment as a distinct circuit function. The 60-minute phase model above is a separate teaching example.4

Proposed functionIntervention and readoutAlternative being tested
Threshold signalingVary total ligand while measuring occupancy and activity.A different state-to-output rule or a depleted free pool.

A next experiment could combine the last two protocols. Use an oscillator's phase to time a pulse that writes a bistable downstream circuit. Measure how writing probability changes with forcing mismatch. This would test whether the clock's phase precision actually improves a downstream decision, a concrete link to the computational tasks of Lecture 12.

Measure relative phase and its recovery across a range of forcing frequencies. A recurring signal becomes an entrained clock only when the specified phase relation is stable.
