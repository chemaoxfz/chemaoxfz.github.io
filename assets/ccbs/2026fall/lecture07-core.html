Equilibrium is a local privilege

A cell is built from parts that individually equilibrate fast, assembled into a system held far from equilibrium. So an equilibrium model of a part is neither automatically wrong nor automatically allowed. It is a privilege, earned by an audit of the state graph, and free energy explains something only when the current it maintains buys a behaviour the equilibrium graph provably cannot produce.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 7 · core page (full) · 2026-09-22 (Tue) 10:40–12:15 · lecturer-led, with student contributions

Lecture 6 ended with an enzyme whose concentrations stay constant while substrate keeps entering and product keeps leaving. Its steady processing state satisfies vin=vcat=koutP∗>0v_{\mathrm{in}}=v_{\mathrm{cat}}=k_{\mathrm{out}}P^*>0. The balances vanish, but the chemical traffic does not. What would distinguish this stationary state from thermodynamic equilibrium?

Fast reduction leaves the same question inside the model. Lecture 6 first derived exact total balances, then reconstructed fast species from an attracting constraint. Rapid binding gave a constraint with KdK_d. The distinct catalytic QSSA gave one with KMK_M. Fast adjustment alone did not make those constraints interchangeable. We now ask when a fast stationary state can specifically be reconstructed from equilibrium weights.

The telegraph promoter supplies a second starting point. Lecture 6 averaged its production hazard over stationary ON and OFF probabilities. Here we will establish what that two-state balance proves, what hidden mechanisms it cannot reveal, and what changes when a larger state graph contains a driven cycle.

Part 1

Earn equilibrium locally

State weights become legitimate only after the boundary, the clock and the observable have been named.

1Put a box around the claim

A local equilibrium approximation needs a specified subsystem, a relaxation time and a specified observable. These choices do not themselves establish equilibrium.

Start by drawing a box around the subnetwork whose states you intend to count. Inside the box go the species whose interconversion you will describe with weights. Outside go the reservoirs the rest of the cell holds fixed: ATP, ADP and phosphate, the ligand concentration, the ionic conditions. Those outside species are chemostatted, meaning something else pays to keep them where they are, and they appear in the model as constants rather than as variables.

The second choice is the clock. Let τexchange\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}} be the relaxation time of the fast subsystem at fixed inputs or totals. This need not equal one molecule's residence time. Let τobs\tau_{\mathrm{obs}} be the shorter relevant time over which the retained inputs or outputs change. Following the stationary fast state requires

τexchange≪τobs.\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}. (1)

For an illustrative binding subsystem relaxing in 1 ms, a 10-minute input or output timescale gives 10−3/600≈1.7×10−610^{-3}/600\approx1.7\times10^{-6}. That supports eliminating the fast transient after it has decayed. A slowly maturing reporter alone cannot establish this separation if the ligand changes rapidly. As in Lecture 6, check the forcing as well as the observed response.

The third choice is the observable. Equilibrium weights predict occupancies, but do not by themselves determine relaxation times or time correlations. Those require kinetic rates. A driven system can also have occupancies identical to an equilibrium model's predictions. Agreement for that observable does not make the system thermodynamically equilibrated. Sections 4–5 supply the additional test.

The failure mode this box is drawn to catch

Removing a chemostatted species from the diagram makes a driven open system look like a closed equilibrium one. If ATP is consumed by a transition inside your box and you have not drawn ATP, the graph you are about to audit is not the graph you are modelling. Draw the fuel.

None of this claims the cell is closed or lifeless. Phillips makes the point with an image worth keeping: the atoms in a portrait of Napoleon are in equilibrium with each other even though nothing about the painting's history was.1 A part can be at equilibrium inside a whole that is not.

You can now decide, before writing a single weight, whether a subnetwork is a candidate for an equilibrium description: name its components, check τexchange≪τobs\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}, and say which observable the claim covers.

2Build the state ledger

An equilibrium probability is one state's weight divided by the sum of the weights of all allowed states.

Under an equilibrium hypothesis, assign state ii a free energy GiG_i per molecular system and weight wi=e−βGiw_i=e^{-\beta G_i}, where β=(kBT)−1\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}. Here kBk_B is Boltzmann's constant and TT is absolute temperature. If states exchange ligands with reservoirs, their effective energies include those reservoirs' chemical potentials. The state probability is the normalized weight:

pi=wiZ,Z=∑jwj,β=(kBT)−1.p_i=\frac{w_i}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_j w_j,\qquad \beta=(k_BT)^{-1}. (2)

Here ZZ is the partition function, and it is a bookkeeping device rather than a force or a rate. Every difficulty in using (2) sits in deciding which states are allowed and what their weights are, never in the division.

Take a receptor that is either empty or ligand-bound. Let cc be free ligand concentration, the quantity called SS in the enzyme example. It is not the supplied total. Give the empty state weight 11. Equilibrium binding then gives the bound state relative weight c/Kdc/K_d, so

Z=1+cKd,pbound=c/Kd1+c/Kd=cKd+c.Z=1+\frac{c}{K_d},\qquad p_{\mathrm{bound}}=\frac{c/K_d}{1+c/K_d}=\pf{\frac{c}{K_d+c}}. (3)

That is the binding isotherm from lecture 3, arrived at a second time and by a different route. Lecture 3 got it from a rate balance, k+cE=k−CESk_+cE=k_-C_{ES}. Here it comes from counting weights. The two agree because the rate ratio k−/k+k_-/k_+ is KdK_d, and that identification is the bridge between the kinetic and the thermodynamic description. It will be assumed again in §6, where it is what lets a ratio of rate constants be read as a free energy.

The concentration enters through a logarithm, and it is worth writing why. The chemical potential of a species at concentration cc is

μ=μ∘+kBTln⁡ ⁣(cc∘),\mu=\mu^{\circ}+k_BT\ln\!\left(\frac{c}{c^{\circ}}\right), (4)

Here chemical potential is energy per molecule in an ideal dilute solution. The standard concentration c∘c^{\circ} is conventionally 1 M. The ratio inside the logarithm is dimensionless. Molar chemical potentials instead use RTRT, with R=NAkBR=N_Ak_B the gas constant. This distinction will matter when we use tabulated ATP energies in kJ/mol.

A binding free energy is not one bond

Binding free energy includes solvent exchange, loss of molecular freedom and conformational changes. Lecture 2's contact calculation deliberately assumed a net gain of 2.5kBT2.5k_BT per model contact. It was not a universal hydrogen-bond energy. The exact conversion is ln⁡10≈2.30\ln10\approx2.30 thermal units per decade of an equilibrium constant. An illustrative additive contact model can estimate a scale, but it cannot identify the energy of an individual bond from an affinity.

You can now write the equilibrium occupancy of any subnetwork whose states you can enumerate: assign weights, sum them, divide. Omitting an allowed state changes the denominator and therefore every probability on the page.

3Let binding reshape an ensemble

Before asking what equilibrium cannot do, see how much it already does: cooperativity and regulation both come out of equation (2) with no drive at all.

Equilibrium allocation can already generate cooperative binding and regulated occupancy. These responses need no sustained chemical driving within the binding layer. Their affinities still reflect free-energy differences. Any transcription or catalysis read out from the occupied states has its own energetic requirements.

Cooperativity, from conformational selection. In the Monod–Wyman–Changeux model an oligomer with nn equivalent sites occupies two concerted conformations, a high-affinity RR state and a low-affinity TT state.2 Write LL for the ratio of TT to RR weights with no ligand bound, and KRK_R and KTK_T for the two dissociation constants. Every subunit binds independently within a conformation, so each conformation contributes a binomial factor and the ledger is

Z=(1+c/KR)n⏟R states+L (1+c/KT)n⏟T states.Z=\underbrace{(1+c/K_R)^{n}}_{R\text{ states}}+\underbrace{L\,(1+c/K_T)^{n}}_{T\text{ states}}. (5)

Differentiate ln⁡Z\ln Z with respect to ln⁡c\ln c to count the mean number of bound ligands. Dividing by the number of sites gives fractional saturation, Yˉ=n−1∂ln⁡Z/∂ln⁡c\bar Y=n^{-1}\partial\ln Z/\partial\ln c:

Yˉ=(c/KR)(1+c/KR)n−1+L (c/KT)(1+c/KT)n−1(1+c/KR)n+L (1+c/KT)n.\bar Y=\frac{(c/K_R)(1+c/K_R)^{n-1}+L\,(c/K_T)(1+c/K_T)^{n-1}}{(1+c/K_R)^{n}+L\,(1+c/K_T)^{n}}. (6)

No term in (5) couples one bound ligand to another. The sharpness comes entirely from ligand binding shifting the population between the two conformations.

Measure sharpness with the local Hill slope nH(c)=dln⁡[Yˉ/(1−Yˉ)]/dln⁡cn_H(c)=d\ln[\bar Y/(1-\bar Y)]/d\ln c. A true Hill curve has constant slope. An MWC curve generally does not, so a slope at half saturation, a maximum slope and a fitted Hill exponent need not agree.3 Figure 3 reports the maximum local slope. With L=103L=10^3 and KT/KR=50K_T/K_R=50, it is 1.001.00, 1.281.28 and 2.672.67 for one, two and four sites.

Four sites, maximum local slope 2.67

In this equilibrium occupancy model, the slope is bounded above by the site count. Its actual value also depends on the conformational weights and affinities. Four sites therefore need not give a slope of four. A phenomenological fit to a different readout, especially versus total rather than free ligand, does not inherit this site-count interpretation automatically.

Competing occupancies can regulate a promoter. Allow three states: empty, RNA-polymerase-bound, or repressor-bound. Double occupation is excluded. Let NP,NRN_P,N_R be available polymerase and repressor copy numbers, and NNSN_{\mathrm{NS}} the much larger nonspecific-site pool. Let Δεp,Δεr\Delta\varepsilon_p,\Delta\varepsilon_r be the corresponding binding-energy differences between promoter and nonspecific sites. The dilute-reservoir state model gives4

pbound=NPNNSe−βΔεp1+NPNNSe−βΔεp+NRNNSe−βΔεr.p_{\mathrm{bound}}=\frac{\frac{N_P}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_p}}{1+\frac{N_P}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_p}+\frac{N_R}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_r}}. (7)

In the weak-promoter limit the polymerase term in the denominator is negligible and the whole effect of the repressor collapses into a single factor,

fold-change≈(1+NRNNSe−βΔεr)−1.\text{fold-change}\approx\left(1+\frac{N_R}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_r}\right)^{-1}. (8)

Fold-change means expression with repressor divided by expression without it. Relating expression to polymerase occupancy assumes the same initiation activity in the two conditions and fast binding relative to that readout. At fixed reservoir conditions, changing NRN_R predicts a change in expression. If target binding depletes the reservoir, the free regulator must instead be found from the total balances introduced in Lecture 6 and developed in Lecture 8.

The test that separates a model from a fit. A single sigmoid can be fitted by many things. Perturb one measured ingredient instead, carry that number through the same partition function, and predict the new curve before measuring it. Agreement across several such interventions is what makes the ledger physical, and one fitted curve is not evidence of the mechanism behind it.

Derive cooperative occupancy without a driven binding cycle. State the readout assumptions before interpreting occupancy as activity or a fitted slope as a site count.

Part 2

Test the graph

Occupancies alone do not identify equilibrium. The edges do.

4Stationarity does not establish equilibrium

Stationarity balances probability flow at each state. Detailed balance balances each reversible channel separately.

Use Lecture 5's continuous-time Markov description. Let kijk_{ij} be the transition hazard from state ii to state jj, in inverse time, and πi\pi_i its stationary probability. At fixed free ligand, a binding transition can have hazard k+ck_+c. The association constant k+k_+ itself has concentration-inverse time-inverse units. A transition hazard is not a concentration flux.

Keep the probability-column convention p˙=Qp\dot p=Qp. Thus Qji=kijQ_{ji}=k_{ij} for distinct states and Qii=−∑j≠ikijQ_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}k_{ij}. Each column sums to zero. Stationarity and detailed balance now impose different conditions:

stationary:∑jπjkji=πi∑jkijfor every node i,detailed balance:πikij=πjkjifor every edge ij.\begin{aligned}\text{stationary:}\quad&\sum_j\pi_jk_{ji}=\pi_i\sum_jk_{ij}\quad\text{for every node }i,\\[2pt]\text{detailed balance:}\quad&\pi_ik_{ij}=\pi_jk_{ji}\quad\text{for every edge }ij.\end{aligned} (9)

The first line is Qπ=0Q\pi=0, together with normalization ∑iπi=1\sum_i\pi_i=1. The second sets every resolved edge current to zero. Summing the edge balances gives node balance, so detailed balance implies stationarity. Node balance alone can allow circulating currents.

Detailed balance fixes the probability ratio across a connected pair: πj/πi=kij/kji\pi_j/\pi_i=k_{ij}/k_{ji}. To interpret these ratios as physical Boltzmann weights, the rates must also be consistent with the specified state energies and reservoirs. Writing Gi=−kBTln⁡πiG_i=-k_BT\ln\pi_i after fitting an arbitrary stationary distribution does not establish that physical consistency or eliminate currents.

Keep the earlier meanings separate

Stationarity means a time-independent probability distribution. Ergodicity licenses a long-trajectory interpretation under the conditions discussed in Lecture 5. Thermodynamic equilibrium additionally rules out driven currents in the resolved physical channels.

Lecture 4 also used “equilibrium” for an ODE fixed point. That mathematical term does not certify thermodynamic equilibrium. Likewise, Γv(x∗)=0\Gamma v(x^*)=0 balances species but need not set every reaction flux to zero. Stability remains the separate question of return after a perturbation.

The practical consequence is sharp, and it is the reason the distinction is worth ten minutes. Occupancies are what an experiment usually measures, and occupancies alone cannot tell the two conditions apart. A stationary ring with probability circulating steadily around it presents constant occupancies at every node, exactly as an equilibrium system would. Fitting the numbers will succeed. The fit will also be meaningless as evidence about the mechanism.

You can now say what a fit to steady-state occupancies does not establish. If someone claims a system is at equilibrium because an equilibrium model fits the occupancies, you know the missing evidence is about edges, and §5 tells you where to look for it.

5Find the cycles

Graph topology identifies where stationary edge currents are possible. Rate ratios decide whether the allowed circulation occurs.

On a finite tree, stationarity forces detailed balance. Assume a connected graph with positive rates in both directions on every edge. A tree has no cycle and, when it has more than one state, has a leaf with only one edge. At the leaf, all probability enters and leaves through that edge. Stationarity therefore forces its current to zero. Remove the leaf and repeat. Every edge current is zero.

Lecture 6's ON/OFF chain is the smallest example. With OFF-to-ON hazard α\alpha and reverse hazard β\beta, stationarity gives πon=α/(α+β)\pi_{\mathrm{on}}=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta) and πoffα=πonβ\pi_{\mathrm{off}}\alpha=\pi_{\mathrm{on}}\beta. This proves reversibility of that two-state chain. Fast switching was needed to average its production hazard, not to obtain the stationary probabilities.

A coarse state graph can hide dissipation

Two different molecular pathways can connect the same observed OFF and ON states. Opposing currents through those pathways can cancel in the combined edge while fuel is consumed. Resolve such channels before making a thermodynamic claim. Detailed balance of a coarse-grained two-state chain does not establish equilibrium of the complete gene-expression machinery.

On a cycle, stationarity need not imply detailed balance. A cycle has no leaf, so the stripping argument never starts. Probability can circulate: each node's inflow balances its outflow while a steady current runs around the loop. For a three-state ring, stationarity at node 1 reads

(π2k21−π1k12)+(π3k31−π1k13)=0,(\pi_2k_{21}-\pi_1k_{12})+(\pi_3k_{31}-\pi_1k_{13})=0, (10)

and the two brackets can cancel each other while neither is zero. Requiring each bracket to vanish separately is the extra demand of detailed balance, and on a cycle it is a genuine restriction on the rate constants:

∏cyclekforward=∏cyclekreverse.\prod_{\mathrm{cycle}}k_{\mathrm{forward}}=\prod_{\mathrm{cycle}}k_{\mathrm{reverse}}. (11)

Equation (11) is Kolmogorov's criterion, and it must hold on every independent cycle of the graph. It follows from (9) by multiplying the edge conditions around the loop, where the πi\pi_i appear once in each position and cancel.

A spanning tree counts the independent cycle tests. Let the connected graph have NN nodes and EE reversible edges. A spanning tree contains every node and exactly N−1N-1 edges. Each remaining edge closes one fundamental cycle when added to that tree. Thus

ncyc=E−N+1.n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=E-N+1. (12)

A four-state square gives ncyc=4−4+1=1n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=4-4+1=1. The spanning tree is a bookkeeping choice, not a claim that its edges carry no current in the full graph. Once the missing edge is restored, circulation can pass through all four edges. A graph that is itself a tree has no such cycle.6

A bridge carries no stationary current, even in a graph with driven loops. Removing a bridge separates a finite graph into two components. Sum stationarity over either component. All internal currents cancel, leaving the bridge current equal to zero. More generally, stationary currents lie in the graph's cycle space. Zero current on a bridge does not establish thermodynamic equilibrium of the whole system or exclude hidden driven channels.6

The topology tells us where circulation is possible. The rate ratios decide whether it occurs. A cyclic diagram alone cannot answer the equilibrium question. For a biochemical state graph, hold its inputs fixed while applying the test, and check that omitted reservoirs or channels have not supplied a hidden drive.5

Count independent cycles, test their rate products, and state the channel resolution. A finite tree has zero stationary edge currents. A graph with cycles needs rate information as well as topology.

6Measure affinity and current

Drive, flow and cost are three different quantities, and a claim that energy buys something has to name all three.

When (11) fails, the amount by which it fails is the natural measure of the drive. Define the dimensionless cycle affinity

A=ln⁡∏kforward∏kreverse,A=\ln\frac{\prod k_{\mathrm{forward}}}{\prod k_{\mathrm{reverse}}}, (13)

The affinity AA is dimensionless. It vanishes when the cycle satisfies detailed balance. Its interpretation as a drive requires local detailed balance: each resolved transition's forward/reverse ratio must account for the state-energy change and exchange with the specified thermal and chemical reservoirs. Around a closed cycle the internal state-energy changes cancel. The remaining free-energy release per cycle is kBTAk_BTA.

The affinity is a potential difference, so it says nothing on its own about how much traffic flows. The net current on an edge is

Jij=πikij−πjkji,J_{ij}=\pi_ik_{ij}-\pi_jk_{ji}, (14)

With probabilities dimensionless and hazards in inverse seconds, JijJ_{ij} has units s−1\mathrm{s}^{-1}. It is net transition frequency per modeled system, not Lecture 3's concentration flux. Around a simple ring, stationarity makes this current the same on every edge. Call that signed current JJ. The entropy-production rate divided by Boltzmann's constant is

σ=J A,\sigma=J\,A, (15)

Here σ\sigma is entropy production in units of kBk_B per time. The physical entropy-production rate is kBσk_B\sigma, and the dissipated power at temperature TT is kBTσk_BT\sigma. Both factors matter. At fixed finite affinity, suppressing the cycle current suppresses its dissipation. Large opposing traffic with a small imbalance need not imply a large net cost.

Both conditions are Kirchhoff laws on the graph, which is worth carrying as a name. Stationarity says what flows into a node flows out of it, and that is the current law. It holds in any steady state, driven or not. Equilibrium adds the demand that the drop around every loop vanish, and that is the voltage law, of which (11) is the multiplicative form. The general version of (15) is written in the same language: total dissipation is σ=∑αJαAα\sigma=\sum_\alpha J_\alpha A_\alpha over the ncycn_{\mathrm{cyc}} independent cycles counted in §5, so a network has exactly one affinity and one current per independent loop and no more.6

ATP supplies a concrete chemical-potential scale. Use the illustrative transformed standard value ΔG∘′=−30.5 kJ/mol\Delta G^{\circ\prime}=-30.5\,\mathrm{kJ/mol} for hydrolysis. The prime indicates biochemical standard conditions with buffered proton activity. Treat water activity as one and approximate solute activities by concentrations divided by c∘=1 Mc^\circ=1\,\mathrm M. The dimensionless activity quotient QATPQ_{\mathrm{ATP}}, distinct from the Markov generator Q, gives

QATP=([ADP]/c∘)([Pi]/c∘)[ATP]/c∘,ΔG=ΔG∘′+RTln⁡QATP.\begin{aligned}Q_{\mathrm{ATP}}&=\frac{([\mathrm{ADP}]/c^\circ)([\mathrm{P_i}]/c^\circ)}{[\mathrm{ATP}]/c^\circ},\\\Delta G&=\Delta G^{\circ\prime}+RT\ln Q_{\mathrm{ATP}}.\end{aligned} (16)

For an order-of-magnitude example, take ATP at 3 mM, ADP at 0.3 mM and phosphate at 5 mM. Then QATP=5×10−4Q_{\mathrm{ATP}}=5\times10^{-4} and ln⁡QATP≈−7.6\ln Q_{\mathrm{ATP}}\approx-7.6. At 37 °C, RT≈2.58 kJ/molRT\approx2.58\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}. Holding the illustrative standard value fixed gives

ΔGRT≈−30.52.58−7.6=−19.4,ΔG≈−50 kJ/mol.\frac{\Delta G}{RT}\approx\frac{-30.5}{2.58}-7.6=\pf{-19.4},\qquad\Delta G\approx-50\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}. (17)

This corresponds to about 19.4kBT19.4k_BT released per hydrolysis. It is an estimate, not a universal cellular ATP value. Quantitative work must match temperature, pH, magnesium and activity conventions to the measured pools.

A chemical drive, not a guaranteed accuracy

The dimensionless drive corresponds to 19.4/ln⁡10≈8.419.4/\ln10\approx8.4 decades of a forward/reverse cycle bias. That conversion is not a universal bound on output discrimination. Accuracy and fuel consumed per correct product also depend on the kinetic network, futile attempts and coupling to the output.

Compute the cycle affinity, current and dissipation with their separate units. Compare kBTAk_BTA with the available fuel free energy without mistaking that comparison for fuel use per output.

Part 3

Name what energy buys

A nonequilibrium claim is incomplete until a forbidden equilibrium behaviour becomes a measured benefit.

7Unlock a forbidden response

Drive matters when it changes which input-output functions are attainable at all, and for one standard promoter that change can be stated as a theorem.

Take the four-state promoter square: the promoter is empty, or bound by a transcription factor, or bound by RNA polymerase, or bound by both. Write x=[TF]/Kx=[\mathrm{TF}]/K for free transcription-factor concentration divided by its dissociation constant K at the empty promoter. Let wPw_P be the polymerase weight at fixed free polymerase, and let ω\omega be the dimensionless interaction factor when both proteins are bound. At equilibrium the four weights are 11, xx, wPw_P and wPxωw_Px\omega. Polymerase occupancy is the sum of the last two over the sum of all four:

pRNAP=wP+wPωx1+x+wP+wPωx=a+bxc+dx.p_{\mathrm{RNAP}}=\frac{w_P+w_P\omega x}{1+x+w_P+w_P\omega x}=\frac{a+bx}{c+dx}. (18)

Equation (18) is a fractional-linear function of xx, and every such function has derivative

ddxa+bxc+dx=bc−ad(c+dx)2.\frac{d}{dx}\frac{a+bx}{c+dx}=\frac{bc-ad}{(c+dx)^{2}}. (19)

The denominator of (19) is a square, so it is positive for every x>0x>0. The numerator contains no xx at all. So the derivative has one sign for the whole range, the response is monotone, and it cannot have an interior maximum. With wP=0.3w_P=0.3 and ω=8\omega=8 the numerator is bc−ad=2.1>0bc-ad=2.1>0 and the response rises everywhere.

The prohibition, stated exactly

For the four-state promoter square with at most one transcription factor bound per state, and with detailed balance holding, the polymerase occupancy is fractional-linear in the transcription-factor concentration and therefore monotone. No choice of the parameters wPw_P, ω\omega or KK produces an interior maximum. This is a statement about this state model, not about equilibrium in general.

A biochemical implementation must identify a fuel-coupled channel consistent with its nonzero affinity.

Same four states, a shape equilibrium cannot make

The interior maximum violates the monotonicity restriction of this equilibrium state model. Its cycle free-energy scale is 6.75kBT6.75k_BT, smaller than the illustrative ATP hydrolysis scale. That comparison does not establish “one third of an ATP” per output. Fuel stoichiometry, competing routes and current must also be specified.7

This is where the audit acquires teeth. A measured interior maximum falsifies the whole equilibrium state model at once, no matter how many parameters it carries, because (19) rules out that shape for every parameter choice. That is a much stronger conclusion than any fit could deliver, and it costs nothing but the derivative.8

You can now convert an equilibrium model into a falsifiable prediction: derive the family of shapes it can produce, and look for a measurement outside that family. One observation of the right kind kills the model for every parameter value.

8Proofread with a reset loop

A driven intermediate can apply a second selective rejection to an already enriched population. The improvement follows from competing exit hazards, the same calculation used for bursts in Lectures 5 and 6.

First specify the baseline. Correct substrate C and incorrect substrate D have equal free concentrations and equal association constants. Binding favors C by a free-energy difference ΔGCD>0\Delta G_{CD}>0. Their equilibrium bound-population ratio is

f0=e−ΔGCD/kBT.f_0=e^{-\Delta G_{CD}/k_BT}. (20)

If both bound states commit slowly at the same frequency, their wrong/right product-flux ratio approaches f0f_0. In this specified one-stage model, that is an error floor, not an accuracy-independent law for every recognition mechanism. For f0=10−2f_0=10^{-2}, the binding difference is kBTln⁡100≈4.6kBTk_BT\ln100\approx4.6k_BT.9

A second kinetic test can improve discrimination without changing that binding difference. Hopfield and Ninio developed mechanisms in which fuel drives transfer from an initially selective bound population into another intermediate. That intermediate can either commit to product or discard its substrate.910

We use an explicit limiting model of that construction. Slow, substrate-independent transfer preserves an incoming wrong/right ratio f0f_0 from the first binding stage. Strong driving suppresses reverse transfer and direct entry into the second intermediate.

The intermediate has two competing exits. Both substrates commit at hazard W. Correct substrate is discarded at hazard ℓC\ell_C, and incorrect substrate at hazard ℓD\ell_D. Assume ℓC=f0ℓD\ell_C=f_0\ell_D, giving the second stage the same discrimination factor as the first. All three hazards have inverse-time units.

The probability of committing before discard is the committing hazard divided by the total exit hazard. Therefore

pC=WW+ℓC,pD=WW+ℓD,f=f0pDpC=f0W+f0ℓDW+ℓD.p_C=\frac{W}{W+\ell_C},\qquad p_D=\frac{W}{W+\ell_D},\qquad f=f_0\frac{p_D}{p_C}=f_0\frac{W+f_0\ell_D}{W+\ell_D}. (21)

Slow commitment gives a second factor of discrimination only when W≪ℓC=f0ℓDW\ll\ell_C=f_0\ell_D. Then pD/pC≈f0p_D/p_C\approx f_0 and f≈f02f\approx f_0^2. Merely requiring W≪ℓDW\ll\ell_D is not sufficient. Conversely, W≫ℓDW\gg\ell_D makes both commitment probabilities nearly one and removes the second test.

The driving assumptions are essential to multiplying the two tests. A thermodynamically consistent reversible network also has reverse and direct-entry routes. Near equilibrium, those routes cannot simply be deleted. Keeping the intermediate strongly biased away from equilibrium makes the sequential limiting calculation possible. Its energy cost is not obtained by converting the improved error ratio directly into ATP molecules.

High specificity alone does not establish proofreading. Binding energies, unequal association or commitment rates, and hidden state lifetimes can also change error. Measure the relevant states and perturb the proposed fuel-coupled step. Compare the error, the product flux and the fuel use against the same specified mechanism.

9Count independent samples

Precision depends on how a cell samples a fluctuating concentration. Specify the sampling device and estimator before assigning an error limit.

Independent samples set the statistical scale. A Poisson count with mean N has standard deviation N\sqrt N, so its fractional uncertainty is N−1/2N^{-1/2}. Doubling precision requires four times as many independent samples. A physical sensor may count the same diffusing molecule repeatedly. Berg and Purcell's calculation connects those correlations to measurement time, geometry and diffusion.11

Berg and Purcell considered a transparent spherical monitoring volume of radius aa. The instrument continuously counts the molecules inside and averages that count for time TT. Molecules diffuse with coefficient DD in a dilute medium of number density cc. Here T denotes observation time, not the temperature in the preceding energy formulas. Accounting for correlations and taking T≫a2/DT\gg a^2/D gives

δcrmsc=(5πDac T3)−1/2.\frac{\delta c_{\mathrm{rms}}}{c}=\left(\frac{5\pi D a c\,T}{3}\right)^{-1/2}. (22)

Every symbol in (22) is a resource. Put in a bacterium: a=1 μa=1\,\mum, D=10−9 m2/sD=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m^2/s} for a small molecule in water, and a ligand at 1 nM, which is 6.0×10176.0\times10^{17} molecules per cubic metre. One second of averaging gives

δcc∣T=1 s=1.8%,\frac{\delta c}{c}\Big|_{T=1\,\mathrm{s}}=\pf{1.8\%}, (23)

and reaching 1 % at that concentration takes 3.23.2 s. The inverse-square-root law is what makes this expensive: the same cell needs 320 s for 0.1 %.

The model qualification matters. A perfectly absorbing sphere counts arrivals at mean rate 4πDac4\pi Dac, the capture calculation from Lecture 2. Its Poisson counting estimate gives fractional uncertainty (4πDacT)−1/2(4\pi DacT)^{-1/2}, a different prefactor from (22). The transparent-volume formula is therefore a useful benchmark, not a universal lower bound for every detector.

Lecture 5 supplies the statistical connection: repeated samples of a correlated signal are not independent observations. Spending energy on receptor cycling or downstream processing can change response times, correlations and accuracy, but the effect depends on the mechanism and estimator.12 Compare the information collected and the energetic cost under the same measurement protocol.

Combine diffusion, observation time and sampling correlations to estimate precision. Keep the detector and estimator attached to any claimed sensing limit.

10Audit the energy claim

Use equilibrium wherever it passes the audit, and add the smallest driven cycle that earns a named function.

The lecture's deliverable is a procedure, and it runs in eight steps.

  1. Boundary. Draw the modelled subnetwork, resolve distinct transition channels and mark chemostatted species, fuel included. Check fast relaxation against retained forcing.
  2. Observable. Say whether the output is an occupancy, an edge flux, an error rate, a time, or a noise statistic.
  3. Equilibrium baseline. Enumerate the states, assign weights, and solve the equilibrium model first.
  4. Prohibition. Prove which behaviours that state model excludes, as in equation (19).
  5. Drive. Count the independent cycles from (12), test (11) on each, and compute its affinity from (13).
  6. Flow. Solve the stationary occupancies and the currents from (14).
  7. Benefit and cost. Report the function together with the dissipation (15), the delay, and the noise.
  8. Challenge. Set every cycle affinity to zero, keeping the same states and observables, and see whether the claimed function survives.

Step eight is what makes an energy claim falsifiable, so it is never the step that gets trimmed. If the function survives with all affinities set to zero, dissipation was not necessary for it and the energy in the story was decoration. Homework 6 supplies three cases to run this on: allostery, a four-state promoter, and stochastic binding.13

Lecture 8 combines this equilibrium test with Lecture 6's total dynamics. It constructs a model class with a compatible fast binding layer and slower state-dependent activities. Catalysis can be reversible. The full network can carry throughput even when binding is approximated by conditional equilibrium at each current total. Finite pools require mass balances alongside the state weights.

A driven promoter or proofreading cycle cannot be hidden inside that equilibrium layer without changing the model. Keep its driven channels explicit or use a separately justified nonequilibrium fast closure. Finally, unique fast equilibrium does not establish stability of the slow total dynamics. Lecture 4's dynamical tests still apply.

The arrival

Use equilibrium weights where the mechanism, reservoirs and timescales justify them. Elsewhere retain the currents. A quantitative energy claim identifies the function, its equilibrium comparator, the drive, the current and the cost per measured output.

You can now defend or refute an energy claim in eight steps, and you know which one of them most claims omit.
