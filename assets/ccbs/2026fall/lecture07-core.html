Equilibrium is a local privilege

A cell is built from parts that individually equilibrate fast, assembled into a system held far from equilibrium. So an equilibrium model of a part is neither automatically wrong nor automatically allowed. It is a privilege, earned by an audit of the state graph, and free energy explains something only when the current it maintains buys a behaviour the equilibrium graph provably cannot produce.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 7 · core page (full) · 2026-09-22 (Tue) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

Last lecture you closed the fast species at fixed totals. You wrote the complex in terms of the totals and a dissociation constant, dropped a derivative, and carried on. Two separate permissions were spent in that one move, and lecture 6 paid for only one of them. Dropping the derivative is licensed by fast exchange, which lecture 6 established. Writing the occupancy as a ratio of Boltzmann weights is licensed by equilibrium, which it did not. This lecture is the bill for the second permission.

The distinction is not pedantry, because a fast subnetwork can settle into a stationary distribution that is not an equilibrium one, and when it does, the weights are wrong while the algebra still looks fine. Lecture 6 handed over exactly such an object: a telegraph promoter with a stationary distribution. The word stationary is the one this lecture has to split.

Part 1

Earn equilibrium locally

State weights become legitimate only after the boundary, the clock and the observable have been named.

1Put a box around the claim

Equilibrium is local in three ways at once: in components, in time, and in observable.

Start by drawing a box around the subnetwork whose states you intend to count. Inside the box go the species whose interconversion you will describe with weights. Outside go the reservoirs the rest of the cell holds fixed: ATP, ADP and phosphate, the ligand concentration, the ionic conditions. Those outside species are chemostatted, meaning something else pays to keep them where they are, and they appear in the model as constants rather than as variables.

The second localization is the clock, and it is the one lecture 6 already taught you to check. Let τexchange\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}} be the time for the states inside the box to interconvert, and let τobs\tau_{\mathrm{obs}} be the time over which the thing you are measuring changes. The box is a candidate for an equilibrium description when

τexchange≪τobs.\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}. (1)

A receptor that exchanges ligand every millisecond, watched through a reporter that matures over ten minutes, satisfies (1) by nearly six orders of magnitude. That is a large margin, and it is why the approximation is worth making.

The third localization is the observable, and it is the one most often skipped. The same box can be at equilibrium for one measurement and not for another. If you watch the relaxed occupancy, fast exchange has erased the transient and the weights describe what you see. If you watch the transient itself, or a flux through the box, or a correlation time, you are watching precisely the quantity the equilibrium description throws away.

The failure mode this box is drawn to catch

Removing a chemostatted species from the diagram makes a driven open system look like a closed equilibrium one. If ATP is consumed by a transition inside your box and you have not drawn ATP, the graph you are about to audit is not the graph you are modelling. Draw the fuel.

None of this claims the cell is closed or lifeless. Phillips makes the point with an image worth keeping: the atoms in a portrait of Napoleon are in equilibrium with each other even though nothing about the painting's history was.1 A part can be at equilibrium inside a whole that is not.

You can now decide, before writing a single weight, whether a subnetwork is a candidate for an equilibrium description: name its components, check τexchange≪τobs\tau_{\mathrm{exchange}}\ll\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}, and say which observable the claim covers.

2Build the state ledger

An equilibrium probability is one state's weight divided by the sum of the weights of all allowed states.

Assign to each state ii of the box a free energy GiG_i and from it a statistical weight wi=e−βGiw_i=e^{-\beta G_i}, where β=(kBT)−1\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}. The probability of the state is that weight normalized by their sum:

pi=wiZ,Z=∑jwj,β=(kBT)−1.p_i=\frac{w_i}{Z},\qquad Z=\sum_j w_j,\qquad \beta=(k_BT)^{-1}. (2)

Here ZZ is the partition function, and it is a bookkeeping device rather than a force or a rate. Every difficulty in using (2) sits in deciding which states are allowed and what their weights are, never in the division.

Do the smallest case immediately. A receptor is either empty or ligand-bound, so there are two states. Give the empty state weight 11 by convention, which costs nothing because (2) divides by the sum. The bound state's weight carries the ligand concentration cc and the dissociation constant KdK_d, so it is c/Kdc/K_d. Then

Z=1+cKd,pbound=c/Kd1+c/Kd=cKd+c.Z=1+\frac{c}{K_d},\qquad p_{\mathrm{bound}}=\frac{c/K_d}{1+c/K_d}=\pf{\frac{c}{K_d+c}}. (3)

That is the binding isotherm from lecture 3, arrived at a second time and by a different route. Lecture 3 got it from a rate balance, k+cE=k−CESk_+cE=k_-C_{ES}. Here it comes from counting weights. The two agree because the rate ratio k−/k+k_-/k_+ is KdK_d, and that identification is the bridge between the kinetic and the thermodynamic description. It will be assumed again in §6, where it is what lets a ratio of rate constants be read as a free energy.

The concentration enters through a logarithm, and it is worth writing why. The chemical potential of a species at concentration cc is

μ=μ∘+kBTln⁡ ⁣(cc∘),\mu=\mu^{\circ}+k_BT\ln\!\left(\frac{c}{c^{\circ}}\right), (4)

where μ∘\mu^{\circ} is the standard chemical potential and c∘c^{\circ} the standard concentration, conventionally 1 M. The ratio c/c∘c/c^{\circ} is dimensionless, which is what makes the logarithm legal. Whenever a concentration appears inside a logarithm in this lecture, a standard state has been chosen and divided out.

A binding free energy is not one bond

It is tempting to convert a single hydrogen-bond energy directly into an affinity. The measured binding free energy also contains solvation, the entropy lost on immobilizing the ligand, and any conformational reorganization the protein pays for. Lecture 2 put a number on the honest version: a good neutral contact is worth roughly 1.4 kBT1.4\,k_BT, and a decade of KdK_d costs ln⁡10=2.30 kBT\ln 10=2.30\,k_BT, so a single neutral contact buys well under one decade. Weights are legitimate; per-bond arithmetic on them is not.2

You can now write the equilibrium occupancy of any subnetwork whose states you can enumerate: assign weights, sum them, divide. Omitting an allowed state changes the denominator and therefore every probability on the page.

3Let binding reshape an ensemble

Before asking what equilibrium cannot do, see how much it already does: cooperativity and regulation both come out of equation (2) with no drive at all.

This section exists to make the next part surprising. A prohibition is only interesting once the room believes the thing being prohibited is powerful, so here are two regulatory behaviours that need no free energy whatsoever.

Cooperativity, from conformational selection. In the Monod–Wyman–Changeux model an oligomer with nn equivalent sites occupies two concerted conformations, a high-affinity RR state and a low-affinity TT state.3 Write LL for the ratio of TT to RR weights with no ligand bound, and KRK_R and KTK_T for the two dissociation constants. Every subunit binds independently within a conformation, so each conformation contributes a binomial factor and the ledger is

Z=(1+c/KR)n⏟R states+L (1+c/KT)n⏟T states.Z=\underbrace{(1+c/K_R)^{n}}_{R\text{ states}}+\underbrace{L\,(1+c/K_T)^{n}}_{T\text{ states}}. (5)

The fractional saturation follows by differentiating the logarithm of (5) with respect to ln⁡c\ln c, or equivalently by counting bound sites in each term:

Yˉ=(c/KR)(1+c/KR)n−1+L (c/KT)(1+c/KT)n−1(1+c/KR)n+L (1+c/KT)n.\bar Y=\frac{(c/K_R)(1+c/K_R)^{n-1}+L\,(c/K_T)(1+c/K_T)^{n-1}}{(1+c/K_R)^{n}+L\,(1+c/K_T)^{n}}. (6)

No term in (5) couples one bound ligand to another. The sharpness comes entirely from ligand binding shifting the population between the two conformations.

Now measure the sharpness the way an experimentalist would, as the steepest slope of ln⁡[Yˉ/(1−Yˉ)]\ln[\bar Y/(1-\bar Y)] against ln⁡c\ln c, which is what a Hill fit reports.4 Evaluating (6) with L=103L=10^3 and KT/KR=50K_T/K_R=50 gives a Hill coefficient of 1.001.00 for one site, 1.281.28 for two, and 2.672.67 for four.

Four sites, Hill coefficient 2.67

The effective Hill coefficient is not the site count and is always below it. It depends on LL and on KT/KRK_T/K_R as well as on nn, so a fitted Hill coefficient of 2.7 is evidence for at least three sites and never a measurement of how many there are.

Regulation, from competing weights. The same ledger describes a promoter. In the energy model of transcriptional regulation, a promoter is either empty, occupied by RNA polymerase, or occupied by a repressor, and the weights are set by copy numbers and binding energies.5 With PP polymerases and RR repressors competing against NNSN_{\mathrm{NS}} non-specific sites,

pbound=PNNSe−βΔεp1+PNNSe−βΔεp+RNNSe−βΔεr.p_{\mathrm{bound}}=\frac{\frac{P}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_p}}{1+\frac{P}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_p}+\frac{R}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_r}}. (7)

In the weak-promoter limit the polymerase term in the denominator is negligible and the whole effect of the repressor collapses into a single factor,

fold-change=(1+RNNSe−βΔεr)−1,\text{fold-change}=\left(1+\frac{R}{N_{\mathrm{NS}}}e^{-\beta\Delta\varepsilon_r}\right)^{-1}, (8)

which depends on the repressor copy number and its binding energy and on nothing else. That is a genuine prediction, and it is the reason the energy model is worth teaching: change RR by a known factor and (8) says exactly where the new curve must sit.

The test that separates a model from a fit. A single sigmoid can be fitted by many things. Perturb one measured ingredient instead, carry that number through the same partition function, and predict the new curve before measuring it. Agreement across several such interventions is what makes the ledger physical, and one fitted curve is not evidence of the mechanism behind it.

You can now produce sharp, tunable, cooperative regulation with no free energy at all, and you can state what a Hill coefficient does and does not measure. Everything after this point is about what this ledger cannot do.

Part 2

Test the graph

Occupancies alone do not identify equilibrium. The edges do.

4Separate three balances

Stationarity cancels the totals at each node. Detailed balance cancels each edge separately. The first does not imply the second.

Model the box as a continuous-time Markov chain on its states, with kijk_{ij} the rate from state ii to state jj and πi\pi_i the probability of state ii. Two conditions can be imposed, and they are not the same condition.

stationary:∑jπjkji=πi∑jkijfor every node i,detailed balance:πikij=πjkjifor every edge ij.\begin{aligned}\text{stationary:}\quad&\sum_j\pi_jk_{ji}=\pi_i\sum_jk_{ij}\quad\text{for every node }i,\\[2pt]\text{detailed balance:}\quad&\pi_ik_{ij}=\pi_jk_{ji}\quad\text{for every edge }ij.\end{aligned} (9)

The first line says that the total probability flowing into each state equals the total flowing out, which is exactly QTπ=0Q^{\mathsf T}\pi=0 for the generator QQ. The second says that each individual edge carries no net traffic. Detailed balance implies stationarity, because if every edge separately cancels then any sum of edges cancels. The converse is false, and the whole of Part 2 is about where it fails.

Only the second condition licenses equation (2). Boltzmann weights are what you get when every edge is separately balanced, because then the ratio of any two state probabilities is fixed by the ratio of the two rates connecting them, independent of everything else in the graph. Under stationarity alone, no such local statement is available, and the occupancies depend on the whole network.

Three words, three questions

Ergodic asks whether the chain visits everything and forgets where it started. Stationary asks whether the occupancies have stopped changing. At equilibrium asks whether every edge separately balances. A chain can be all three, or the first two and not the third, and it is the third that this lecture is about. The 2025 notes write the node equation and give it the name of the edge equation; the mathematics that follows there is right, and it is the label that decides whether a fitted occupancy counts as evidence.6

The practical consequence is sharp, and it is the reason the distinction is worth ten minutes. Occupancies are what an experiment usually measures, and occupancies alone cannot tell the two conditions apart. A stationary ring with probability circulating steadily around it presents constant occupancies at every node, exactly as an equilibrium system would. Fitting the numbers will succeed. The fit will also be meaningless as evidence about the mechanism.

You can now say what a fit to steady-state occupancies does not establish. If someone claims a system is at equilibrium because an equilibrium model fits the occupancies, you know the missing evidence is about edges, and §5 tells you where to look for it.

5Find the cycles

Whether stationarity forces equilibrium is a question about the shape of the graph, and it can be answered before any experiment.

On a tree, stationarity is equilibrium. Take any finite tree, meaning a connected graph with no closed loop. A tree has at least one leaf, a node with exactly one edge. At that leaf, all inflow and outflow travel on the single edge, so stationarity forces the current on it to be zero. Now delete the leaf and its edge. What remains is a smaller tree whose stationarity is unaffected, since the deleted edge carried nothing. Repeat. Every edge is stripped in turn and every edge current is zero, which is detailed balance.

That argument explains something you have already seen. Lecture 6's telegraph promoter switches between two states, on and off. Two states joined by one edge form a tree, so its stationary distribution is automatically an equilibrium one, no matter how lopsided the two rates are. Lecture 6 was entitled to its weights. It was entitled to them because of the graph's shape, not because the switching was fast.

On a cycle, it need not be. A cycle has no leaf, so the stripping argument never starts. Probability can circulate: each node's inflow balances its outflow while a steady current runs around the loop. For a three-state ring, stationarity at node 1 reads

(π2k21−π1k12)+(π3k31−π1k13)=0,(\pi_2k_{21}-\pi_1k_{12})+(\pi_3k_{31}-\pi_1k_{13})=0, (10)

and the two brackets can cancel each other while neither is zero. Requiring each bracket to vanish separately is the extra demand of detailed balance, and on a cycle it is a genuine restriction on the rate constants:

∏cyclekforward=∏cyclekreverse.\prod_{\mathrm{cycle}}k_{\mathrm{forward}}=\prod_{\mathrm{cycle}}k_{\mathrm{reverse}}. (11)

Equation (11) is Kolmogorov's criterion, and it must hold on every independent cycle of the graph. It follows from (9) by multiplying the edge conditions around the loop, where the πi\pi_i appear once in each position and cancel.

How many cycles you have to test is a counting question. Let the connected graph have NN nodes and EE reversible edges. Pick any spanning tree. It uses N−1N-1 edges and by the stripping argument carries nothing. Put back one of the leftover edges and it closes exactly one new loop, so the number of independent cycles is

ncyc=E−N+1.n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=E-N+1. (12)

Schnakenberg writes this count ν\nu and calls the leftover edges chords, which is the spelling to expect in the literature. A four-state square with four edges gives ncyc=4−4+1=1n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=4-4+1=1, so a single application of (11) settles it. A tree gives ncyc=0n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0, which is the first paragraph of this section again.

Real graphs are mixed, and the general statement is about edges rather than whole graphs. A promoter with a driven loop and a few dangling states is neither a tree nor a cycle, so neither argument above applies to it as written. Schnakenberg's network analysis settles the mixed case: an edge lying on no cycle carries zero stationary current, exactly as a leaf does.8 The current-carrying part of a graph is precisely its cycle space. Everything hanging off that space sits at equilibrium even while the loop it hangs from does not.

Two things are worth naming about what the criterion depends on. It is a condition on the topology and the rate constants, so it can be checked from a diagram with no data at all. And linearity is not what decides the matter: a linear chain and a linear ring differ here, while the ring's nonlinearity or lack of it is irrelevant. Closure and cycle structure are what count.7

You can now decide from a diagram alone whether an equilibrium description is even available: count ncyc=E−N+1n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=E-N+1, and test (11) on that many independent cycles. ncyc=0n_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0 settles the question in your favour with no data at all. Any branch hanging off the cycles is at equilibrium whatever the loop is doing.

6Measure affinity and current

Drive, flow and cost are three different quantities, and a claim that energy buys something has to name all three.

When (11) fails, the amount by which it fails is the natural measure of the drive. Define the dimensionless cycle affinity

A=ln⁡∏kforward∏kreverse,A=\ln\frac{\prod k_{\mathrm{forward}}}{\prod k_{\mathrm{reverse}}}, (13)

which is zero exactly when Kolmogorov's criterion holds and grows logarithmically with the imbalance. It is measured in units of kBTk_BT because each rate ratio is an exponentiated free energy difference, which is the identification made in §2 and used again here. This assumption has a name, local detailed balance: every individual transition is assumed to be a thermal process whose rate ratio reports the free energy it releases, even when the network as a whole is driven.

The affinity is a potential difference, so it says nothing on its own about how much traffic flows. The net current on an edge is

Jij=πikij−πjkji,J_{ij}=\pi_ik_{ij}-\pi_jk_{ji}, (14)

which vanishes on every edge at detailed balance. Around a simple loop, node balance makes this current the same on every edge, so a single number JJ describes the circulation. The rate at which the cycle dissipates free energy is the product of the two,

σ=J A,\sigma=J\,A, (15)

and both factors are needed. A large affinity across a blocked edge carries almost no current and costs almost nothing. Heavy traffic near balance has large gross flux and nearly zero net cost. Collapsing affinity, current and dissipation into one idea called "energy" is the commonest error in a nonequilibrium claim.

Both conditions are Kirchhoff laws on the graph, which is worth carrying as a name. Stationarity says what flows into a node flows out of it, and that is the current law. It holds in any steady state, driven or not. Equilibrium adds the demand that the drop around every loop vanish, and that is the voltage law, of which (11) is the multiplicative form. The general version of (15) is written in the same language: total dissipation is σ=∑αJαAα\sigma=\sum_\alpha J_\alpha A_\alpha over the ncycn_{\mathrm{cyc}} independent cycles counted in §5, so a network has exactly one affinity and one current per independent loop and no more.8

Now put a number on the budget. The cell's fuel is ATP hydrolysis, and the tabulated standard free energy is ΔG∘′=−30.5\Delta G^{\circ\prime}=-30.5 kJ/mol. That number alone is not what a cycle can spend, because a cell does not hold its nucleotides at standard concentrations. Add the mass-action term from equation (4):

ΔG=ΔG∘′+kBTln⁡[ADP][Pi][ATP].\Delta G=\Delta G^{\circ\prime}+k_BT\ln\frac{[\mathrm{ADP}][\mathrm{P_i}]}{[\mathrm{ATP}]}. (16)

Take the standard bacterial bracket: a few millimolar ATP, roughly a tenth as much ADP, and millimolar phosphate. With [ATP]=3[\mathrm{ATP}]=3 mM, [ADP]=0.3[\mathrm{ADP}]=0.3 mM and [Pi]=5[\mathrm{P_i}]=5 mM, the ratio inside the logarithm is 5×10−45\times10^{-4} M and its logarithm is −7.6-7.6. At 37 °C one kBTk_BT is 2.58 kJ/mol, so the standard term is −11.8 kBT-11.8\,k_BT and the total is

ΔG≈−11.8−7.6=−19.4 kBT≈−50 kJ/mol.\Delta G\approx-11.8-7.6=\pf{-19.4\,k_BT}\approx-50\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}. (17)
One hydrolysis, 19.4 kBTk_BT, and 8.4 decades

The mass-action term supplies nearly two fifths of the total, so quoting the standard value alone understates the cell's budget by that much. Divide by the 2.30 kBT2.30\,k_BT that buys one decade of KdK_d and a single hydrolysis is worth at most 8.4 decades of discrimination. That is the ceiling every energy claim in §7 and §8 is measured against.

You can now price a driven cycle: compute its affinity from (13), its current from (14), its dissipation from (15), and compare the affinity against the 19.4 kBT19.4\,k_BT that one hydrolysis provides.

Part 3

Name what energy buys

A nonequilibrium claim is incomplete until a forbidden equilibrium behaviour becomes a measured benefit.

7Unlock a forbidden response

Drive matters when it changes which input-output functions are attainable at all, and for one standard promoter that change can be stated as a theorem.

Take the four-state promoter square: the promoter is empty, or bound by a transcription factor, or bound by RNA polymerase, or bound by both. Write xx for the transcription-factor concentration in units of its dissociation constant, wPw_P for the polymerase weight, and ω\omega for the interaction between the two proteins when both are bound. At equilibrium the four weights are 11, xx, wPw_P and wPxωw_Px\omega, and the polymerase occupancy is the sum of the last two over the sum of all four:

pRNAP=wP+wPωx1+x+wP+wPωx=a+bxc+dx.p_{\mathrm{RNAP}}=\frac{w_P+w_P\omega x}{1+x+w_P+w_P\omega x}=\frac{a+bx}{c+dx}. (18)

Equation (18) is a fractional-linear function of xx, and every such function has derivative

ddxa+bxc+dx=bc−ad(c+dx)2.\frac{d}{dx}\frac{a+bx}{c+dx}=\frac{bc-ad}{(c+dx)^{2}}. (19)

The denominator of (19) is a square, so it is positive for every x>0x>0. The numerator contains no xx at all. So the derivative has one sign for the whole range, the response is monotone, and it cannot have an interior maximum. With wP=0.3w_P=0.3 and ω=8\omega=8 the numerator is bc−ad=2.1>0bc-ad=2.1>0 and the response rises everywhere.

The prohibition, stated exactly

For the four-state promoter square with at most one transcription factor bound per state, and with detailed balance holding, the polymerase occupancy is fractional-linear in the transcription-factor concentration and therefore monotone. No choice of the parameters wPw_P, ω\omega or KK produces an interior maximum. This is a statement about this state model, not about equilibrium in general.

Now break detailed balance on the same four states. Choose rates that violate (11) around the single independent cycle, solve the stationary distribution from (9), and read the polymerase occupancy off it. The cycle affinity of the rate set used here is 6.75 kBT6.75\,k_BT, comfortably inside the 19.4 kBT19.4\,k_BT that (17) says one hydrolysis provides. The response now rises from 0.3380.338 at low input to a maximum of 0.8760.876 near x=2.65x=2.65, then falls back to 0.3380.338.

Same four states, a shape equilibrium cannot make

Output returns to exactly where it started, so the non-monotonicity is unmistakable rather than a matter of curve-fitting. The affinity of 6.75 kBT6.75\,k_BT is about a third of one ATP, so a cell can afford this three times over. The number is a witness and not a universal price: different driven edges reach the same boundary at very different cost.9

This is where the audit acquires teeth. A measured interior maximum falsifies the whole equilibrium state model at once, no matter how many parameters it carries, because (19) rules out that shape for every parameter choice. That is a much stronger conclusion than any fit could deliver, and it costs nothing but the derivative.10

You can now convert an equilibrium model into a falsifiable prediction: derive the family of shapes it can produce, and look for a measurement outside that family. One observation of the right kind kills the model for every parameter value.

8Proofread with a reset loop

A fuel-coupled intermediate lets a wrong substrate leave twice, and two chances at the same discrimination square the error.

Start with what equilibrium binding can do. If a correct and an incorrect substrate differ by a binding free energy ΔGCD\Delta G_{CD}, the best achievable error fraction, meaning the rate of wrong product over the rate of right product at equal substrate concentrations, is

f0=e−ΔGCD/kBT.f_0=e^{-\Delta G_{CD}/k_BT}. (20)

That is a ceiling, and no amount of clever equilibrium binding beats it, because the discrimination available is exactly the free energy difference available.11 With the lecture-2 arithmetic, an error of 10−210^{-2} needs 2×2.30=4.6 kBT2\times2.30=4.6\,k_BT of discrimination.

Raising that ceiling is often not available, which is why a cell would pay instead. One DNA polymerase copies all four bases. dCTP is the correct substrate when the template reads G, and a dangerous analogue in the three other contexts, so a single site has to make four different discriminations and cannot be shaped around any one of them. Ninio drew the conclusion in 1975: accuracy has two strategies, a better binding site or a shrewder mechanism, and only the second is open to an enzyme with several accuracy problems at once.12

Chaining steps normally loses discrimination rather than compounding it. That is the result to have in hand before reading the construction below. Surveying reaction schemes, Ninio found that a multi-step process is generally less selective than its most selective step, because intermediates that both substrates reach dilute whatever discrimination was already made. Squaring is therefore not accumulation and it is not free. It needs a particular topology and a particular expenditure, and the rest of this section is about which ones.

Hopfield's construction inserts a second, driven intermediate between initial binding and product. The substrate binds, is transferred to an intermediate by a step that costs fuel and is insensitive to which substrate it is carrying, and only then commits to product. Both the first and the second state can release the substrate, and both releases read the same off-rate difference. The key point is that the second discrimination acts on a population that the first has already enriched by a factor f0f_0, so the two multiply:

f⟶f02when W≪ℓ1.f\longrightarrow f_0^{2}\qquad\text{when } W\ll \ell_1. (21)

Read the condition, because it names the knob. WW is Hopfield's incorporation rate, the flux that commits a bound substrate to product, and ℓ1\ell_1 is the off-rate from the driven intermediate. Squaring appears when commitment is slower than discard, so that a substrate reaching the intermediate is far more likely to be thrown out and re-tested than to be incorporated. That is what "a second chance to reject" means, stated as an inequality.

Ninio reached the same mechanism independently, presenting it at Roscoff in May 1974 and adding a comparison with Hopfield only after his own manuscript had been submitted, which is why the scheme carries both names. Evaluating the two-stage scheme with f0=10−2f_0=10^{-2} gives an error of 1.1×10−41.1\times10^{-4} at W/ℓ1=10−3W/\ell_1=10^{-3}, against the ideal f02=10−4f_0^2=10^{-4}, and it climbs back to 9.1×10−39.1\times10^{-3}, essentially f0f_0, at W/ℓ1=10W/\ell_1=10. Without the drive the scheme is a closed reversible sequence, which by §5 carries no cycle current and relaxes to equilibrium, where the ceiling of (20) reapplies. The mechanism does nothing at all without the drive, which is the cleanest possible demonstration that the energy is doing the work.

What the fuel actually purchased

Squaring the error is the same as doubling the binding free energy difference, in Hopfield's own words. So proofreading buys 4.6 kBT4.6\,k_BT of extra discrimination here, which is under a quarter of one hydrolysis from (17). The budget is not the binding constraint. The throughput is.

The bill has three lines and reporting one of them is not a result. Slowing commitment lowers the error toward its square, and the same move drops the throughput and raises the number of discards per product: at W/ℓ1=10−3W/\ell_1=10^{-3} the scheme discards a thousand bound substrates, correct ones included, for every product it makes. Error, throughput and fuel move together as the rates change, so a proofreading claim must report all three and say where in the network the discrimination happens.

Observed specificity on its own is not evidence of proofreading, and two alternatives have to be excluded. Structural complementarity can widen ΔGCD\Delta G_{CD} and raise the equilibrium ceiling (20) directly. Repeated equilibrium binding and release can produce long effective delays that mimic a reset. Distinguishing them takes resolving the intermediate's dwell time and perturbing the fuel-coupled step to see whether the error moves as the network predicts.

You can now recognize the signature of a proofreading scheme, and state its price in kBTk_BT: an error below the equilibrium ceiling (20), a driven intermediate whose removal restores that ceiling, and a throughput cost that moves in the opposite direction to the accuracy.

9Count independent samples

Free energy can shorten correlations, but it cannot manufacture independent observations, and that sets a floor no mechanism escapes.

A cell inferring a concentration is counting molecular arrivals, and counting is subject to Poisson statistics. If NN independent events are counted, the fractional uncertainty is N−1/2N^{-1/2}, so doubling the precision requires four times as many samples. The physical content of Berg and Purcell's analysis is in converting that statement into a statement about time, geometry and diffusion, since what limits NN is how fast fresh molecules arrive and how long the cell waits.13

For a perfect instrument monitoring a sphere of radius aa in a medium of diffusion constant DD and number density cc over a time TT, they obtain

δcrmsc=(5πDac T3)−1/2.\frac{\delta c_{\mathrm{rms}}}{c}=\left(\frac{5\pi D a c\,T}{3}\right)^{-1/2}. (22)

Every symbol in (22) is a resource. Put in a bacterium: a=1 μa=1\,\mum, D=10−9 m2/sD=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m^2/s} for a small molecule in water, and a ligand at 1 nM, which is 6.0×10176.0\times10^{17} molecules per cubic metre. One second of averaging gives

δcc∣T=1 s=1.8%,\frac{\delta c}{c}\Big|_{T=1\,\mathrm{s}}=\pf{1.8\%}, (23)

and reaching 1 % at that concentration takes 3.23.2 s. The inverse-square-root law is what makes this expensive: the same cell needs 320 s for 0.1 %.

A driven receptor changes what it can, which is the sample count and not the law. Spending energy to release a bound ligand, or to reset an adapted sensor, shortens the correlation time and so raises the number of effectively independent observations inside a fixed window.14 What it cannot do is move the exponent in (22) or extract more information than the arrivals carry. Energy and time are both resources, and they do not exchange at a universal rate.

You can now separate two claims that sound alike: energy that buys a better sampling strategy, which is real and bounded by (22), and energy that would buy precision outright, which no mechanism delivers.

10Audit the energy claim

Use equilibrium wherever it passes the audit, and add the smallest driven cycle that earns a named function.

The lecture's deliverable is a procedure, and it runs in eight steps.

  1. Boundary. Draw the modelled subnetwork and mark every chemostatted species, fuel included.
  2. Observable. Say whether the output is an occupancy, an edge flux, an error rate, a time, or a noise statistic.
  3. Equilibrium baseline. Enumerate the states, assign weights, and solve the equilibrium model first.
  4. Prohibition. Prove which behaviours that state model excludes, as in equation (19).
  5. Drive. Count the independent cycles from (12), test (11) on each, and compute its affinity from (13).
  6. Flow. Solve the stationary occupancies and the currents from (14).
  7. Benefit and cost. Report the function together with the dissipation (15), the delay, and the noise.
  8. Challenge. Set every cycle affinity to zero, keeping the same states and observables, and see whether the claimed function survives.

Step eight is what makes an energy claim falsifiable, so it is never the step that gets trimmed. If the function survives with all affinities set to zero, dissipation was not necessary for it and the energy in the story was decoration. Homework 6 supplies three cases to run this on: allostery, a four-state promoter, and stochastic binding.15

What this hands to lecture 8. Lecture 8 will restrict the whole cell to two kinds of reaction, binding that equilibrates and catalysis that does not, and it will summarize the binding layer by a partition function. This lecture is what licenses that move: the binding layer is exactly the part that passes the audit. The complement matters just as much. The driven promoter of §7, the proofreading loop of §8, and adaptive sensing all fail the audit, and they are precisely the parts lecture 8's binding layer cannot absorb.

The arrival

Equilibrium is not the opposite of life. It is a local privilege, earned when fast exchange on a graph without driven cycles erases current from the observable you are watching. Free energy becomes explanatory only when the current it maintains is drawn, computed, priced against the 19.4 kBT19.4\,k_BT in one hydrolysis, and connected to something the corresponding equilibrium graph provably cannot do.

You can now defend or refute an energy claim in eight steps, and you know which one of them most claims omit.
