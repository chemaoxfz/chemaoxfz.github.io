Which experiments identify an equilibrium mechanism?

Occupancies, response curves and sampled movies constrain different parts of a molecular model.

CCBS · Lecture 7 audience extension
Editorial synthesis of eight Week 4 student contributions · prepared with Codex assistance · 19 October 2026

An equilibrium curve does not determine how a molecule moves between its states. The core establishes this distinction through stationary probabilities and circulating currents. The audience contributions turn it into an experimental question: which additional measurement would distinguish the mechanisms that remain possible?

We will add information in three steps. First, an occupancy curve supplies static probabilities and their response to free ligand. Second, a timed experiment supplies a kinetic clock, but only through its sampling and measurement rules. Third, resolved traffic supplies a current and lets us ask what that current accomplishes. Each step identifies information the preceding experiment leaves undetermined.

Names link to accepted, commit-pinned submissions. The connecting arguments and independently recomputed figures are editorial additions. Numerical rate choices are illustrative models, not fitted biological measurements. The complementary Lecture 8 question is which molecular pool the experiment controls and measures.

Part I

Determine what the equilibrium curve actually fixes

A normalized formula supplies probabilities. Its physical interpretation requires more information.

1Separate a normalization rule from a physical mechanism

Shared algebra does not establish a shared mechanism. Weihan Luo compares equilibrium state weights with the normalized exponentials used in attention. If a state has a dimensionless score sis_i, the normalization

pi=esi∑jesjp_i=\frac{e^{s_i}}{\sum_j e^{s_j}}

produces nonnegative numbers summing to one. In a thermodynamic model, a score can be a negative free energy divided by thermal energy. In a computational model, a score can come from a similarity calculation. Normalization alone does not identify either construction.

A physical equilibrium interpretation also constrains transitions. With the course's column-vector convention, QijQ_{ij} is the transition hazard from state jj to state ii. Detailed balance requires Qijpj=QjipiQ_{ij}p_j=Q_{ji}p_i on every connected pair. Merely arranging Qp=0Qp=0 allows nonzero currents around cycles. The same stationary probabilities can therefore belong to different physical dynamics.

To use equilibrium binding weights predictively, specify the allowed states and the controlled reservoir. In the next section, cc is free ligand concentration maintained by a large reservoir. Replacing it by a finite total ligand concentration would require an additional conservation calculation.

Ask which state list, reservoir and transition constraints justify a normalized weight before treating the weight as a molecular mechanism.

2Connect the static slope to occupancy fluctuations

The slope of an equilibrium binding curve contains a precise fluctuation statement. Peng Su takes states with ligand counts nin_i and weights

wi=ai(c/c0)ni,Z=∑iwi,pi=wi/Z.w_i=a_i(c/c_0)^{n_i},\qquad Z=\sum_iw_i,\qquad p_i=w_i/Z.

Here c0c_0 is a fixed concentration scale. The positive coefficients aia_i include the state energies and any degeneracy, and do not change with cc. Define the mean ligand count as ⟨n⟩=∑inipi\langle n\rangle=\sum_i n_ip_i. Differentiating the numerator and normalization gives

dpidlog⁡c=(ni−⟨n⟩)pi,d⟨n⟩dlog⁡c=∑ini2pi−⟨n⟩2=Var⁡(n).\frac{dp_i}{d\log c}=(n_i-\langle n\rangle)p_i, \qquad \frac{d\langle n\rangle}{d\log c} =\sum_i n_i^2p_i-\langle n\rangle^2=\operatorname{Var}(n).

A steeper mean curve therefore accompanies larger equilibrium count fluctuations under these assumptions. At half occupancy, four independent identical sites have a binomial count distribution with mean two and variance one. An ideal concerted model that is empty or fully occupied with equal probability has the same mean and variance four. Its fractional occupancy θ=⟨n⟩/4\theta=\langle n\rangle/4 has four times the slope at the midpoint.

Static variance does not determine the precision of a timed measurement. Repeated observations of a slowly switching receptor remain correlated. Bialek and Setayeshgar explicitly distinguish increased mean sensitivity from improved detectability and include diffusion and dynamical noise in that comparison. Their result does not say that cooperativity is useless. It says that an increased slope alone does not remove the physical noise floor.1

We therefore need a measurement of the clock before interpreting a steep response as a fast or precise sensor. The next two experiments hold affinity fixed while changing the measurement protocol.

Use the equilibrium slope to infer a count variance only under the stated weight model. Measure temporal correlations separately when assessing a finite observation record.

Part II

Include the input protocol and observation clock

Affinity fixes a ratio of rates. Dynamic experiments can reveal the rates, provided the readout and sampling law are retained.

3Choose the readout before setting an accuracy tolerance

The same receptor needs different amounts of time for different predictions to become accurate. Kehan Lang asks whether a receptor follows an oscillating ligand. Xiaomeng Zhu asks how much output accumulates during a finite pulse. Both questions use the two-state equation

p˙=k+c(t)(1−p)−k−p,Kd=k−/k+.\dot p=k_+c(t)(1-p)-k_-p,\qquad K_d=k_-/k_+.

Here pp is bound probability, k+k_+ has units of concentration inverse per time, and k−k_- has units of inverse time. At held concentration c0c_0, the equilibrium occupancy is p0=c0/(Kd+c0)p_0=c_0/(K_d+c_0). The relaxation rate is λ=k+c0+k−\lambda=k_+c_0+k_-, with relaxation time trelax=1/λt_\mathrm{relax}=1/\lambda. Multiplying both rates by a common factor leaves affinity unchanged and changes this time.

A small oscillation tests amplitude and phase separately. Write c(t)=c0+δc(t)c(t)=c_0+\delta c(t) and p(t)=p0+δp(t)p(t)=p_0+\delta p(t). Keeping first-order terms gives δp˙+λδp=k+(1−p0)δc\delta\dot p+\lambda\delta p=k_+(1-p_0)\delta c. For a settled sinusoid of angular frequency ω\omega,

dynamic amplitudeequilibrium amplitude=11+(ω/λ)2,phase lag=arctan⁡(ω/λ).\frac{\text{dynamic amplitude}}{\text{equilibrium amplitude}} =\frac1{\sqrt{1+(\omega/\lambda)^2}},\qquad \text{phase lag}=\arctan(\omega/\lambda).

A loss of at most 10% in amplitude permits ω/λ≤0.484\omega/\lambda\leq0.484. A phase lag of at most ten degrees requires ω/λ≤0.176\omega/\lambda\leq0.176. These are different specifications for the same linear response. The formula applies to small perturbations after the transient has decayed.

A constant pulse permits an exact comparison of endpoint and accumulated output. Start with an unbound receptor and hold the ligand for a duration TT. During that interval, p(t)=p0(1−e−λt)p(t)=p_0(1-e^{-\lambda t}). Suppose the output rate is proportional to occupancy and does not alter receptor dynamics. Count only output formed during the pulse. The two fractions of their equilibrium predictions are

p(T)p0=1−e−x,∫0Tp(t) dtp0T=1−1−e−xx,x=λT.\frac{p(T)}{p_0}=1-e^{-x},\qquad \frac{\int_0^T p(t)\,dt}{p_0T}=1-\frac{1-e^{-x}}x, \qquad x=\lambda T.

The endpoint reaches 95% at x=2.996x=2.996. The integrated output needs approximately x=20x=20. A nearly settled receptor cannot retroactively make the output lost while occupancy was rising. This explains the slower convergence of the integrated readout.

State whether the experiment needs endpoint occupancy, accumulated output, amplitude or phase. Derive the timing criterion for that observable.

4Infer physical rates through the sampling and measurement laws

A difference between consecutive samples does not count every molecular jump. Haobo Zheng examines a symmetric two-state process with hazard kk in both directions. The number of jumps in an interval Δ\Delta is Poisson with mean kΔk\Delta. The endpoints differ only when the number of jumps is odd. Summing those probabilities gives

pΔ=1−e−2kΔ2,k=−log⁡(1−2pΔ)2Δ.p_\Delta=\frac{1-e^{-2k\Delta}}2, \qquad k=-\frac{\log(1-2p_\Delta)}{2\Delta}.

Using pΔ/Δp_\Delta/\Delta as a rate misses all even-return histories and increasingly underestimates kk. The exact inverse has a different limitation. Its derivative is dk/dpΔ=1/[Δ(1−2pΔ)]dk/dp_\Delta=1/[\Delta(1-2p_\Delta)], which grows without bound near one half. A coarse movie makes the rate hard to estimate even when the model is correct.

A finite data set can yield an estimated transition probability at or above one half. That does not establish an infinite molecular hazard. It places the estimate outside the finite-rate symmetric model's range. The appropriate response is to retain statistical uncertainty and test the model or improve the sampling interval.

The observed signal may not directly reveal a state at all. Chengwei Jin makes this issue concrete with noisy fluorescence. A threshold can label noise excursions as transitions. A hidden Markov model instead specifies both a transition matrix for unobserved states and an emission distribution for the measured signal conditional on each state. Chodera and colleagues use this separation for single-molecule force spectroscopy, including uncertainty in the inferred parameters.2

The transition matrix still has to be converted to continuous-time rates. For a two-state process, let u=Pr⁡(1∣0)u=\Pr(1\mid0) and v=Pr⁡(0∣1)v=\Pr(0\mid1) over one interval. If the constant hazards are aa from 0 to 1 and bb from 1 to 0, then

r=−log⁡(1−u−v)Δ,a=ruu+v,b=rvu+v,0<u+v<1.r=-\frac{\log(1-u-v)}\Delta, \qquad a=\frac{ru}{u+v},\qquad b=\frac{rv}{u+v}, \qquad 0<u+v<1.

This formula accounts for unresolved returns. Fitting an emission model does not by itself prove that the selected number of hidden states or constant-hazard assumption is correct. The distinction becomes stronger with three states: even perfect endpoint observations can leave the generator ambiguous.

Keep physical hazards, sampled transition probabilities and measurement emissions separate. Test each conversion before assigning a molecular rate.

Part III

Resolve molecular traffic and then ask what it achieves

A current can be invisible to one observation protocol. When it is resolved, its function still requires a declared output and cost.

5Test whether a sampled movie can hide a circulating current

Sampling can erase a current exactly, rather than merely make it difficult to estimate. Zixuan Wang constructs a homogeneous three-state ring. Clockwise transitions have hazard aa, and counterclockwise transitions have hazard bb. With states ordered 0, 1, 2, the generator is

Q=(−(a+b)baa−(a+b)bba−(a+b)).Q=\begin{pmatrix}-(a+b)&b&a\\a&-(a+b)&b\\b&a&-(a+b)\end{pmatrix}.

Its stationary distribution is uniform for every positive a,ba,b. The clockwise current on each edge is J=(a−b)/3J=(a-b)/3. Compare it with a detailed-balanced ring whose two directional hazards both equal c=(a+b)/2c=(a+b)/2. That ring has the same uniform distribution and zero current.

The generators differ, but their sampled transition matrices can coincide. Write Q=S+AQ=S+A, where S=(Q+Q⊤)/2S=(Q+Q^\top)/2 is the comparison generator and A=(Q−Q⊤)/2A=(Q-Q^\top)/2. These two matrices commute. On the two-dimensional subspace orthogonal to the uniform vector, SS acts as multiplication by −3c-3c, while AA generates a rotation with angular frequency Ω=3(a−b)/2\Omega=\sqrt3(a-b)/2.

A complete rotation disappears between samples. For a≠ba\ne b, choose Δ∗=2π/∣Ω∣\Delta_*=2\pi/|\Omega|. Then eAΔ∗=Ie^{A\Delta_*}=I and

eQΔ∗=eSΔ∗=Π+e−3cΔ∗(I−Π),Π=1311⊤.e^{Q\Delta_*}=e^{S\Delta_*} =\Pi+e^{-3c\Delta_*}(I-\Pi),\qquad \Pi=\frac13\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top.

The equality concerns the entire transition matrix. With the same initial distribution, every finite sequence of sampled endpoints therefore has the same probability under the two models. An arbitrarily long movie at this exact interval cannot distinguish them. With a stationary uniform start, the common sampled movie also satisfies detailed balance.

The exact ambiguity depends on the specified ring and interval. It is not a claim that every driven mechanism is indistinguishable from equilibrium at every sampling rate. A shorter second sampling interval separates this pair. Recording individual jump directions separates it directly. A measurement designed to test equilibrium should therefore challenge the observation protocol as well as the fitted stationary distribution.

Test a proposed equilibrium mechanism with more than one sampling interval or with resolved directional traffic. Equal sampled transition matrices can hide unequal physical currents.

6Count the output and cost of selective rejection

A resolved current makes a functional question possible: what does continued processing buy? Lipei Fang asks this question for proofreading, including the fuel consumed by unsuccessful attempts. Hopfield's mechanism uses a driven intermediate and a rejection route to obtain discrimination beyond the corresponding equilibrium limit. The original paper also proposes fuel-per-product measurements as evidence for rejection after activation.3

A simple attempt model separates fidelity from yield. Suppose attempts enter a post-activation state with wrong-to-correct arrival ratio f0f_0, where 0<f0<10<f_0<1. An attempt commits to product at hazard WW. Correct attempts are rejected at hazard f0ℓf_0\ell, while wrong attempts are rejected at hazard ℓ\ell. These are effective state-transition hazards. No elementary chemical mechanism is asserted by this bookkeeping model.

Competing exponential exits determine the chance of success. For a correct attempt it is sR=W/(W+f0ℓ)s_R=W/(W+f_0\ell). For a wrong attempt it is sW=W/(W+ℓ)s_W=W/(W+\ell). Weighting these outcomes by their incoming frequencies gives

f=wrong productscorrect products=f0W+f0ℓW+ℓ,Y=all productsall attempts=sR+f0sW1+f0.f=\frac{\text{wrong products}}{\text{correct products}} =f_0\frac{W+f_0\ell}{W+\ell},\qquad Y=\frac{\text{all products}}{\text{all attempts}} =\frac{s_R+f_0s_W}{1+f_0}.

Reducing W/ℓW/\ell gives rejection more opportunity to discriminate. The error ratio approaches f02f_0^2, but the yield approaches zero. For a fixed arrival flux JarrJ_\mathrm{arr}, product flux is JarrYJ_\mathrm{arr}Y. The model does not determine that arrival flux from enzyme abundance or cycle duration, so yield alone is not a throughput prediction.

A fuel count needs an explicit convention. If each arrival consumes exactly one fuel molecule, successful and rejected attempts together give 1/Y1/Y fuel molecules per product. That quantity is neither energy dissipation nor work. Converting it into an energy account needs the fuel chemical potential difference and any other coupled reactions. This distinction preserves Lipei's accounting question without assigning an energy cost the attempt model does not contain.

The next experiment can now be stated precisely. For a putative equilibrium receptor, measure its static occupancy curve, repeat its movie at two short sampling intervals, and independently count its output events. Determine whether one state model explains all three. If it does not, ask whether the missing ingredient is an observation rule, an unresolved state, or a maintained chemical current.

Report error, successful-attempt fraction, output flux and fuel count as different observables. Specify the extra measurements needed to connect them.
