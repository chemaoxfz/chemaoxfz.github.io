Stochastic dynamics: the master equation and Gillespie

A molecular reaction is an event. Give every event an integer jump and a state-dependent clock, then derive the probability equation, exact sample paths, and the deterministic limit from the same object.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 5 · core page (full) · 2026-09-15 (Tue) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

Lectures 3 and 4 taught us to turn reaction arrows into x˙=Sv(x)\dot{x}=Sv(x), simulate the resulting trajectory, and ask what behaviors the vector field permits. That is substantial power. It still leaves out a question an individual cell can force us to answer: which reaction happened next, and when?

The original repressilator makes the gap concrete. Its three-repressor network was designed with a deterministic model that admitted sustained oscillations. Individual lineages oscillated, but their periods and amplitudes varied, and some peaks were delayed or omitted. The authors identified stochastic fluctuations as one possible source.1 A smooth trajectory and an irregular single-cell record are not rival pictures of the same observable. They are different observables.

The claim

At molecular scale, a reaction network is an integer state moved by random events. State-change vectors and event clocks define the process. Probability conservation gives the chemical master equation. Competing clocks give Gillespie paths. The biological observable decides whether a path, a distribution, or the deterministic limit is the honest description.

Part 1

Find the stochastic object

The word noise is too vague. Begin with an observable, count the relevant events, and then specify the process that generates them.

1A smooth trajectory is not a single cell

A deterministic model can be correct for a concentration and unable to answer the path-level question.

Suppose half the cells contain no transcript and half contain twenty. Their mean is ten, although no cell is near ten. Or suppose every cell eventually reaches the same level, but the time of the first transcript determines whether a downstream switch fires. In both cases a mean trajectory suppresses the observation that carries the biology.

There are three questions to keep separate from the first minute:

objectquestion it answersexample observable
one path X(t)X(t)what happened to this cell, and when?first-passage time, pulse duration, event order
a distribution p(x,t)p(x,t)how likely is each possible state?probability of zero, tail probability, fraction above threshold
a smooth state x(t)x(t)what macroscopic concentration trajectory emerges?large-population mean level or deterministic attractor

A stochastic model is warranted when the observable depends on an integer boundary, a distribution, an event time, or an individual history. The word cell is not enough. A millimolar metabolite in a bacterium may be well described by concentration, while one promoter copy remains discrete in a much larger cell.

Room check, 60 seconds. A population-average assay reports ten transcripts per cell. List two single-cell distributions with that mean and different probabilities of zero. Which downstream behavior could distinguish them?

2Count the events before calling them noise

Molecule count sets one discreteness scale. The number of events during the observable's memory time sets another.

For molar concentration cc, volume VV in liters, and the exact Avogadro constant NA=6.02214076×1023 mol−1N_A=6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1}, the expected molecule count is

N=cVNA.N=cVN_A. (1)

Carry the units. A regulator at 5 nM5\,\mathrm{nM} in a 2 fL2\,\mathrm{fL} cell gives

N=(5×10−9 mol L−1)(2×10−15 L)(6.02214076×1023 mol−1)=6.02  molecules.N=(5\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{mol\,L}^{-1})(2\times10^{-15}\,\mathrm{L})(6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol}^{-1})=\pf{6.02\;\text{molecules}}. (2)

By contrast, 1 μM1\,\mu\mathrm{M} in 1 fL1\,\mathrm{fL} is 602.214602.214 molecules. One molecule in that volume is 1.66054 nM1.66054\,\mathrm{nM}. These are conversions, not thresholds. Whether six copies matter depends on the mechanism and the measured statistic.

Now count events. If independent events occur at constant total rate aa for a time TT, their number is Poisson with parameter λ=aT\lambda=aT. Its mean and variance are both λ\lambda, so

CV=Var⁡(K)⟨K⟩=λ−1/2.\mathrm{CV}=\frac{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(K)}}{\langle K\rangle}=\lambda^{-1/2}. (3)

Ten thousand effective events give a relative scale near one percent. One event gives order-one variation. The relevant count may be smaller than the protein abundance. A short-lived mRNA can be translated many times, so a large protein pool may be refreshed by only a few transcript initiations during one protein lifetime. Single-molecule transcript counts make those expression episodes directly visible.7 The event count is the bridge from copy number to mechanism.

Variation is not a mechanism

Reaction-event stochasticity, shared but unobserved cell state, and measurement error can all broaden a dataset. Paired reporters expose reporter-specific and shared components through their covariance.56 A stochastic reaction model describes the first source conditional on its chosen state. It does not turn every unexplained residual into intrinsic noise.

3One reaction list becomes integer jumps and clocks

The stochastic model is specified before the random numbers appear.

Write reaction rr with reactant vector αr\alpha_r, product vector βr\beta_r, and change vector νr=βr−αr\nu_r=\beta_r-\alpha_r. This is the same event accounting used in Lecture 3. The state is now the count vector

X(t)=(N1(t),…,Nn(t))T∈Z≥0n.X(t)=(N_1(t),\ldots,N_n(t))^T\in\mathrm{Z}_{\geq0}^{n}. (4)

The new object is the propensity ar(x)a_r(x). It is defined by the short-time probability

Pr⁡{one r event in [t,t+dt)∣X(t)=x}=ar(x)dt+o(dt).\Pr\{\text{one }r\text{ event in }[t,t+dt)\mid X(t)=x\}=a_r(x)dt+o(dt). (5)

A propensity has units of events per time. It counts eligible reactant combinations in the present state. Constant addition ∅⇝X\varnothing\rightsquigarrow X has jump +1+1 and clock bb. First-order removal X⇝∅X\rightsquigarrow\varnothing has jump −1-1 and clock dnXdn_X. These squiggles declare composite events, so their clocks are stated separately. For an elementary channel A+B→kCA+B\xrightarrow{k}C, the count-scale clock is proportional to nAnBn_A n_B. For elementary 2A→kC2A\xrightarrow{k}C, eligible unordered pairs number nA(nA−1)/2n_A(n_A-1)/2.

That last expression is not cosmetic. It switches the channel off when fewer than two AA molecules exist. The state, jump, and propensity together guarantee that an event cannot consume molecules that are absent.

Part 2

Turn events into probability

A chemical master equation is a conservation law on the state graph. Derive it by following arrows, not by memorizing indices.

4Probability bookkeeping becomes dynamics

Probability reaches a state through predecessor events and leaves it through clocks running there.

Let p(x,t)=Pr⁡{X(t)=x}p(x,t)=\Pr\{X(t)=x\}. To occupy state xx at time t+dtt+dt, one of two mutually exclusive things happens to first order in dtdt:

  1. The system was already at xx and no reaction fired.
  2. It was at predecessor x−νrx-\nu_r and reaction rr fired once.

The probability of two or more events is o(dt)o(dt). Therefore

p(x,t+dt)=p(x,t)[1−∑rar(x)dt]+∑rp(x−νr,t)ar(x−νr)dt+o(dt).p(x,t+dt)=p(x,t)\left[1-\sum_r a_r(x)dt\right]+\sum_r p(x-\nu_r,t)a_r(x-\nu_r)dt+o(dt). (6)

Subtract p(x,t)p(x,t), divide by dtdt, and take the limit:

∂p(x,t)∂t=∑r[ar(x−νr)p(x−νr,t)−ar(x)p(x,t)].\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}=\sum_r\left[a_r(x-\nu_r)p(x-\nu_r,t)-a_r(x)p(x,t)\right]. (7)

This is the chemical master equation. The first term for channel rr is evaluated at the predecessor because its clock rings before the jump. The second term removes probability from the state being described. The equation is linear in pp, even if propensities are nonlinear in xx.

For constant addition and first-order removal, write pn(t)=Pr⁡{N(t)=n}p_n(t)=\Pr\{N(t)=n\}. Reading every arrow that touches count nn gives

p˙n=bpn−1+d(n+1)pn+1−(b+dn)pn.\dot p_n=b p_{n-1}+d(n+1)p_{n+1}-(b+dn)p_n. (8)

5Zero is a biological boundary

Negative counts are not states. Which clocks remain at zero determines what the biology can do next.

At n=0n=0, the removal propensity dndn is zero. There is no state −1-1, so set p−1=0p_{-1}=0 or omit that predecessor explicitly. Equation (8) becomes

p˙0=dp1−bp0.\dot p_0=d p_1-bp_0. (9)

Now sum equation (8) over every non-negative count. Shift the index in each incoming sum. Every internal addition flow cancels one outgoing addition flow, and every removal flow cancels in the same way. The result is

ddt∑n=0∞pn(t)=0.\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_n(t)=0. (10)

This is the probability analogue of a conservation law. If the sum does not remain one, an index, boundary, or unlisted outside state is wrong.

The biological meaning can be stronger. For an autocatalyst whose addition clock is proportional to its own count, both addition and removal stop at zero. Zero is then absorbing. A deterministic solution may approach zero without ever reaching it, while a molecular path can hit zero exactly and never recover.

6One solvable system calibrates the model

Constant addition plus first-order removal makes equation, distribution, moments, and simulation meet at one answer.

At stationarity, neighboring probability flows balance:

bpn=d(n+1)pn+1⟹pn+1=b/dn+1pn.b p_n=d(n+1)p_{n+1}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_{n+1}=\frac{b/d}{n+1}p_n. (11)

Define λ=b/d\lambda=b/d. Iteration gives pn=p0λn/n!p_n=p_0\lambda^n/n!. Normalization finishes the derivation:

1=∑n=0∞pn=p0∑n=0∞λnn!=p0eλ⟹pn=e−λλnn!.1=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_n=p_0\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^n}{n!}=p_0e^{\lambda}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_n=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^n}{n!}. (12)

The stationary count is Poisson with

⟨N⟩=Var⁡(N)=λ=bd,F=Var⁡(N)⟨N⟩=1.\langle N\rangle=\operatorname{Var}(N)=\lambda=\frac{b}{d},\qquad F=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(N)}{\langle N\rangle}=1. (13)

The moment calculation reaches the same result without solving all probabilities. For any function gg of the count, the event generator is

(Lg)(n)=b[g(n+1)−g(n)]+dn[g(n−1)−g(n)].(\mathcal{L}g)(n)=b[g(n+1)-g(n)]+dn[g(n-1)-g(n)]. (14)

Using g(n)=ng(n)=n and then g(n)=n2g(n)=n^2 gives

d⟨N⟩dt=b−d⟨N⟩,dVar⁡(N)dt=b+d⟨N⟩−2dVar⁡(N).\frac{d\langle N\rangle}{dt}=b-d\langle N\rangle,\qquad \frac{d\operatorname{Var}(N)}{dt}=b+d\langle N\rangle-2d\operatorname{Var}(N). (15)

For b=4 min−1b=4\,\mathrm{min}^{-1} and d=0.2 min−1d=0.2\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}, theory gives mean and variance 2020. Five thousand seeded event simulations give sample mean 19.97519.975 and unbiased sample variance 19.56719.567. Those numbers come from the lecture's reproducible analysis ledger.9

Why this closes

The propensities are affine in count, so the mean and variance equations close. If a clock contains N2N^2, then the mean equation contains ⟨N2⟩\langle N^2\rangle, whose equation can contain a third moment. Agreement between the deterministic equation and the exact stochastic mean is special here.

Part 3

Make histories and limits agree

The master equation evolves a distribution. Gillespie's direct method samples one path from that same law without stepping through empty time.

7Sample the event, not the timestep

The state remains fixed between events, so the next waiting time can be drawn directly.

A small-timestep simulator repeatedly asks whether an event occurred during Δt\Delta t. It becomes slow when most steps are empty and biased when Δt\Delta t is too large. The direct method asks a different question: how long until the next event?

At state xx, let a0(x)=∑rar(x)a_0(x)=\sum_r a_r(x). Let S(τ)S(\tau) be the probability that no event occurs during the next τ\tau. Conditional on no event, the state and every propensity are unchanged. Therefore

S(τ+dτ)=S(τ)[1−a0dτ]⟹dSdτ=−a0S,S(0)=1.S(\tau+d\tau)=S(\tau)[1-a_0d\tau]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{dS}{d\tau}=-a_0S,\qquad S(0)=1. (16)

Solving gives S(τ)=e−a0τS(\tau)=e^{-a_0\tau}. If u1u_1 is uniform on (0,1)(0,1), inverse-transform sampling gives the next waiting time

τ=−ln⁡u1a0(x).\tau=-\frac{\ln u_1}{a_0(x)}. (17)

Which channel fires? Independent exponential clocks race. Conditional on an event, channel rr wins with probability

Pr⁡{r∣an event}=ar(x)a0(x).\Pr\{r\mid\text{an event}\}=\frac{a_r(x)}{a_0(x)}. (18)

Now perform one complete step. At n=3n=3, with b=4b=4 and d=0.2d=0.2 per minute, the two propensities are a+=4a_+=4 and a−=0.6a_-=0.6, so a0=4.6a_0=4.6. Take u1=0.25u_1=0.25. Equation (17) gives τ=0.3014 min\tau=0.3014\,\mathrm{min}. Take u2=0.93u_2=0.93. Its threshold is u2a0=4.278u_2a_0=4.278. Addition occupies [0,4)[0,4) and removal occupies [4,4.6)[4,4.6), so removal wins. Advance the time, update n:3↦2n:3\mapsto2, and rebuild both clocks.

  1. Compute every ar(x)a_r(x) and their sum a0a_0.
  2. If a0=0a_0=0, the state is absorbing. Stop.
  3. Draw u1,u2u_1,u_2 independently and uniformly from (0,1)(0,1).
  4. Set τ=−ln⁡(u1)/a0\tau=-\ln(u_1)/a_0.
  5. Choose the first channel whose cumulative propensity exceeds u2a0u_2a_0.
  6. Update t←t+τt\leftarrow t+\tau and x←x+νrx\leftarrow x+\nu_r.
  7. Recompute propensities in the new state and repeat.

8One model has three honest views

A path, a probability distribution, and a deterministic trajectory are related outputs, not interchangeable evidence.

A direct-method run is one possible history. Repeating the same initial condition creates an empirical distribution at a chosen time. Solving the CME creates that distribution directly. Averaging many paths estimates ⟨N(t)⟩\langle N(t)\rangle. For this affine process, equation (15) says that mean follows the deterministic equation exactly. No individual cell follows the smooth mean.

Every comparison needs three labels:

  1. Initial condition. A stationary sample and a population initialized at zero answer different questions.
  2. Observation time. At t≪1/dt\ll1/d, the ensemble has not reached its stationary Poisson law.
  3. Averaging operation. A time average along one path equals an ensemble average only under suitable stationarity and ergodicity conditions.

The deterministic limit is also a declared comparison. Scale the volume by Ω\Omega while holding concentration and macroscopic rate constants fixed. Counts grow with Ω\Omega. For a Poisson reference, relative fluctuations shrink as Ω−1/2\Omega^{-1/2}. More generally, density-dependent jump processes converge to deterministic chemical kinetics under the scaling made precise by Kurtz.4

The boundary can survive the limit question

A large mean may justify an ODE near its central trajectory while leaving a rare extinction or threshold-crossing probability biologically decisive. Model adequacy belongs to the observable, not to copy number alone.

Part 4

Make a biological claim

An exact algorithm cannot rescue an incomplete state. Finish by naming the world the clocks describe and the observation that could prove it inadequate.

9Exact for a stated world

The direct method exactly samples the stated well-mixed Markov jump process.

The exactness claim has four hypotheses. Molecular amounts are represented as discrete counts. The reaction volume is well mixed. Every hazard is determined by the current modeled state. Propensities stay fixed between reaction events. Under those assumptions, the direct method has no timestep approximation and samples the same path law defined by the CME.23

It is not exact molecular physics. Spatial gradients can make location part of the state. Crowding and rebinding can create memory. Transcriptional elongation can create a non-exponential delay. Hidden conformations can change a waiting-time law. Cell growth changes volume and therefore count-scale propensities. Each failure points to a repair: enlarge the state, add space or age, or change the model class.

10Make the observable choose the model

A stochastic model is complete only when it names what it predicts and what would reject it.

For a new biological network, produce this six-line contract:

  1. State. Name each integer component and the legal state space.
  2. Events. List every reaction, jump vector, and propensity with units.
  3. Probability. Write one interior CME equation and every special boundary equation.
  4. Path. Hand-trace one direct-method event with both uniform draws recorded.
  5. Observable. Choose a mean, Fano factor, zero probability, waiting time, or first-passage statistic.
  6. Falsifier. Name one data pattern the model cannot produce and one missing state or process that could repair it.

For example, a Fano factor greater than one rejects the constant-addition, first-order-removal Poisson baseline. It does not identify the replacement. Promoter switching, translation packets, feedback, shared cell-cycle state, and mixed subpopulations can all widen a count distribution. Models that agree on a histogram can disagree on inter-event waiting times.8 Keep event times when the claim concerns a mechanism's internal steps.

The core story

A smooth trajectory leaves event order and probability invisible. Molecule and event counts reveal when that matters. Jumps and clocks define the stochastic reaction network. Probability flow gives the CME. A legal zero closes it. The Poisson system calibrates analysis against simulation. Survival and competition give Gillespie. System-size scaling earns the ODE. The observable and its falsifier decide which view is useful.

Lecture 6 begins where this contract becomes difficult. If promoter binding or an enzyme complex is much faster than the observed species, can it be removed? A deterministic slow trajectory may survive while a waiting-time distribution, correlation, or rare transition does not. Reduction is therefore a claim about which observable is preserved.
