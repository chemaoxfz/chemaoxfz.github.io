From logical responses to controlled cell-state decisions

How physical memory, promoter logic, sequestration and synthetic circuit experiments changed the questions asked of molecular computation.

CCBS · Lecture 12 · lineage · 19 October 2026

Molecular computation developed through several distinct questions. Can physical dynamics represent a logical or numerical operation? Can molecular components implement that operation? Can several components be connected? Can a transient signal choose a state that a living cell retains? Each question demands evidence beyond the previous one.

The core uses these questions to reconsider the architecture of cellular computation. This lineage explains what selected researchers were trying to establish and what their evidence permits. The exposition supplies the molecular balances and calculations. The historical account below distinguishes conceptual parallels from documented implementations. It does not claim that all these programmes form one chain of direct influence.

Part I

Give physical dynamics a computational interpretation

The first question concerns what the system's state and response mean, before asking how molecules realize them.

1The engineering analogy contains several different research programmes

The logic-gate analogy asks whether a molecular response can implement a truth table. Low and high concentration ranges acquire logical labels, and a reaction network maps one set of labels to another. The analogy is precise only after the input encoding and the measured output have been stated.

The neural-network analogy asks how many inputs can be combined before applying a nonlinear response. In a weighted threshold unit, the abstract operation is a signed comparison. The molecular implementation must explain how nonnegative concentrations and rates supply the signed influence. Layered composition then adds its own interface and timing requirements.

The state-space interpretation asks a different question: can the system retain or reconstruct a choice? The relevant object is a trajectory and its destination, not just a single-valued response curve. A circuit can have a steep response without memory. It can have several attracting states without a usable way to select them.

These questions coexist in the historical record. Experimental gate libraries remain valuable even when a binding-based state-selection architecture suits another task. The core's gate and weight counts are therefore tests of proposed architectures. They do not establish a historical verdict that cells abandoned logic gates or that neural networks cannot describe biological computation.

Identify whether a historical computing claim concerns representation, molecular implementation, composition or retained state selection before comparing it with another claim.

2Hopfield made attraction in state space a concrete memory operation

Hopfield's 1982 paper asked whether useful computation could emerge collectively from many simple interacting units. Its target was content-addressable memory: an incomplete or imperfect pattern should lead the network to the stored pattern it represents. The physical picture was a flow toward locally stable states from surrounding regions of state space.1

The model used binary units that updated asynchronously according to weighted inputs. The stored patterns were encoded through the couplings. For symmetric interactions and the stated update rule, Hopfield exhibited an energy-like function that could not increase when a unit updated. That supplied a reason for convergence in the special model, rather than merely a visual resemblance between a state-space landscape and memory.

What the correspondence establishes

An attracting state can represent a stored item, and its basin can correct a partially corrupted input. A chemical circuit inherits this interpretation only after its initial molecular state encodes the input and its attracting output encodes the desired answer. It does not inherit symmetric interactions, an energy function or a learning rule simply by being multistable.

The distinction between possible patterns and stable memories already matters in this construction. A binary vector of length NN has 2N2^N possible values. The coupling rule does not make all those values independently stable and accurately retrievable. The number of usable memories depends on how patterns are embedded and which perturbations are tolerated.

This provides a useful conceptual comparison for cell-fate circuits. An expression state can retain history through attraction and feedback. Yet a cell-fate experiment need not implement associative recall from a corrupted pattern, and a synthetic multistable circuit need not learn its states from experience. The shared object is attracting dynamics. The computational task can differ.

Use the state-space interpretation to explain memory, while checking separately how inputs, stored states and convergence are implemented.

3Promoter-state models put complex logic inside a molecular component

Buchler, Gerland and Hwa asked in 2003 how much combinatorial regulation a single cis-regulatory region could implement. Their starting point was a contrast with an integrated circuit: a gene-regulatory node may be slow, but its response to several regulators can be sophisticated. They therefore examined the internal molecular states of the node, rather than treating it as an unexplained activation or repression symbol.2

The model assigned statistical weights to promoter configurations. Site affinities, steric exclusion and cooperative interactions determined their occupancies. RNA-polymerase occupancy supplied the transcriptional readout. Concentrations remained continuous. Boolean functions described the response at selected low and high concentration extremes.

The paper constructed regulatory functions through molecular arrangement. AND, OR and NAND could emerge from suitable productive states and interactions. XOR highlighted a stronger point: the effect of one regulator could depend on the other regulator's state. The authors discussed combining regulatory regions to obtain such logic without adding an intermediate expressed gene for every operation.

The work also stated physical obstacles to extensive use. Long-distance regulation, promoter geometry and unintended crosstalk constrain which constructions can coexist. A theoretical arrangement of one promoter therefore establishes a possible local response under stated assumptions. It does not automatically establish a scalable genome-wide implementation.

The lasting methodological change is visible in the core. A signed weight is derived from two positive expression laws and a sequestration comparison. It is not inserted as an unexplained biological parameter. This makes the architecture inspectable at the level where affinities, state activities and conserved totals constrain its responses.

Read promoter logic as a consequence of molecular state weights and activities, then check the physical assumptions required to compose those responses.

Part II

Test the molecular component and its connections

A plausible construction becomes an engineering method when its response and failures can be measured.

4Sequestration experiments turned a sharp response into a tunable mechanism

Buchler and Cross's 2009 experiment asked whether protein sequestration itself could create ultrasensitive gene regulation. They built a synthetic budding-yeast circuit in which an inhibitor bound an active transcription factor into an inactive complex. This isolated a stoichiometric mechanism for sharpness that does not require many cooperative promoter sites.3

The experiment varied the inhibitor abundance and measured the response. Sequestration converted a graded transcriptional response into a much sharper one. The threshold and apparent steepness changed with inhibitor level, as the titration model predicted. The key evidence was therefore an intervention on the proposed threshold-setting component, rather than a steep curve by itself.

The molecular accounting explains the intervention. For one pair, qR=R+CRSq_R=R+C_{RS} and qS=S+CRSq_S=S+C_{RS}, so R−S=qR−qSR-S=q_R-q_S. Tight binding leaves an appreciable free pool chiefly on the side with excess total. Changing the partner total moves the onset of that free pool.

This mechanism supports a physical comparison primitive. It does not supply an exact Boolean boundary at finite affinity. Both free pools remain positive, and a reporter responds continuously. The exposition's finite-affinity bounds make that distinction quantitative. A reported large apparent Hill coefficient also need not count cooperative binding sites when upstream sequestration changes the relationship between the controlled total and the free ligand seen by the promoter.

The course's weighted construction builds on that logic in a specific way. Regulated expression creates two input-dependent totals. Sequestration compares them. A reporter reads the remaining free repressor. The exact linear equality of the producer totals and the finite reporter's half-output contour are different objects. Their distinction follows from the mechanism, rather than from an objection to using a logical description.

Look for interventions that move the proposed molecular threshold. Use the resulting mechanism to explain sharpness without equating response steepness with binding-site number.

5Circuit automation made composition and failure part of the evidence

Nielsen and colleagues' 2016 Cello work asked whether genetic circuit design could become a reproducible translation from a specified function to DNA. A user supplied a logical description, sensors, outputs and operating constraints. Design algorithms selected gates and their connections from a characterized library.4

The molecular interface was central to that programme. Transcriptional gates used RNA-polymerase flux on DNA as a common signal. Measured response functions and operating ranges guided connections. Genetic insulation reduced sensitivity to sequence context. Growth effects were included in gate selection. These were physical conditions for applying the abstract circuit diagram.

The study tested complete circuits. Of 60 designed circuits built without additional tuning, 45 behaved correctly in every tested output state. Across all circuits, 92% of the 412 tested states behaved as predicted. The largest designs involved up to ten regulators and 55 genetic parts. The counts describe that library and experimental programme, not a universal biological capacity ceiling.

The failures supplied mechanistic information. Two circuits had growth defects. Incorrect intermediate responses propagated through some designs. A four-layer circuit briefly produced an incorrect output because one signal traversed more layers before reaching the final gate. A correct steady truth table therefore left a separate timing problem.

This evidence prevents an overly simple reading of the core's scaling puzzles. Borrowed engineering architectures can work in cells. The question is what resources and interfaces they require for a specified task. Binding-based computation offers another way to organize molecular interactions, but its own outputs also impose loads and have finite settling times.

Judge a composable architecture by measured input ranges, complete-circuit outputs, timing and growth effects, as well as by isolated component behavior.

Part III

Ask whether a living cell retains a selected state

A parallel experimental tradition examines what remains after a stimulus is removed and which mechanism maintains it.

6Stimulus removal and feedback disruption test a memory mechanism

Xiong and Ferrell's 2003 study asked how a transient hormonal signal could commit an oocyte to a persistent response. The problem was biological irreversibility under molecular turnover. A long-lived response might reflect residual stimulus, slow removal of an intermediate or active feedback. Those possibilities require different interventions.5

The authors compared induction with maintenance. They measured the response to progesterone, then removed the hormone and tested whether kinase activity and maturation persisted. They also checked hormone binding, addressing the possibility that incomplete washout supplied an unnoticed continuing input.

Feedback interventions made the interpretation more specific. The work examined the Mos–MAPK-related feedback system and used protein-synthesis inhibition or targeted interference with the feedback route. Disrupting feedback made the kinase response reversible after the activating input was removed. The retained response therefore depended on an actively maintained circuit state.

The evidence distinguishes persistence from a molecular freeze. The active phosphorylation state could turn over while feedback replenished it. Memory was carried by the dynamics of the system, not by one unchanged molecule. That is why a steady concentration can describe ongoing biochemical activity.

The experimental distinction

First remove the writing signal. Then perturb the mechanism proposed to maintain the state. Retention after washout supports memory. Loss of retention after feedback disruption supports the particular feedback explanation. Neither observation alone proves that every developmental decision uses the same circuit.

The selected full text includes a later correction to a model equation. The experimental argument used here rests on washout, activity measurements and feedback interventions. It does not require the uncorrected historical equation. The distinction between a model illustration and the measured evidence matters when reading an influential mechanistic paper.

Separate residual stimulus, slow relaxation and active maintenance by combining signal removal with interventions on the proposed memory mechanism.

7MultiFate joined a reusable molecular architecture to controlled state selection

Zhu and colleagues' 2022 MultiFate study asked how to construct an expandable multistable system in mammalian cells. The architecture combined two molecular functions: active homodimers promoted their own expression, while inactive heterodimers coupled different factors through competition. Adding another factor could therefore add cross-inhibition through the shared protein-binding mechanism.6

The components were tested before the complete memory circuit. The authors engineered dimer-dependent transcription factors, checked homodimer activation and examined inhibition by matching versus nonmatching dimerization partners. These tests connected the abstract network signs to physical molecular interactions.

The two-factor circuit supported three maintained expression states. A high, B high and both high were not merely three labels drawn on a diagram. Sorted populations and time-lapse observations tested whether the corresponding expression patterns persisted. Dimerization and protein-stability conditions were part of that operating regime.

Controlled switching then tested the role of the available attractors. In the tristable condition, transient B induction changed an A-high population into the both-high state. Combining B induction with temporarily reduced protein stability moved a separately prepared both-high population into the B-high state. Restoring the original conditions did not automatically restore the initial expression state.

The intervention changes more than the current expression rate. Reduced protein stability can remove the both-high attractor. When the original stability condition returns, that attractor can reappear while the cells remain in another basin. This gives a concrete mechanism for history dependence under the same final environment.

The three-factor circuit demonstrated seven maintained expression states. The study followed them for eighteen days under the stated dimerization and stability conditions. Reducing protein stability altered which combinations remained stable. The experiment thus tested both an expanded repertoire and control over that repertoire.7

The larger-model scaling argument has a narrower evidential status. A model can enumerate stable fixed points as factor number changes. Such enumeration depends on its parameter choices and stability criterion. It is distinct from the seven experimentally maintained states, and both are distinct from the 2N2^N possible binary expression labels. The course's numerical enumeration is labeled separately from the paper's larger-system curve.

MultiFate establishes an engineered expression-state memory. It does not show that seven distinct downstream differentiated functions have been constructed, or that all native fate systems use the same dimerization architecture. Its contribution to the core is more specific: a compact shared-binding mechanism can generate, select and retain several molecular choices.

Trace a cell-state claim through component validation, state retention and controlled switching, and distinguish experimental states from model enumerations.

Part IV

State what the mathematical and experimental results leave open

Response richness, physical efficiency and dynamic controllability remain different claims.

8Binding approximation extends the response question under explicit resource assumptions

The local binding approximation programme asks which static maps an equilibrium molecular system can represent. It assigns nonnegative activities to binding states and uses conserved ligand totals as inputs. The output is the occupancy-weighted average of those activities. The question concerns a family of response maps, rather than one Boolean table or one memory circuit.

The inspected October 2026 tutorial gives two constructive routes. An ordered binding ladder concentrates occupancy near neighboring states. A separate independent-site construction uses occupancy statistics and calibrates them against conserved totals. The constructions establish approximation of continuous nonnegative targets on compact positive input domains when the architecture and parameters may depend on the target and accuracy.8

The resource assumptions carry part of the theorem. More states can resolve finer target structure. A smaller scaffold pool changes ligand depletion and physical output amplitude. The ladder can demand a wide span of affinities. An activity table is itself a set of molecular readout specifications. A universal family of permitted designs is therefore different from a fixed small circuit that performs every task cheaply.

The printed tutorial attributes its synthesis to the binding approximation manuscripts and XU Ye's work. No individual authorship is inferred beyond the inspected source. Its status here is an unpublished mathematical contribution. It supplies a useful existence result and resource analysis, not a settled experimental account of cellular computing capacity.

The connection to the older promoter-state work is conceptual and mathematical. Both read activity from a distribution of molecular states. The new construction adds controlled-total calibration and an explicit approximation argument over a function class. Neither fact establishes arbitrary autonomous state transitions. Production, removal, stability and allowed controls still have to be analyzed.

Read a universality statement with its domain, output, norm and variable resources, and distinguish its static theorem from an experimental or dynamical claim.

9The record supports a hierarchy of evidence, not a single capacity number

The selected history explains why molecular computation needs several levels of evidence. A truth-table construction shows representability under a model. A prototype tests the molecular response. A library tests composition. A washout experiment tests retention. A control protocol tests which state can be selected. A scaling study asks how those achievements change as resources grow.

The most promising comparison now uses a common task. Hold the required input distinctions, output accuracy, reading time, downstream load and retention interval fixed. Then compare the components, molecule numbers, affinity ranges and turnover demands of competing implementations. This makes an architectural advantage open to both confirmation and refutation.

The course's biomachine perspective supplies the organizing question. Binding and catalysis already describe the molecular components. Choosing computation as the function asks how those components select, preserve and execute a response. The historical record makes that question sharper by showing which experiments and arguments are needed at each step.

Match the strength of a computing claim to the evidence that establishes it, then design the next comparison around a shared task and resource budget.
