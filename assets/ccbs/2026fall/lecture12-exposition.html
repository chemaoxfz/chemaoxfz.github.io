From binding mechanisms to readable and retained decisions

A worked method for specifying molecular computations, deriving their responses, checking composition and selecting states that persist after a signal ends.

CCBS · Lecture 12 · exposition · 19 October 2026

A molecular computing claim is established by a chain of calculations. Specify the input and output. Write the constituent balances. Reconstruct the relevant free species. Calculate the response and its error. If the task requires memory, also find attracting states and a signal protocol that selects them. This chapter teaches each step and then transfers the method to a new input encoding.

The core asks how a compact cell can make complex decisions. This exposition supplies the derivations behind that argument. The lineage follows the historical questions and experiments. You can read this chapter without having attended the lecture.

Concentration and reaction conventions

A bare species symbol, such as RR, denotes its free concentration. A total qRq_R counts every molecular state containing that constituent. A complex CRSC_{RS} names its constituents. Dissociation constants have concentration units. An elementary binding step has a straight arrow with microscopic rate constants. A composite process, including expression or unresolved removal, has a bare squiggly arrow and a separately stated rate law.

Part I

Specify the task and build its molecular comparison

Start with a response that can be checked. Then derive the molecular balances that realize it.

1Specify the input, readout and required history

A computational task is a statement about observable behavior. A Boolean gate assigns output labels to specified input ranges. An analog computation returns a concentration related to its inputs. A memory task retains a choice after the writing signal ends. These definitions determine what the model has to establish.

For a static classifier, give five specifications. Name the controlled input quantity, the allowed input values, the measured output, the permitted output error and the reading time. For example, let the inputs be held free repressor concentrations I1,I2I_1,I_2. Let the output be the active fraction pp of a reporter promoter. Declare output 1 successful when p≥0.9p\ge0.9 and output 0 successful when p≤0.1p\le0.1, after the specified equilibration and expression interval.

Free input and total input are different interventions. Holding free ligand fixed requires a sufficiently large reservoir or active control. Holding total ligand fixed requires including every bound ligand in conservation. The same binding site can give different input-response curves under those two choices.

For memory, specify an initial state and a signal protocol. Let qq collect changing protein totals, let I(t)I(t) denote the external controls, and let yy be the measured output:

q˙=f(q,I(t)),y=h(q).\dot q=f(q,I(t)),\qquad y=h(q).

The writing protocol changes I(t)I(t) for a finite time. Retention is then measured after I(t)I(t) returns to its original value. A high output maintained only by a permanently high input is a response, not this specified memory task.

Checkpoint: what must be held fixed to test a reporter load?

Compare the reporter response while holding producer totals, affinities, input conditions and the reading time fixed. Vary the reporter-site total. If adding the reporter also changes growth or expression resources, measure those effects and enlarge the model before attributing the change to sequestration alone.

Write a computation specification that states which concentration is controlled, which quantity is read and whether the signal must remain present.

2A molecular NAND needs the right stationary output and time to reach it

A gate calculation must show that the output reaches its required state. Let two molecular forms X0,X1X_0,X_1 encode the first bit and Y0,Y1Y_0,Y_1 encode the second. Hold the selected input forms fixed. Two output forms share a conserved total qZ=qZ0+qZ1q_Z=q_{Z_0}+q_{Z_1}. The following composite conversions implement the intended NAND directions:

X0+Y0+Z0⇝X0+Y0+Z1,X0+Y1+Z0⇝X0+Y1+Z1,X1+Y0+Z0⇝X1+Y0+Z1,X1+Y1+Z1⇝X1+Y1+Z0.\begin{aligned}X_0+Y_0+Z_0&\rightsquigarrow X_0+Y_0+Z_1,\\X_0+Y_1+Z_0&\rightsquigarrow X_0+Y_1+Z_1,\\X_1+Y_0+Z_0&\rightsquigarrow X_1+Y_0+Z_1,\\X_1+Y_1+Z_1&\rightsquigarrow X_1+Y_1+Z_0.\end{aligned}

The arrows state conversion accounting. They do not assert elementary three-body chemistry. Suppose the unresolved catalytic mechanisms supply effective conversion frequencies a≥0a\ge0 from output 0 to output 1 and b≥0b\ge0 in reverse. After incorporating the held inputs, both frequencies have inverse-time units.

The output-1 fraction z=qZ1/qZz=q_{Z_1}/q_Z obeys a linear balance. Solving it gives both the stationary answer and the transient:

z˙=a(1−z)−bz,z∗=aa+b,z(t)=z∗+[z(0)−z∗]e−(a+b)t.\dot z=a(1-z)-bz,\qquad z^*=\frac{a}{a+b},\qquad z(t)=z^*+[z(0)-z^*]e^{-(a+b)t}.

This formula assumes a+b>0a+b>0. If both frequencies vanish, the output remains at its initial value and does not evaluate the gate. In the ideal construction, inputs 00, 01 and 10 have a>0,b=0a>0,b=0, while 11 has a=0,b>0a=0,b>0.

Leakage changes the stationary truth table. A high output has error 1−z∗=b/(a+b)1-z^*=b/(a+b). A low output has error z∗=a/(a+b)z^*=a/(a+b). The remaining distance from that stationary output decays on the relaxation time tgate=1/(a+b)t_{\mathrm{gate}}=1/(a+b). Accuracy must therefore budget leakage and finite waiting separately.

∣z(t)−ytarget∣≤∣z∗−ytarget∣+e−(a+b)t.|z(t)-y_{\mathrm{target}}|\le |z^*-y_{\mathrm{target}}|+e^{-(a+b)t}.
Checkpoint: a leaky high-output gate with a five-percent target error

Let a=1 s−1a=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1} and b=0.01 s−1b=0.01\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}. The leakage error is 0.01/1.01≈0.0099010.01/1.01\approx0.009901. A sufficient waiting time for total error at most 0.05 is

t≥log⁡[1/(0.05−0.009901)]1.01≈3.18 s.t\ge\frac{\log[1/(0.05-0.009901)]}{1.01}\approx3.18\,\mathrm{s}.

This bound allows any initial fraction. These rates are illustrative assumptions.

Connecting gates introduces additional conditions. The output of one gate must lie in the input range expected by the next, and the last gate must wait for relevant upstream changes. Cello's measured circuit failures and propagation-delay example show why a correct isolated truth table does not certify a connected circuit.1

Solve the gate's balance, leakage error and relaxation time before deciding when its output can be interpreted as a logical value.

3Derive a weighted comparison from promoter occupancy and expression

The three-gene construction combines several inputs before thresholding them. Two producer genes make proteins RR and SS. Their inactive complex is CRSC_{RS}. A third gene produces output protein OO and is repressed by free SS.

First reconstruct the free producer promoters. Input IjI_j binds promoter GRG_R with dissociation constant KRjK_{Rj}. Assume exclusive occupancy, so no promoter binds two inputs simultaneously. At equilibrium,

CGRIj=GRIjKRj,qGR=GR+∑jCGRIj,GR=qGRDR,DR=1+∑jIjKRj.C_{G_RI_j}=\frac{G_R I_j}{K_{Rj}},\qquad q_{G_R}=G_R+\sum_jC_{G_RI_j},\qquad G_R=\frac{q_{G_R}}{D_R},\quad D_R=1+\sum_j\frac{I_j}{K_{Rj}}.

Part II

Check the finite molecular implementation

A correct sign comparison becomes a useful output only when affinity, downstream binding and relaxation preserve its accuracy.

6Check which fast approximation and removal law justify the response

A fast binding layer and a steady expression layer answer different questions. Binding equilibrium reconstructs free species at current totals. Expression steady state determines the totals after an input has been held long enough. A transient calculation must not replace changing totals by their final expression values prematurely.

A resolved pair shows how removal can alter an apparent dissociation constant. Take elementary binding R+S⇌k−k+CRSR+S\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{RS}. Suppose free proteins and the entire complex are removed at the common frequency δ\delta. Complex removal eliminates one constituent of each type. Then

C˙RS=k+RS−k−CRS−δCRS,q˙R=vR−δqR,q˙S=vS−δqS.\dot C_{RS}=k_+RS-k_-C_{RS}-\delta C_{RS},\qquad \dot q_R=v_R-\delta q_R,\qquad \dot q_S=v_S-\delta q_S.

The constituent balances retain the simple total-proportional form. The full stationary complex relation, however, uses Keff=(k−+δ)/k+K_{\mathrm{eff}}=(k_-+\delta)/k_+. Pure binding equilibrium uses Kd=k−/k+K_d=k_-/k_+ and is a good replacement for that stationary relation when δ≪k−\delta\ll k_-. Faster binding relaxation alone does not justify discarding a competing removal route.

The local binding relaxation time has a separate expression. With temporarily fixed totals and no removal, differentiate the complex rate with respect to CRSC_{RS}. At equilibrium,

tbind=1k+(R+S)+k−.t_{\mathrm{bind}}=\frac{1}{k_+(R+S)+k_-}.

Compare this time with the input variation time and the protein-total relaxation times. Near equal totals under tight binding, the free pools become small. The binding relaxation can then slow relative to what a high-total concentration estimate suggests.

The expression system adds its own clocks. At fixed free promoter, transcript error decays as e−δTte^{-\delta_Tt}. Protein error is driven by that transient and decays through δR\delta_R. Its slow mode is set by the smaller of the two removal frequencies. When they coincide, the transient can contain a factor te−δRtt e^{-\delta_Rt}. Waiting several binding times does not guarantee a settled protein output.

Checkpoint: quantify a steady binding correction

Part III

Build and select a persistent expression state

The next calculations turn a binding response into feedback, then distinguish a fixed point from a usable memory.

7Shared dimerization couples self-activation and inhibition

MultiFate uses the same protein pool for self-activation and mutual inhibition. Homodimers activate their own expression. Heterodimers are inactive. The mechanism lets an additional factor compete with the existing factors through protein binding.3

Specify the microscopic convention before writing the equilibrium constants. Use equal homodimer association constants k+k_+ and dissociation constants k−k_-. Let heterodimer association be 2k+2k_+ with dissociation k−k_-:

A+A⇌k−k+CAA,B+B⇌k−k+CBB,A+B⇌k−2k+CAB.A+A\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{AA},\qquad B+B\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{BB},\qquad A+B\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{2k_+}C_{AB}.

With Kd=k−/k+K_d=k_-/k_+, this convention gives CAA=A2/KdC_{AA}=A^2/K_d, CBB=B2/KdC_{BB}=B^2/K_d and CAB=2AB/KdC_{AB}=2AB/K_d. Every homodimer contains two copies of its constituent:

qA=A+2CAA+CAB,qB=B+2CBB+CAB.q_A=A+2C_{AA}+C_{AB},\qquad q_B=B+2C_{BB}+C_{AB}.

To solve these balances, sum the free monomers. Let U=A+BU=A+B and Q=qA+qBQ=q_A+q_B. Both total equations contain the same factor:

qA=A(1+2U/Kd),qB=B(1+2U/Kd),Q=U+2U2/Kd.q_A=A(1+2U/K_d),\quad q_B=B(1+2U/K_d),\quad Q=U+2U^2/K_d.

The positive quadratic root yields a common free fraction:

ϕ=11+2U/Kd=21+1+8Q/Kd,A=ϕqA,B=ϕqB.\phi=\frac{1}{1+2U/K_d}=\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+8Q/K_d}},\qquad A=\phi q_A,\quad B=\phi q_B.

Adding B at fixed A total lowers the common free fraction and reduces CAA=ϕ2qA2/KdC_{AA}=\phi^2q_A^2/K_d. Raising A's own total has a direct positive effect on its homodimer as well. This explicit allocation is the origin of the inhibitory cross-coupling and self-activation in the reduced dynamics.

Close the dynamics with a stated promoter response. Let α\alpha be basal production, β\beta the additional production scale, KMK_M the active-homodimer half-response concentration, and δ\delta a common constituent removal frequency. The model is

q˙i=α+βCiinKMn+Ciin−δqi,i∈{A,B}.\dot q_i=\alpha+\beta\frac{C_{ii}^{n}}{K_M^n+C_{ii}^{n}}-\delta q_i,\qquad i\in\{A,B\}.

This is a fast-binding reduction. Promoter-bound protein is neglected in the constituent totals. The exponent nn describes an effective expression response. It is not the stoichiometry of the dimerization reaction.

Checkpoint: where would the two-factor model fail if the heterodimer were active?

The constituent conservation equations would still count heterodimer binding. The expression law would change because CABC_{AB} would now contribute to one or both promoters. A shared binding pool alone does not determine the sign of regulation. The activity assigned to each molecular state is essential.

Checkpoint: test the two perturbation modes at the both-high state

Exchange symmetry gives a Jacobian of the form (abba)\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix}. A common increase of both totals has eigenvalue a+ba+b. Increasing one while decreasing the other has eigenvalue a−ba-b. Both must be negative for local stability. A stable common mode alone cannot exclude a symmetry-breaking instability.

Find candidate fixed points in a bounded positive region, differentiate the binding reconstruction and test every independent perturbation mode.

9A transient protocol must reach the basin of the state to be retained

A stable state is useful for memory only if a permitted signal can select it. Start from the A-high fixed point in Section 8. Let sB∈[0,1]s_B\in[0,1] supplement the unoccupied fraction of B's expression response, and let γ=δ/δref\gamma=\delta/\delta_{\mathrm{ref}} change the common removal frequency. Keep the baseline clock τ\tau fixed:

qˉA′=0.8+20hA−γqˉA,qˉB′=0.8+20[hB+sB(1−hB)]−γqˉB.\begin{aligned}\bar q_A'&=0.8+20h_A-\gamma\bar q_A,\\\bar q_B'&=0.8+20[h_B+s_B(1-h_B)]-\gamma\bar q_B.\end{aligned}

Primes denote derivatives with respect to τ\tau. Full induction sB=1s_B=1 reaches the existing maximal B-production rate 20.8. This is a bounded illustrative control, not a calibrated drug-response law.

Compare three protocols from the same initial state. No control leaves the system A high. A five-lifetime pulse with sB=1,γ=1s_B=1,\gamma=1 selects the both-high state. The same induction with γ=2\gamma=2 selects the B-high state. After the pulse, restore sB=0,γ=1s_B=0,\gamma=1 in every case. The final environments are identical.

The experiments identify corresponding physical interventions. In MultiFate, doxycycline can induce B, trimethoprim controls protein stability, and AP1903 enables dimerization. One sorted A-high population reached both-high after B induction. A separately sorted both-high population reached B-high after B induction combined with reduced protein stability. The writing controls were removed or restored, and the resulting expression choices persisted.34

The measured and computed experiments have different scopes. The calculation fixes one initial state and imposes dimensionless rate controls. The experiments use drug concentrations, cell populations and time in days. Mapping between them requires dose-response and removal-rate calibration. Both distinguish the writing interval from the subsequent retention interval.

Checkpoint: distinguish four kinds of state count

For three binary labels there are eight possible patterns. The cited experiment maintained seven expression states under its operating conditions. A deterministic model may have a different number of stable equilibria under another parameter choice. A restricted signal family may reach only some of those equilibria. These four counts answer different questions.

Specify the initial state, allowed pulse and restored environment, then test both the selected basin and retention after the signal ends.

Part IV

Transfer the calculation to new tasks

A method is usable when it survives a change of response shape, input encoding or computational mechanism.

10State activities make an equilibrium response rise and fall

A scaffold with several binding states can represent a response more complex than one threshold. Let CjC_j contain one scaffold and jj copies of ligand SS, for j=0,…,mj=0,\ldots,m. Successive elementary binding steps have dissociation constants σj\sigma_j:

Cj−1+S⇌k−,jk+,jCj,σj=k−,j/k+,j.C_{j-1}+S\xrightleftharpoons[k_{-,j}]{k_{+,j}}C_j,\qquad \sigma_j=k_{-,j}/k_{+,j}.

Define the occupancy fractions pj=Cj/qEp_j=C_j/q_E, where qEq_E is the fixed scaffold total. Equilibrium gives relative weights w0=1w_0=1 and wj=Sj/(σ1⋯σj)w_j=S^j/(\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_j). Thus

pj=wj∑k=0mwk,qS=S+qE∑j=0mjpj.p_j=\frac{w_j}{\sum_{k=0}^{m}w_k},\qquad q_S=S+q_E\sum_{j=0}^{m}j p_j.

The second equation calibrates free ligand against the controlled total. Its right side increases with SS. For example, the derivative of mean occupancy with respect to log⁡S\log S is the occupancy variance, which is nonnegative. The ligand balance therefore has a unique physical solution for each positive total in this scaffold model.

Assign a nonnegative activity ωj\omega_j to each state. The normalized readout is f=∑jωjpjf=\sum_j\omega_jp_j. A physical production flux with per-scaffold reference rate k0k_0 is v=k0qEfv=k_0q_Ef. The readout is assumed not to disturb the binding allocation.

Ordered tight binding produces interpolation through conservation. Suppose adjacent affinities and their overall scale are chosen so that, near qS/qE=j+uq_S/q_E=j+u, only states jj and j+1j+1 retain appreciable occupancy and free ligand is negligible. The two conservation equations then give pj≈1−up_j\approx1-u and pj+1≈up_{j+1}\approx u. The output is approximately the straight segment joining ωj\omega_j and ωj+1\omega_{j+1}.

The approximation theorem needs two controlled errors. First choose closely spaced activity samples of a continuous nonnegative target. Then make the occupancy distribution sufficiently concentrated. The inspected tutorial supplies finite-parameter bounds for an ordered ladder and an alternative independent-site construction. Its theorem allows the number of states, pool sizes, affinities and activity table to depend on the target and requested tolerance.5

Let the target gg be bounded by MM and continuously extended outside the target interval. Assign ωj=g(jqE)\omega_j=g(jq_E). For an occupancy random variable JJ with probabilities pjp_j, define U=qEJU=q_EJ. Conservation gives E⁡U=qS−S\operatorname{E}U=q_S-S. Hence

A uniform bound on free ligand and occupancy variance therefore gives a uniform approximation bound. The molecular construction must supply those bounds. Merely increasing the number of named states does not.

Static richness does not establish dynamical memory. The scaffold response above is uniquely determined by its totals under the specified equilibrium model. Multiple retained expression choices require the additional feedback dynamics of Sections 7–9.

11Design an XOR response when the inputs are totals

The transfer problem changes the input convention. Build a two-input XOR response on one scaffold with one site for ligand XX and one for ligand YY. The sites bind independently. The controlled quantities are ligand totals qX,qYq_X,q_Y, not free reservoirs.

The four scaffold states are empty, X-bound, Y-bound and doubly bound. Assign activity 1 to the two singly occupied states and activity 0 to the other two. Let pX=X/(KX+X)p_X=X/(K_X+X) and pY=Y/(KY+Y)p_Y=Y/(K_Y+Y) be the individual occupied fractions. Independence gives the normalized activity

12Prove a dynamical winner-take-all computation

A second transfer problem tests a dynamical answer rather than a static binding map. The historical analog-computation exercise starts with two species and asks the larger initial population to take the entire pool. The following elementary reactions provide a mechanism:

X+Y→k2Z,Z+X→k2X,Z+Y→k2Y.X+Y\xrightarrow{k}2Z,\qquad Z+X\xrightarrow{k}2X,\qquad Z+Y\xrightarrow{k}2Y.

The positive constant kk has inverse-concentration-per-time units. The model has no external input after initialization. Its concentration dynamics and conserved sum are

X˙=kX(Z−Y),Y˙=kY(Z−X),Z˙=k[2XY−Z(X+Y)],qsum=X+Y+Z.\begin{aligned}\dot X&=kX(Z-Y),\qquad \dot Y=kY(Z-X),\\\dot Z&=k[2XY-Z(X+Y)],\qquad q_{\mathrm{sum}}=X+Y+Z.\end{aligned}

The computation preserves the sign of the initial advantage. Set Δ=X−Y\Delta=X-Y. Subtracting the first two equations gives Δ˙=kZΔ\dot\Delta=kZ\Delta. Therefore a positive initial advantage stays positive and cannot shrink.

To prove convergence, identify where the motion can end. LaSalle’s invariance principle supplies a sufficient test. In a compact forward-invariant region, a trajectory approaches the largest invariant subset where a differentiable, nonincreasing function has zero time derivative. Invariant means that subsequent motion stays inside the set.

Here the initial advantage supplies the required function and region. With Δ(0)>0\Delta(0)>0, trajectories remain in the compact nonnegative simplex at fixed qsumq_{\mathrm{sum}}, with Δ≥Δ(0)\Delta\ge\Delta(0). The function −Δ-\Delta is nonincreasing, and its derivative vanishes only when Z=0Z=0 in this region. To remain at Z=0Z=0, the third equation also requires XY=0XY=0. The positive advantage then forces Y=0Y=0 and X=qsumX=q_{\mathrm{sum}}. The invariance principle therefore gives convergence to the X-winning state. The reversed initial advantage gives the Y-winning state.

An exact tie gives a different answer. On the invariant line X=Y>0X=Y>0, substitute Z=qsum−2XZ=q_{\mathrm{sum}}-2X. The equation becomes X˙=kX(qsum−3X)\dot X=kX(q_{\mathrm{sum}}-3X), which approaches X=Y=Z=qsum/3X=Y=Z=q_{\mathrm{sum}}/3. That fixed point is stable within the tie line but unstable to an unequal perturbation. The task must therefore exclude ties or state how a tie is to be interpreted.

Checkpoint: recover the fixed points and their stability after eliminating Z

The reduced equations are X˙=kX(qsum−X−2Y)\dot X=kX(q_{\mathrm{sum}}-X-2Y) and Y˙=kY(qsum−2X−Y)\dot Y=kY(q_{\mathrm{sum}}-2X-Y). The two winning corners have eigenvalues −kqsum-kq_{\mathrm{sum}} twice. The all-Z corner has eigenvalues kqsumkq_{\mathrm{sum}} twice. The equal-mixture fixed point has eigenvalues −kqsum-kq_{\mathrm{sum}} and kqsum/3kq_{\mathrm{sum}}/3. These local tests agree with the global sign and invariance argument.

The two transfer problems establish different functions with the same discipline. The scaffold XOR uses a unique equilibrium response and explicit input calibration. Winner-take-all uses conserved dynamics and a global convergence argument. MultiFate uses open production and removal to retain a signal-selected expression choice. In each case, the computational interpretation follows from the specified experiment and the mechanism.

A chosen expression state must ultimately affect a physical action. Lecture 13 asks whether the resulting metabolic fluxes can satisfy material and energy constraints. That next step tests what the decision enables the cell to do.
