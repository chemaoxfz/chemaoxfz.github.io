Timescale separation: QSSA and the telegraph model

A fast variable may be eliminated only after its scale, attracting branch, initial transient, and preserved observable are named. Enzyme catalysis and promoter switching show why all four matter.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 6 · core page (full) · 2026-09-17 (Thu) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

Lecture 5 made molecular events explicit. Real models immediately contain too many of them. This lecture asks when a fast process can be removed without erasing the behavior we care about. We first reduce an enzyme mechanism, where the observable is product formation. We then reduce promoter switching, where matching the mean can still leave the distribution wrong. The arrival is an audit: a reduced model is a claim about an observable on a timescale, not permission to delete an equation.

The deliverable

By minute 95, every team should be able to identify a dimensionless fast-to-slow ratio, draw the initial boundary layer, distinguish rapid equilibrium from standard and total QSSA, select the physical quadratic root, compare full and reduced trajectories, and explain why a stochastic reduction must be tested on more than its mean.

Part 1

Find the slow story

A fast label is dimensional. A fast-to-slow ratio and an attracting branch make it useful.

1Fast is a ratio

A rate constant alone cannot be fast. Fast means small relative time after units and concentration scales are included.

A bimolecular association constant konk_{\mathrm{on}} has units of concentration−1^{-1} time−1^{-1}. Its pseudo-first-order rate is konS∗k_{\mathrm{on}}S_\ast at a substrate scale S∗S_\ast. If product formation acts at rate kcatk_{\mathrm{cat}}, a first comparison is

ε=τfastτslow∼kcatkonS∗+koff.\varepsilon=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{fast}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{slow}}}\sim\frac{k_{\mathrm{cat}}}{k_{\mathrm{on}}S_\ast+k_{\mathrm{off}}}. (1)

The 2025 note mixes molar and micromolar units in one estimate. With kon=1 μM−1s−1k_{\mathrm{on}}=1\,\mu\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} and S∗=1 mM=103 μMS_\ast=1\,\mathrm{mM}=10^3\,\mu\mathrm{M}, the association rate is 103 s−110^3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}, not 106 s−110^6\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}.1 The conclusion may still be “fast,” but only relative to the slow clock being modeled.

Room check, 60 seconds. If association takes 1 ms1\,\mathrm{ms} and catalysis takes 0.5 s0.5\,\mathrm{s}, estimate ε\varepsilon. Would association still be instantaneous for a 20 μs20\,\mu\mathrm{s} input pulse?

2The boundary layer comes first

A fast state does not begin on its slow manifold. It approaches one while the slow state barely moves.

Write a generic system after nondimensionalization as

dxdτ=f(x,y),εdydτ=g(x,y),0<ε≪1.\frac{dx}{d\tau}=f(x,y),\qquad \varepsilon\frac{dy}{d\tau}=g(x,y),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\ll1. (2)

On the fast time θ=τ/ε\theta=\tau/\varepsilon, xx is approximately frozen while yy moves toward a root of g(x,y)=0g(x,y)=0. Only after this boundary layer does the reduced trajectory drift along that algebraic set. Tikhonov's convergence result requires an isolated attracting root over the region used.2 If the branch loses stability or folds, setting g=0g=0 can choose the wrong motion.

There are therefore two approximations, not one. During the initial layer, freeze xx and solve the fast subsystem. During the later outer solution, constrain yy to the attracting root and evolve xx. A uniformly useful description either matches these pieces or begins after the layer has decayed. Saying only “ε=0\varepsilon=0” suppresses the very transient that justifies arriving on the manifold.

3Keep the full enzyme mechanism visible

A reduced formula is interpretable only while the mechanism and its conserved pools remain beside it.

Use the mechanism developed in the 2025 Lecture 3 note:

E+S⇌ koff  kon C→kcatE+P.E+S\xrightleftharpoons[\,k_{\mathrm{off}}\,]{\,k_{\mathrm{on}}\,}C\xrightarrow{k_{\mathrm{cat}}}E+P. (3)

Mass action and enzyme conservation give

S˙=−kon(ET−C)S+koffC,C˙=kon(ET−C)S−(koff+kcat)C,P˙=kcatC.\dot S=-k_{\mathrm{on}}(E_T-C)S+k_{\mathrm{off}}C,\quad \dot C=k_{\mathrm{on}}(E_T-C)S-(k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})C,\quad \dot P=k_{\mathrm{cat}}C. (4)

Here ET=E+CE_T=E+C and S+C+P=S0S+C+P=S_0. The full model guarantees 0≤C≤min⁡(ET,ST)0\le C\le\min(E_T,S_T), where ST=S+CS_T=S+C is total unconverted substrate. Any reduced expression that violates this bound is physically impossible before its numerical error is considered.

4Three reductions make three claims

Rapid equilibrium, standard QSSA, and total QSSA can look similar while relying on different physics.

Briggs and Haldane's steady-complex argument produces KMK_M, not the equilibrium dissociation constant KdK_d.3 Segel and Slemrod identify the boundary layer and the small parameter behind standard QSSA.4 Borghans, De Boer, and Segel show why changing the slow variable to total substrate can extend the approximation.5

Part 2

Reduce, then test

The enzyme mechanism now supplies two reductions whose errors can be computed side by side.

5Standard QSSA needs substrate excess

Balance the complex while treating free substrate as the slow input.

Set equation (4)'s complex derivative approximately to zero:

0=kon(ET−C)S−(koff+kcat)C.0=k_{\mathrm{on}}(E_T-C)S-(k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})C. (5)

Define KM=(koff+kcat)/konK_M=(k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})/k_{\mathrm{on}} and solve the linear equation in CC:

CsQSSA(S)=ETSKM+S,P˙=kcatETSKM+S.C_{\mathrm{sQSSA}}(S)=\frac{E_TS}{K_M+S},\qquad \dot P=\frac{k_{\mathrm{cat}}E_TS}{K_M+S}. (6)

The board derivation is short because the hypothesis does the hard work. Free substrate must change little while the complex relaxes. The standard sufficient ratio εs=ET/(S0+KM)≪1\varepsilon_s=E_T/(S_0+K_M)\ll1 captures substrate excess or weak sequestration. It is not equivalent to kcat≪koffk_{\mathrm{cat}}\ll k_{\mathrm{off}}. Standard QSSA can hold without rapid equilibrium, and rapid equilibrium uses KdK_d rather than KMK_M.

A board-level validity check now takes three lines. Estimate the complex scale as C∗≤ETC_\ast\le E_T. Estimate how much free substrate could be hidden inside that complex by C∗/(S0+KM)C_\ast/(S_0+K_M). If this fraction is small, replacing total substrate by free substrate is self-consistent after the fast layer. If it is order one, equation (6) may still be algebraically neat, but its input is no longer the slowly evolving pool.

6Total QSSA chooses the physical root

When complex formation sequesters appreciable substrate, make total unconverted substrate the slow coordinate.

Let ST=S+CS_T=S+C. Substitute S=ST−CS=S_T-C and E=ET−CE=E_T-C into the quasi-steady complex balance:

KMC=(ET−C)(ST−C).K_MC=(E_T-C)(S_T-C). (7)

This quadratic has two algebraic roots. Only the smaller root obeys 0≤C≤min⁡(ET,ST)0\le C\le\min(E_T,S_T):

C∗(ST)=ET+ST+KM−(ET+ST+KM)2−4ETST2.C^\ast(S_T)=\frac{E_T+S_T+K_M-\sqrt{(E_T+S_T+K_M)^2-4E_TS_T}}{2}. (8)

The plus root is not an alternative regime. It exceeds a conserved pool and is unphysical. The reduced slow equation is S˙T=−kcatC∗(ST)\dot S_T=-k_{\mathrm{cat}}C^\ast(S_T), with P˙=kcatC∗(ST)\dot P=k_{\mathrm{cat}}C^\ast(S_T). Changing variables is the central move. It keeps sequestration inside the algebraic manifold instead of pretending that free and total substrate coincide.

7A comparison decides which reduction survives

A validity slogan is useful only after the full and reduced models are compared on the claimed output.

The analysis ledger integrates equation (4), standard QSSA, and total QSSA with one solver and one tolerance.9 In the left regime, ET/(S0+KM)=0.0392E_T/(S_0+K_M)=0.0392, so both reductions closely track product. In the right regime the ratio is 2.3812.381, free substrate differs strongly from total substrate, and standard QSSA advances product too quickly. At t=0.5t=0.5, its product error is 0.613S00.613S_0, while total QSSA's is 0.00343S00.00343S_0.

What the comparison does not prove

One successful parameter set does not establish a theorem, and one time-course match does not guarantee preserved equilibria, bifurcations, first-passage times, or rare-event probabilities. The observable and parameter region belong in the claim.

Part 3

Preserve more than a mean

Promoter switching repeats the fast-variable problem, but now the discarded fluctuations may be the phenotype.

8Telegraph moments expose the hidden clock

Two models can share a mean while their cell-to-cell distributions remember very different promoter dynamics.

Let one promoter switch between off and on, with G∈{0,1}G\in\{0,1\}:

G0⇌ β  α G1,G1→kG1+M,M→γ∅.G_0\xrightleftharpoons[\,\beta\,]{\,\alpha\,}G_1,\qquad G_1\xrightarrow{k}G_1+M,\qquad M\xrightarrow{\gamma}\varnothing. (9)

At stationarity, the on fraction is ⟨G⟩=α/(α+β)\langle G\rangle=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta). The first-moment equation d⟨M⟩/dt=k⟨G⟩−γ⟨M⟩d\langle M\rangle/dt=k\langle G\rangle-\gamma\langle M\rangle gives the mean. Carrying the mixed moment ⟨GM⟩\langle GM\rangle closes the second-moment calculation and yields the homework's exact result:

⟨M⟩=kαγ(α+β),FM=1+kβ(α+β)(γ+α+β).\langle M\rangle=\frac{k\alpha}{\gamma(\alpha+\beta)},\qquad F_M=1+\frac{k\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)(\gamma+\alpha+\beta)}. (10)

The extra Fano contribution contains the comparison α+β\alpha+\beta versus γ\gamma. It vanishes as promoter switching becomes rapid relative to mRNA turnover. It can remain large when one promoter episode lasts long enough for mRNA to follow it. This is the stochastic version of a slow variable filtering a fast one, but equation (10) makes clear that the filtered quantity includes variance, not only mean.6

The closure works here because G2=GG^2=G for a binary promoter and every propensity is linear in GG and MM. Write equations for ⟨G⟩\langle G\rangle, ⟨M⟩\langle M\rangle, ⟨GM⟩\langle GM\rangle, and ⟨M2⟩\langle M^2\rangle, set their derivatives to zero in that order, then use Var⁡(M)=⟨M2⟩−⟨M⟩2\operatorname{Var}(M)=\langle M^2\rangle-\langle M\rangle^2. A nonlinear feedback propensity would usually open the hierarchy instead of closing it.

9Fast averages; slow remains

Hold promoter occupancy and mean mRNA fixed, change only the clock, and the distribution changes.

Take k=20k=20 and γ=1\gamma=1. Fast switching with α=12,β=8\alpha=12,\beta=8 and slow switching with α=0.12,β=0.08\alpha=0.12,\beta=0.08 both spend 60% of the time on. Both have exact mean 12. Yet equation (10) gives Fano factors 1.38 and 7.67. The fast promoter is averaged by mRNA lifetime. The slow promoter leaves episodes in the trace and a broad distribution in the ensemble.

A histogram also need not identify the mechanism. Telegraph-like and more detailed transcription models can generate very similar count distributions but different waiting-time distributions.7 Retaining time-resolved data is therefore part of model design, not merely a visualization preference.

10Audit the observable

Deterministic agreement does not automatically license the analogous stochastic propensity.

When fast promoter switching has no feedback, replacing kGkG by its occupancy average kα/(α+β)k\alpha/(\alpha+\beta) can preserve the slow mean and approach the fast-switching count law. With feedback, the fast promoter state can remain correlated with the slow molecule count. Then a Hill-shaped deterministic reduction need not be the correct stochastic propensity. Full-text comparisons show that the common shortcut can fail even under timescale separation.8

Run four questions before accepting any reduction:

  1. Ratio. Which dimensionless number is small over the experiment's parameter range?
  2. Manifold. Which algebraic branch is physical and attracting?
  3. Boundary layer. What initial transient is omitted, and does the experiment observe it?
  4. Observable. Must the model preserve a mean, trajectory, distribution, waiting time, stability boundary, or rare event?
Exit ticket. For your team's model, name one candidate fast state and answer all four questions in one sentence each. If question 4 names a distribution, design a full-versus-reduced simulation rather than checking only ODE trajectories.
The core story

Fast is a ratio. The ratio creates an initial layer and then an attracting slow manifold. Enzyme kinetics shows that different coordinates and hypotheses create different reductions. Promoter switching shows that a reduced mean can be right while the distribution is wrong. A reduction is accepted only after the observable it claims to preserve survives comparison.
