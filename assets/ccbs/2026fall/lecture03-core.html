From reactions to systems: how biochemical behavior emerges

Stoichiometry turns molecular events into dynamics. Gene expression, metabolism, signaling, sequestration, and growth then reveal what the same equation means in different biological settings.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 3 · core page (full) · 2026-09-08 (Tue) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

Lectures 1 and 2 gave us credible parts. We estimated molecular sizes and copy numbers, collision and catalytic times, and the rate laws associated with elementary binding and catalytic steps. A parts list still does not explain a phenotype. This lecture changes scale. It asks how coupled reactions become an evolving system, and how levels, timescales, saturation, competition, and signal processing emerge from that system.1

The deliverable

By the end of 95 minutes, the room should be able to translate a reaction list into x˙=Sv(x)\dot{x}=S v(x), regroup the same system into production and loss, identify a conserved pool, derive Michaelis–Menten and promoter-occupancy laws from fast binding, derive growth dilution from molecule number and volume, and recognize the local balance whose stability Lecture 4 will test.

Part 1

From parts to a system

A reaction drawing, a rate law, and a state vector answer different questions. The dynamics close only when all three are connected.

1We know the parts; behavior belongs to the network

A single reaction can have a rate. Only a connected state can have a trajectory.

Imagine measuring every enzyme in a pathway and every one of its isolated rates. That still does not tell us whether an intermediate accumulates, a signal is filtered, or a protein concentration returns after a perturbation. Each reaction changes the concentrations used by other reactions. The output of one arrow becomes the input or regulator of another. Behavior is therefore a property of the coupled vector field, not an adjective attached to one arrow.

The dominant controlled grammar is reusable. Molecules bind and unbind. Bound states enable catalytic, transport, or fuel-coupled changes. This is not a claim that literally every cellular event is enzymatic. Spontaneous chemistry, diffusion, assembly, transport, and physical deformation also matter. It is a modeling claim: much biochemical regulation can be expressed as binding redistributing molecules among states, followed by productive transitions whose availability depends on those states.

Opening prediction. Two cells contain the same enzymes at the same concentrations. In one cell the enzymes form a chain; in the other they compete for one substrate. Must the cells have the same dynamics? No. The local parts match, but the wiring changes every balance.

2One reaction becomes one change vector

The left side determines what must be present. The difference between right and left determines what one event changes.

Fix an order for nn species, x=(x1,…,xn)Tx=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)^T. Write reaction ii as

α1iX1+⋯+αniXn⟶β1iX1+⋯+βniXn.\alpha_{1i}X_1+\cdots+\alpha_{ni}X_n \longrightarrow \beta_{1i}X_1+\cdots+\beta_{ni}X_n.(1)

The reactant vector is αi=(α1i,…,αni)T\alpha_i=(\alpha_{1i},\ldots,\alpha_{ni})^T. The product vector is βi\beta_i. One firing changes the state by the stoichiometric vector

νi=βi−αi.\nu_i=\beta_i-\alpha_i.(2)

For 2A+B→3B2A+B\rightarrow3B, ordered as (A,B,C)(A,B,C), the three vectors are

α=(2,1,0)T,β=(0,3,0)T\alpha=(2,1,0)^T,\quad\beta=(0,3,0)^T
ν=(−2,+2,0)T.\nu=(-2,+2,0)^T.

Do not confuse the first and last vectors. The event consumes two AA and one BB, so elementary mass action will use A2BA^2B. Yet one event changes BB by +2+2. Stoichiometry is a difference. Kinetics counts the reactants that must meet.

3A reaction list becomes an autonomous system

Stack change vectors into a matrix, stack event rates into a vector, and the arrows can run forward in time.

For mm reactions, place νi\nu_i in column ii of S∈Rn×mS\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}. If vi(x)v_i(x) is the rate per volume of reaction ii, then bookkeeping gives

x˙=Sv(x)=∑i=1mνivi(x).\pf{\dot{x}=S v(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_i v_i(x).}(3)

For an elementary reaction in a well-mixed concentration model, mass action closes its rate:

vi(x)=ki∏j=1nxjαji.v_i(x)=k_i\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_j^{\alpha_{ji}}.(4)

The exponent comes from αi\alpha_i, not νi\nu_i. A composite arrow such as transcription or a whole enzyme turnover can still occupy a column of SS, but its rate law must be derived, measured, or explicitly assumed. Stoichiometric accounting does not grant it mass action.

Work the enzyme cycle once

Use species order (E,S,C,P)(E,S,C,P) for E+S⇌C→E+PE+S\rightleftharpoons C\rightarrow E+P. Association, dissociation, and catalysis give

S=[−111−1101−1−1001],v=[k+ESk−CkcatC].S=\begin{bmatrix}-1&1&1\\-1&1&0\\1&-1&-1\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad v=\begin{bmatrix}k_{+}ES\\k_{-}C\\k_{\mathrm{cat}}C\end{bmatrix}.(5)

Multiplication gives all four ODEs without inventing signs. It also shows immediately that E+C=ETE+C=E_T and S+C+P=STS+C+P=S_T are conserved. With fixed parameters and environment, (3) is autonomous: its right-hand side depends on the current state, not explicitly on time. A timed nutrient pulse would make the model nonautonomous unless that environment were promoted to a state variable.

4One system has two useful accounting views

The matrix form emphasizes coupling. The production-loss split emphasizes the balance of one species.

Read row ii of (3) and collect positive and negative contributions:

x˙i=∑j:Sij>0Sijvj(x)−∑j:Sij<0∣Sij∣vj(x)=fi+(x)−fi−(x).\dot{x}_i=\sum_{j:S_{ij}>0}S_{ij}v_j(x)-\sum_{j:S_{ij}<0}|S_{ij}|v_j(x)=f_i^{+}(x)-f_i^{-}(x).(6)

Metabolic networks may contain hundreds of metabolites, branching reactions, and shared cofactors. There the compact form x˙=Sv\dot{x}=Sv keeps the coupling visible. Gene expression often has simple stoichiometry but a complicated regulated source. There the form x˙i=fi+(x)−fi−(x)\dot{x}_i=f_i^+(x)-f_i^-(x) puts the biological question on the page: which processes make this species, which remove it, and where does regulation enter? This is also called the production-degradation or birth-death split, although loss can include dilution, export, or conversion rather than molecular destruction. These are two readings of the same equation, not two kinds of chemistry.

Part 2

The same grammar in living systems

Four scenarios reuse the same accounting but expose different mechanisms: regulated production, enzyme saturation, shared metabolic pools, and competitive signaling.

5Gene expression makes regulation a production law

Promoter state controls production. Messenger degradation and growth control memory.

A useful gene-expression network has several players even before feedback is added. A transcription factor AA binds promoter DD. RNA polymerase produces messenger RNA MM from an active promoter state. Ribosomes translate MM into protein PP. RNases remove MM, while intrinsic proteolysis and growth remove protein concentration:

M˙=ftx(A)−(δM+λ)M,P˙=ktlM−(δP+λ)P.\dot M=f_{\mathrm{tx}}(A)-(\delta_M+\lambda)M,\qquad \dot P=k_{\mathrm{tl}}M-(\delta_P+\lambda)P.(7)

At steady state, solve in causal order:

M∗=ftx(A)δM+λ,P∗=ktlδP+λM∗.M^*=\frac{f_{\mathrm{tx}}(A)}{\delta_M+\lambda},\qquad P^*=\frac{k_{\mathrm{tl}}}{\delta_P+\lambda}M^*.(8)

The same denominators set response times. A genome-wide E. coli study found about 80% of measured mRNA half-lives between 3 and 8 minutes.5 For many stable bacterial proteins, intrinsic degradation is small compared with dilution, so δP≈0\delta_P\approx0 is useful. It is not a universal statement that bacterial proteins never degrade. Growth also changes gene dosage, polymerase and ribosome supply, and cell size, so dilution is the first whole-cell coupling, not the last.6

6Fast binding leaves a saturating effective rate

Michaelis–Menten and Hill laws resemble one another because both remove fast binding states from a slower model.

Enzyme reduction. Return to (5). Use E=ET−CE=E_T-C and set the complex derivative approximately to zero after its short boundary layer:

0≃C˙=k+(ET−C)S−(k−+kcat)C.0\simeq\dot C=k_+(E_T-C)S-(k_-+k_{\mathrm{cat}})C.
  1. Collect the terms multiplying CC.
  2. Define KM=(k−+kcat)/k+K_M=(k_-+k_{\mathrm{cat}})/k_+.
  3. Solve C=ETS/(KM+S)C=E_TS/(K_M+S).
  4. Use v=kcatCv=k_{\mathrm{cat}}C to obtain v=Vmax⁡S/(KM+S)v=V_{\max}S/(K_M+S).

This is the Briggs–Haldane quasi-steady argument.3 It is not the same as rapid equilibrium, and KMK_M is not generally a binding dissociation constant. Lecture 6 asks when the reduction is accurate.

Promoter reduction. Let one activator bind rapidly, D+A⇌DAD+A\rightleftharpoons D_A, with KD=DA/DAK_D=DA/D_A and DT=D+DAD_T=D+D_A. Solving the two equations gives

pbound=DADT=AKD+A,pfree=DDT=KDKD+A.p_{\mathrm{bound}}=\frac{D_A}{D_T}=\frac{A}{K_D+A},\qquad p_{\mathrm{free}}=\frac{D}{D_T}=\frac{K_D}{K_D+A}.(9)

If the bound state is active, ftx=r0+(r1−r0)pboundf_{\mathrm{tx}}=r_0+(r_1-r_0)p_{\mathrm{bound}}. If binding blocks transcription, use pfreep_{\mathrm{free}}. A concerted or effective cooperative model replaces AA by AnA^n and KDK_D by KnK^n:

fact=r0+(r1−r0)AnKn+Anf_{\mathrm{act}}=r_0+(r_1-r_0)\frac{A^n}{K^n+A^n}
frep=r0+(r1−r0)KnKn+Rn.f_{\mathrm{rep}}=r_0+(r_1-r_0)\frac{K^n}{K^n+R^n}.

The exponent of a fitted Hill curve is an effective slope unless a binding mechanism justifies a literal stoichiometry. Binding equilibrium, time-scale separation, and promoter conservation are the reasons these production laws are not elementary mass-action monomials. Allosteric state models make the same warning concrete: a compact occupancy curve can follow from several coupled binding states rather than one literal nn-body collision.4

7Metabolism exposes coupled fluxes and shared pools

Stoichiometry routes matter through the network. Allosteric binding changes a flux without changing its stoichiometric column.

In glycolysis, glucose-derived carbon passes through branches while ATP, ADP, NADH, phosphate, and enzyme pools participate in several reactions. The form x˙=Sv(x)\dot{x}=Sv(x) prevents a local rate law from hiding those shared obligations. At a metabolic steady state, Sv(x∗)=0Sv(x^*)=0. Individual fluxes need not be zero. Material can flow through every reaction while each internal concentration remains constant, which is why the same stoichiometric matrix also anchors constraint-based metabolic analysis.7

Phosphofructokinase couples F6P+ATP→F1,6BP+ADPF6P+ATP\rightarrow F1,6BP+ADP near a committed glycolytic step. Fast binding at regulatory sites changes that catalytic flux. Mammalian muscle PFK has functionally opposed adenine-nucleotide sites: AMP and ADP can activate, while ATP and ADP can inhibit depending on the site and concentration.8 ATP is also a substrate. The rate law is therefore not “ATP is bad” or “AMP is good”; it is a state-dependent consequence of catalytic and allosteric occupancy.

On that timescale, write the approximate pool and its energy coordinate as

AT=[ATP]+[ADP]+[AMP],EC=[ATP]+12[ADP]AT,ATP+AMP⇌2ADP.A_T=[ATP]+[ADP]+[AMP],\qquad \mathrm{EC}=\frac{[ATP]+\tfrac12[ADP]}{A_T},\qquad ATP+AMP\rightleftharpoons2ADP.(10)

Chapman, Fall, and Atkinson measured an energy charge near 0.8 in growing E. coli, with marked decline during starvation.9 The useful systems point is the constraint, not an alleged equivalence. AMP activation cannot generally be relabeled ATP inhibition. Only on the specified pool manifold do the two concentrations carry related information, and the PFK regulation itself remains organism-specific.

8Conserved pools turn binding into computation

A conserved total limits the reachable state. Competition for that total couples reactions that never touch in a pathway cartoon.

If a row vector ℓT\ell^T lies in the left null space of SS, then

ℓTS=0⟹ddt(ℓTx)=ℓTSv(x)=0.\ell^T S=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{d}{dt}(\ell^Tx)=\ell^TSv(x)=0.(11)

This invariant confines each initial condition to one stoichiometric compatibility class, the structural state space emphasized in chemical reaction network theory.2

A phosphorylation cycle redistributes a response regulator between YY and YPY_P. Without synthesis or degradation on the signaling timescale, YT=Y+YPY_T=Y+Y_P, or that sum plus explicitly modeled enzyme complexes, is fixed. Kinase and phosphatase activities control the fraction phosphorylated. In the EnvZ/OmpR system, writing the finite EnvZ and OmpR pools explicitly is essential to the model's input-output behavior.10

Binding conservation creates the same kind of coupling. The BMP system contains multiple ligand variants and multiple type-I and type-II receptor variants. Ligands compete for shared receptor pools, and the resulting complexes can phosphorylate Smad with different activities. Antebi and colleagues showed that this competitive architecture can generate additive, ratiometric, balance-detection, and imbalance-detection responses.11 Sequestration is therefore not passive storage. Occupying one receptor changes every route that needs that receptor.

Room check, 2 minutes. Double every molecule in a closed phosphorylation pool without changing rate constants. The conserved total doubles, but the phosphorylated fraction need not. Now add synthesis or degradation. The old invariant is gone because the network itself changed.

9Growth becomes dilution

Molecule number and concentration are different state variables in a growing cell.

Let N(t)N(t) be molecule number, V(t)V(t) cell volume, and x=N/Vx=N/V concentration. Balanced exponential growth at rate λ\lambda gives V˙=λV\dot V=\lambda V. Differentiate the quotient:

x˙=N˙V−NVV˙V=N˙V−λx.\dot x=\frac{\dot N}{V}-\frac{N}{V}\frac{\dot V}{V}=\frac{\dot N}{V}-\lambda x.(12)

A zero-order source in a concentration model means production is constant per unit volume: N˙=aV\dot N=aV. Then

x˙=a−λx,x∗=a/λ.\pf{\dot x=a-\lambda x,\qquad x^*=a/\lambda.}(13)

Starting from zero, x(t)=x∗(1−e−λt)x(t)=x^*(1-e^{-\lambda t}). After one doubling time Td=ln⁡2/λT_d=\ln2/\lambda, concentration has reached half of its steady value even though both volume and molecule number increased. If one fixed molecular machine instead makes kk molecules per minute, then x˙=k/V(t)−λx\dot x=k/V(t)-\lambda x. The dilution term survives, but the concentration-level source is not constant.

Part 3

The question that remains

We can now write the full vector field and identify some constraints. We have not yet shown that its steady states are stable.

10Balance is visible; stability is next

A steady state is where production balances loss. Stability asks what happens after the state is nudged.

A steady state satisfies Sv(x∗)=0Sv(x^*)=0. It does not require vi(x∗)=0v_i(x^*)=0 for every reaction. A metabolic steady state can carry nonzero flux. In one dimension, x˙=f+(x)−f−(x)\dot x=f^+(x)-f^-(x), a crossing is locally restoring when production exceeds loss just below it and loss exceeds production just above it.

The core story in eight moves
  1. Local estimates make reaction mechanisms plausible.
  2. Reactant and product vectors turn each mechanism into a change vector.
  3. Stacking those vectors and rates gives an autonomous system.
  4. The same accounting reads as stoichiometric flux or production minus loss.
  5. Fast binding makes effective Michaelis–Menten and Hill laws.
  6. Conserved pools couple metabolism, phosphorylation, and sequestration.
  7. Growth adds a universal concentration-dilution term.
  8. These structures suggest why simple systems remain bounded, but stability is a separate dynamical claim.

The cell resembles an extraordinarily dense chemical plant. Thousands of reactions share one interior, while an industrial plant separates far fewer reactions among vessels. Why does the cell neither run away nor stall? Today supplied partial answers: finite pools restrict state space, saturation caps some fluxes, dilution supplies loss, and regulation can oppose departures. None is a universal guarantee. The next lecture begins at x∗x^* and asks whether nearby trajectories return, depart, oscillate, or switch. The companion research essay develops the model-boundary and structural arguments beyond what fits in the room.13
