Reaction networks, with the central dogma as the worked example

Start with molecular events, separate stoichiometry from kinetics, and carry one gene all the way from arrows to timescales, steady state, regulation, and a testable simulation.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 3 · core page (full) · 2026-09-08 (Tue) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

A reaction network is a compact causal story. Each reaction column says what one event changes. Each rate law says how often that event occurs in the current state. Multiply the two and the arrows become a vector field. This lecture installs that translation once, then uses transcription and translation to show why the same equation contains steady abundance, response time, regulation, and growth.

The deliverable

By the end of 95 minutes, the room should be able to take a small reaction scheme, write x˙=Γv(x)\dot{x}=\Gamma v(x) with correct signs and units, find conserved totals, derive the two-stage gene-expression steady state, distinguish lifetime from half-life, add growth dilution, and explain exactly which assumption turns enzyme rates into Michaelis–Menten form.

0–8 min
Hook. How can one gene produce a steady protein level and still respond to an input?
8–25 min
Translation. Reaction events → stoichiometric columns → mass-action rates → ODE.
25–37 min
Structure. Units, positivity, conservation, and accessible state space.
37–57 min
Worked system. Build and solve the two-stage central-dogma model.
57–70 min
Timescales. Derive lifetime, half-life, and dilution from exponential growth.
70–81 min
Regulation. Derive the activating and repressing Hill forms as effective promoter occupancies.
81–91 min
Mechanism reduction. Derive Michaelis–Menten and name the QSSA assumption.
91–95 min
Exit. Audit one proposed model and choose a team extension.
Part 1

Arrows become equations

One convention for signs, one rule for elementary rates, and three structural checks before any solver is opened.

1The modeling contract

A reaction drawing answers “what changes?” A kinetic law answers “how often?” Only together do they close the dynamics.

Everything in this lecture is read off one picture, so write it down before naming anything. A reaction turns a list of reactants into a list of products, and the numbers in front of the species are the whole content of the drawing:

α1iX1+α2iX2+⋯+αniXn  →  ki    β1iX1+β2iX2+⋯+βniXn\alpha_{1i}X_{1} + \alpha_{2i}X_{2} + \dots + \alpha_{ni}X_{n} \;\xrightarrow{\;k_{i}\;}\; \beta_{1i}X_{1} + \beta_{2i}X_{2} + \dots + \beta_{ni}X_{n}

So αji\alpha_{ji} is how many copies of species XjX_{j} reaction ii consumes, and βji\beta_{ji} is how many it produces. Both are counted on the left and the right of the same arrow, and both are read straight off the drawing. Take one reaction and do it:

Table 1. Every vector in this lecture, read off one reaction. The species order is fixed once, here (A,B,C)(A,B,C), and never changes.
2A+B→3B2A + B \to 3BAABBCCread it as
consumed, αi\alpha_{i}210what stands left of the arrow
produced, βi\beta_{i}030what stands right of it
net change, γi\gamma_{i}−2+20one column of Γ\Gamma
rate exponents210the α\alpha row again, never the γ\gamma row

The last two rows are the ones that get confused, and the table exists to keep them apart. Stoichiometry is a difference and kinetics is a count. What the reaction changes is γi=(−2,+2,0)\gamma_{i} = (-2,+2,0). How often it fires is set by how many molecules must meet, which is αi=(2,1,0)\alpha_{i} = (2,1,0), giving v=kA2Bv = kA^{2}B. That BB appears in the rate with exponent 1 while its net change is +2+2 is the whole point: BB is a reactant and a product at once.

Now the general statement. For reaction ii, write its reactant vector αi\alpha_{i} and product vector βi\beta_{i}. The net change from one firing is

γi=βi−αi.\gamma _{i} = \beta _{i} - \alpha _{i}.(1)

Place these columns in the stoichiometric matrix Γ\Gamma. If vi(x)v_{i}(x) is the firing rate per volume, bookkeeping gives

x˙=Γv(x).\pf{\dot{x} = \Gamma v(x)}.(2)

Equation (2) is an identity once the species, reactions, and rates are chosen. For an elementary reaction, mass action supplies

vi(x)=ki∏jxjαji.v_{i}(x)=k_{i}\prod _{j}x_{j}^{\alpha_{ji}}.(3)

The product contains reactants only. The net stoichiometric coefficients belong in Γ\Gamma, not in the rate monomial. The 2025 notes introduce this two-way relation as x→vx\to v from mechanism and v→xv\to x from bookkeeping.2

2Work one network completely

Binding plus catalysis is the smallest network that exposes reversible events, an intermediate, conservation, and a composite rate law.

E+S⇌C→E+P.E+S \rightleftharpoons C \to E+P.(4)

Use species order (E,S,C,P)(E,S,C,P) and reaction order association, dissociation, catalysis. Build one column at a time:

eventΔEΔSΔCΔPrate
E+S→CE+S\to C−1−1+10konESk_{\mathrm{on}}ES
C→E+SC\to E+S+1+1−10koffCk_{\mathrm{off}}C
C→E+PC\to E+P+10−1+1kcatCk_{\mathrm{cat}}C
Γ=[−1+1+1−1+10+1−1−100+1],v=[konESkoffCkcatC].\Gamma = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & +1 & +1 \\ -1 & +1 & 0 \\ +1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & +1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad v = \begin{bmatrix} k_{\mathrm{on}}ES \\ k_{\mathrm{off}}C \\ k_{\mathrm{cat}}C \end{bmatrix}.(5)

Multiply the rows by the rate column. Nothing is guessed:

E˙=−konES+koffC+kcatCS˙=−konES+koffCC˙=−konES−(koff+kcat)CP˙=−kcatC\begin{aligned} \dot{E} &= -k_{\mathrm{on}}ES + k_{\mathrm{off}}C + k_{\mathrm{cat}}C \\ \dot{S} &= -k_{\mathrm{on}}ES + k_{\mathrm{off}}C \\ \dot{C} &= \phantom{-}k_{\mathrm{on}}ES - (k_{\mathrm{off}} + k_{\mathrm{cat}})C \\ \dot{P} &= \phantom{-}k_{\mathrm{cat}}C \end{aligned}(6)

The distinction between an elementary straight arrow and a composite net arrow is essential. Lecture 2 derives the diffusion ceiling for an elementary bimolecular association. A squiggly arrow such as S⇝PS⇝P only records net change; it does not license v=kSv=kS.1

3Invariants before solutions

The reaction matrix already identifies the physical state space. Solve inside it, not in all of Euclidean space.

A row vector ℓT\ell ^{T} in the left nullspace of Γ\Gamma obeys

d(ℓTx)/dt=ℓTΓv(x)=0.d(\ell ^{T}x)/dt = \ell ^{T}\Gamma v(x)=0.(7)

For (4), add the enzyme equations and add the substrate-containing equations:

E+C=ETE+C=E_{\mathrm{T}}
S+C+P=STS+C+P=S_{\mathrm{T}}

Those conserved totals reduce four variables to two. They also provide two exact simulation checks. Mass action preserves nonnegativity: at a boundary where one concentration is zero, every reaction that consumes it has zero rate. Horn and Jackson made stoichiometric compatibility classes central to chemical reaction network theory.3

Three checks before simulation
  1. Every term in each ODE has concentration-per-time units.
  2. The vector field points inward on every zero-concentration boundary.
  3. Every claimed conserved total has derivative exactly zero after substitution.
Part 2

One gene, worked through

The central dogma becomes a two-stage filter. Its rates determine abundance; its losses determine memory.

4The two-stage central-dogma model

Transcription produces a short-lived message; translation turns that message into a longer-lived output.

Write four composite events, each with its declared effective rate:

G⇝G+M  (ktxG),  M→∅  (δMM),M⇝M+P  (ktlM),  P→∅  (δPP).G \rightsquigarrow G+M \;(k_{\mathrm{tx}}G), \; M\to \emptyset \;(\delta _{\mathrm{M}}M), \\ M \rightsquigarrow M+P \;(k_{\mathrm{tl}}M), \; P\to \emptyset \;(\delta _{P}P).(8)

At the concentration level in exponentially growing cells, growth adds dilution rate μ\mu. The model is

M˙=ktxG−(δM+μ)MP˙=ktlM−(δP+μ)P.\pf{\dot{M}=k_{\mathrm{tx}}G-(\delta _{\mathrm{M}}+\mu )M} \\ \pf{\dot{P}=k_{\mathrm{tl}}M-(\delta _{P}+\mu )P.}(9)

Set the derivatives to zero in causal order:

  1. Solve the mRNA balance: M∗=ktxG/(δM+μ)M^{*} = k_{\mathrm{tx}}G/(\delta _{\mathrm{M}}+\mu ).
  2. Insert that result into the protein balance: P∗=ktlM∗/(δP+μ)P^{*} = k_{\mathrm{tl}}M^{*}/(\delta _{P}+\mu ).
  3. Combine them: P∗=ktxktlG/[(δM+μ)(δP+μ)]P^{*}=k_{\mathrm{tx}}k_{\mathrm{tl}}G/[(\delta _{\mathrm{M}}+\mu )(\delta _{P}+\mu )].

Use minutes and nM. These are deliberately round teaching values, not a fit to a particular promoter: ktxG=2nMmin⁡−1k_{\mathrm{tx}}G=2 nM \min^{-1}, ktl=10min⁡−1k_{\mathrm{tl}}=10 \min^{-1}, mRNA intrinsic half-life 3 min, protein intrinsic half-life 60 min, and cell doubling time 30 min.

In a 1 fL cell, 1 nM corresponds to 0.602 molecule on average, so this parameter set corresponds to about 4.7 mRNAs and 1.37×103 proteins. The conversion is not needed by the ODE, but it tells us that mRNA discreteness will matter in Lecture 5. Single-molecule measurements confirm that bacterial mRNAs often occupy this low-copy regime while proteins span a much wider range.7

Room check, 2 minutes. If translation doubles while every loss rate stays fixed, M∗M^{*} is unchanged and P∗P^{*} doubles. If growth doubles, both denominators change, so the effect is stronger than “protein is diluted faster.”

5Lifetime, half-life, and growth dilution

For x˙=a−bx\dot{x}=a-bx, the same coefficient bb sets steady abundance and response speed.

Subtract the steady state x∗=a/bx^{*}=a/b. The deviation q=x−x∗q=x-x^{*} obeys q˙=−bq\dot{q}=-bq, hence

x(t)=x∗+[x(0)−x∗]e−bt,    τ=1/b,    t1/2=ln⁡2/b.x(t)=x^{*}+[x(0)-x^{*}]e^{-bt}, \;\; \tau =1/b, \;\; t_{1/2}=\ln2/b.(10)

Growth dilution is not an extra degradation reaction. Derive it from concentration. If molecule amount is NN, cell volume is VV, and x=N/Vx=N/V, then

  1. Balanced exponential growth gives V˙=μV\dot{V}=\mu V.
  2. Differentiate the quotient: x˙=N˙/V−(N/V)(V˙/V)\dot{x}=\dot{N}/V-(N/V)(\dot{V}/V).
  3. Substitute V˙/V=μ\dot{V}/V=\mu: x˙=N˙/V−μx\dot{x}=\dot{N}/V-\mu x.
  4. If molecular degradation adds −δx-\delta x, the total concentration loss is −(δ+μ)x-(\delta +\mu )x.

For the worked protein, intrinsic half-life 60 min and dilution half-life 30 min combine to an effective half-life of 20 min:

t1/2,eff=ln⁡2δP+μ=[160+130]−1=20 min.t_{1/2,\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{\ln 2}{\delta_{P}+\mu} = \left[\tfrac{1}{60}+\tfrac{1}{30}\right]^{-1} = 20\ \text{min}.(11)

For a perfectly stable protein, δP=0\delta _{P}=0; its concentration still forgets with the doubling time. Growth also changes gene copy number, polymerase and ribosome supply, and cell volume, so dilution is only the first layer of growth coupling.6 In contrast, a genome-wide study found that roughly 80% of measured E. coli mRNAs had half-lives between 3 and 8 min.5

6Regulation as an effective production rate

A Hill function can summarize promoter occupancy. It is not itself an elementary reaction and its exponent is not automatically a molecule count.

Suppose a promoter is inactive when free and active when bound by an nn-mer of activator:

P0+nA⇌PA,    Kn=[P0] An[PA].P_{0}+nA \rightleftharpoons P_{\mathrm{A}}, \;\; K^{n}=\frac{[P_{0}]\,A^{n}}{[P_{\mathrm{A}}]}.(12)

With PT=P0+PAP_{\mathrm{T}}=P_{0}+P_{\mathrm{A}}, solve for the active fraction:

pon=[PA]PT=AnKn+An.p_{\mathrm{on}}=\frac{[P_{\mathrm{A}}]}{P_{\mathrm{T}}}=\frac{A^{n}}{K^{n}+A^{n}}.(13)

If transcription is fast enough to be summarized by occupancy, replace the constant source in (9) by kmax⁡pon(A)k_{\max}p_{\mathrm{on}}(A). For a repressor that blocks transcription when bound, the unbound fraction is

pon=KnKn+Rn=11+(R/K)n.p_{\mathrm{on}}=\frac{K^{n}}{K^{n}+R^{n}}=\frac{1}{1+(R/K)^{n}}.(14)
Do not over-read n

The derivation in (12)–(13) gives a literal binding stoichiometry only for the stated concerted equilibrium. In real promoter models, a Hill coefficient is often an effective log-slope. Use it to describe input–output shape; do not infer a unique microscopic mechanism from the fit.

Part 3

Eliminate a mechanism carefully

Michaelis–Menten is the first example where an intermediate disappears from the final rate law, but its assumptions must remain visible.

7Michaelis–Menten from rates

Conservation removes free enzyme. Quasi-steady state removes the complex derivative. The resulting constant is KMK_{\mathrm{M}}, not generally KdK_{d}.

Return to (4) and use E=ET−CE=E_{\mathrm{T}}-C:

C˙=kon(ET−C)S−(koff+kcat)C.\dot{C}=k_{\mathrm{on}}(E_{\mathrm{T}}-C)S-(k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})C.(15)
  1. After the initial complex-building transient, assume C˙≈0\dot{C}\approx 0. Lecture 6 derives when this standard QSSA is valid.
  2. Move the loss term: konETS=[konS+koff+kcat]Ck_{\mathrm{on}}E_{\mathrm{T}}S=[k_{\mathrm{on}}S+k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}}]C.
  3. Divide by konk_{\mathrm{on}} and define KM=(koff+kcat)/konK_{\mathrm{M}}=(k_{\mathrm{off}}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})/k_{\mathrm{on}}.
  4. Solve C=ETS/(KM+S)C=E_{\mathrm{T}}S/(K_{\mathrm{M}}+S).
  5. Product forms at v=P˙=kcatCv=\dot{P}=k_{\mathrm{cat}}C, so v=Vmax⁡S/(KM+S)v=V_{\max}S/(K_{\mathrm{M}}+S) with Vmax⁡=kcatETV_{\max}=k_{\mathrm{cat}}E_{\mathrm{T}}.

Briggs and Haldane's 1925 argument is a steady-state argument for the enzyme complex.4 Rapid equilibrium instead assumes catalysis is slow compared with dissociation and uses Kd=koff/konK_{d}=k_{\mathrm{off}}/k_{\mathrm{on}}. The constants agree only when kcat≪koffk_{\mathrm{cat}}\ll k_{\mathrm{off}}.

Room check, 2 minutes. If kcatk_{\mathrm{cat}} doubles, Vmax⁡V_{\max} doubles and KMK_{\mathrm{M}} also changes. If a derivation calls KMK_{\mathrm{M}} a binding affinity without checking kcat≪koffk_{\mathrm{cat}}\ll k_{\mathrm{off}}, the interpretation is not justified.

8Code and audit the model

A simulation is useful when it tests the equations and their regime, not when it merely redraws the expected curve.

A minimal implementation should keep the reaction representation visible:

state x; parameters k; matrix Γ
rate(x):    return [k_on·E·S, k_off·C, k_cat·C]
rhs(t, x):  return Γ @ rate(x)
integrate rhs; verify x ≥ 0 and the conserved totals.

The one plot worth showing live

Step the promoter source from 0 to its working value and plot M(t)M(t) and P(t)P(t) on the same normalized axis. Mark each time constant. Then double growth rate and predict, before running the code, which steady state and response time move. The prediction is the learning; the solver is the check.

Teach and extend

The core page supplies the complete spine. The student team should deepen one link without breaking the chain.

9The core story and beyond

Every later biomachine starts with the same move: list events, write Γ\Gamma, write vv, then ask what structure survives parameter uncertainty.

The story in six sentences
  1. Reaction events fix net changes.
  2. Elementary mechanisms fix mass-action rates.
  3. The product Γv(x)\Gamma v(x) is the vector field.
  4. Stoichiometry and positivity restrict where that field can move.
  5. For gene expression, production sets level while loss sets both level and memory, with growth contributing a universal dilution loss.
  6. Hill and Michaelis–Menten laws are reduced descriptions whose hidden assumptions must travel with them.
