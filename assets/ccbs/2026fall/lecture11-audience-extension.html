What do reaction orders predict before a response has settled?

Separate a component's sensitivity, an adapted endpoint, a transient excursion and a local stability boundary.

CCBS · Lecture 11 audience extension
Editorial synthesis of seven Week 6 student contributions · prepared with Codex assistance · 3 November 2026

A circuit can return to its original output after a large intervening excursion. The endpoint alone does not tell us whether that excursion was useful, harmless or too slow. The core separates reaction orders from turnover times to make these questions calculable. The audience contributions ask what each resulting prediction actually constrains.

We begin by identifying the derivative being measured. We then change the reaction order of one removal process, change the clocks while preserving exact adaptation, and change the norm used to measure a disturbance. Finally, a repressilator example locates a stability boundary without rounding a continuous response to an integer.

Names link to accepted, commit-pinned submissions. All numerical examples below are declared teaching models. Their calculations and regenerated figures are editorial work, independently checked against the submitted arguments.

1Name the derivative before interpreting saturation

Saturation makes several sensitivities small, but they are not the same quantity. Shunkang examines the response of a flux to an available carrier. For a positive carrier concentration EE, take

v=VEKE+ESKS+S,Hv←E:=∂log⁡v∂log⁡E=KEKE+E.v=V\frac{E}{K_E+E}\frac{S}{K_S+S},\qquad H^{v\leftarrow E}:=\frac{\partial\log v}{\partial\log E}=\frac{K_E}{K_E+E}.

The other substrate concentration SS, capacity VV and positive concentration scales KE,KSK_E,K_S are held fixed. At E/KE=0.1,1,10E/K_E=0.1,1,10, the reaction order is about 0.909, 0.5 and 0.091. This is a local fractional response to carrier concentration. A chemical potential difference is a different variable, with energy units and its own relation to concentrations. The same saturation formula cannot be transferred to it by changing the label.

An occupancy response exposes a second distinction. Weihan Luo writes independent-site occupancy as a sigmoid of log concentration. With free ligand S>0S>0, dissociation constant Kd>0K_d>0 and coordinate a=log⁡(S/Kd)a=\log(S/K_d),

p=SKd+S=11+e−a,dpda=p(1−p),dlog⁡pdlog⁡S=1−p.p=\frac{S}{K_d+S}=\frac1{1+e^{-a}},\qquad \frac{dp}{da}=p(1-p),\qquad \frac{d\log p}{d\log S}=1-p.

The ordinary derivative with respect to log input peaks at one quarter. The reaction order approaches one at low occupancy. The distinction matters when comparing a biochemical response with the gradient of an abstract neural network. Five unit-weight sigmoid layers, with biases chosen to place each layer at its midpoint, multiply their ordinary derivatives to give (1/4)5≃9.77×10−4(1/4)^5\simeq9.77\times10^{-4}. This chain-rule example does not identify every biochemical cascade with those layers. Its conclusion depends on the coordinates, weights and operating points.

Specify whether the derivative measures an absolute output change or a fractional output change. Keep the perturbed concentration and the held quantities visible.

2Test adaptation through the rate law, not only the diagram

An incoherent feedforward diagram does not by itself guarantee input cancellation. Fei Wang changes how strongly the mediator accelerates removal. Let qˉ1,qˉ2\bar q_1,\bar q_2 be two positive totals divided by their respective reference concentrations. Let w>0w>0 be input relative to its baseline. On the declared dimensionless clock, use

qˉ1′=w−(K+1)qˉ1qˉ2K+qˉ2,qˉ2′=w−qˉ2,K>0.\bar q_1'=w-(K+1)\bar q_1\frac{\bar q_2}{K+\bar q_2},\qquad \bar q_2'=w-\bar q_2,\qquad K>0.

The factor K+1K+1 makes both totals equal one at baseline input. The saturating factor concerns the mediator total qˉ2\bar q_2. It is not a claim that every enzyme-removal mechanism has this rate law. Solving the two steady balances gives

qˉ2∗=w,qˉ1∗=K+wK+1,dlog⁡qˉ1∗dlog⁡w=wK+w.\bar q_2^*=w,\qquad \bar q_1^*=\frac{K+w}{K+1},\qquad \frac{d\log\bar q_1^*}{d\log w}=\frac{w}{K+w}.

When K≫wK\gg w, removal is nearly proportional to the mediator and the steady gain is small. When the mediator-dependent factor saturates, the indirect branch cannot offset the direct input as effectively. At K=0.5K=0.5, doubling input from one to two changes the endpoint from one to 5/35/3.

The endpoint is reached locally because the Jacobian is triangular with negative diagonal entries. At a positive fixed point those entries are −(K+1)w/(K+w)-(K+1)w/(K+w) and −1-1. Thus the loss of exact adaptation in this example does not result from a local instability. It results from a changed steady cancellation.

Calculate the settled input-output derivative from the specified rates. Check stability separately before interpreting that derivative as an observed endpoint response.

3Keep turnover times when predicting an adapting transient

Exact adaptation can leave a wide range of transient amplitudes. Haobo Zheng keeps the core's bilinear rate law and changes the clocks:

q˙1=k1w−δq1q2,q˙2=k2w−γq2.\dot q_1=k_1w-\delta q_1q_2,\qquad \dot q_2=k_2w-\gamma q_2.

All constants are positive and have the units required by the balances. At a held positive input w∗w^*, the endpoint is q1∗=k1γ/(δk2)q_1^*=k_1\gamma/(\delta k_2). Define its pool relaxation time τ1=1/(δq2∗)\tau_1=1/(\delta q_2^*) and the mediator relaxation time τ2=1/γ\tau_2=1/\gamma. Let zi=δqi/qi∗z_i=\delta q_i/q_i^* and zw=δw/w∗z_w=\delta w/w^* denote small relative changes. The linearized balances are

τ1z˙1=zw−z1−z2,τ2z˙2=zw−z2.\tau_1\dot z_1=z_w-z_1-z_2,\qquad \tau_2\dot z_2=z_w-z_2.

For a small constant input step from an adapted state, solve the second equation first. Its delayed rise supplies the time-dependent forcing of the first. With r=τ2/τ1≠1r=\tau_2/\tau_1\ne1,

z1(t)zw=rr−1(e−t/τ2−e−t/τ1),tpeakτ1=rlog⁡rr−1,z1,peakzw=r−1/(r−1).\frac{z_1(t)}{z_w}=\frac{r}{r-1}\left(e^{-t/\tau_2}-e^{-t/\tau_1}\right),\qquad \frac{t_\mathrm{peak}}{\tau_1}=\frac{r\log r}{r-1},\qquad \frac{z_{1,\mathrm{peak}}}{z_w}=r^{-1/(r-1)}.

When the two times are equal, the response is (t/τ1)e−t/τ1(t/\tau_1)e^{-t/\tau_1} times the input step. Its peak fraction is 1/e1/e. A fast mediator suppresses the excursion. A slow mediator allows the output to respond before the compensating branch catches up. Both return to zero relative output change.

The unchanged endpoint concerns a constant input. A continually changing input is another task. For example, if ww grows exponentially at rate β>0\beta>0, the exact mediator balance asymptotically gives q2/w→k2/(γ+β)q_2/w\to k_2/(\gamma+\beta). Within the unbounded bilinear model, the first total then tends to k1(γ+β)/(δk2)k_1(\gamma+\beta)/(\delta k_2). The fractional offset from the constant-input set point is βτ2\beta\tau_2. This is a model prediction for an indefinitely growing input, not a contradiction of step adaptation.

Retain the clock ratio when predicting pulse size and timing. State whether the input becomes constant or continues to change.

4Translate a stability certificate into the measured disturbance

A decreasing weighted distance does not force every species displacement to decrease. Siheng Wang and Zixuan Wang independently construct triangular local systems with this property. Use relative pool deviations on a common dimensionless clock:

z˙1=−z1+κz2,z˙2=−z2,(z1(0),z2(0))=(0,ϵ).\dot z_1=-z_1+\kappa z_2,\qquad \dot z_2=-z_2, \qquad (z_1(0),z_2(0))=(0,\epsilon).

Both eigenvalues are minus one for every coupling κ\kappa. Yet the exact solution is z2=ϵe−tz_2=\epsilon e^{-t} and z1=κϵte−tz_1=\kappa\epsilon t e^{-t}. At κ=8\kappa=8, a 1% second-pool displacement produces a first-pool peak of about 2.94% at time one. The ordinary Euclidean distance peaks at about 2.97 times its initial value.

The diagonal certificate remains valid. Choose

V=z12+(1+κ24)z22,V˙=−2(z1−κz22)2−2z22<0away from zero.V=z_1^2+\left(1+\frac{\kappa^2}{4}\right)z_2^2, \qquad \dot V=-2\left(z_1-\frac{\kappa z_2}{2}\right)^2-2z_2^2<0 \quad\text{away from zero}.

This is precisely the distinction needed to interpret diagonal stability. The certificate bounds one weighted quadratic quantity. Its associated weighted norm is V\sqrt V. The structural result in the core covers every constant positive turnover-time vector while keeping the balance-order matrix fixed. Its Lyapunov weights include those chosen times. It does not assert monotone decay of every displayed coordinate or of an arbitrarily chosen norm.1

Name the measured coordinate or norm and bound that quantity. Use a stability certificate for its stated return property, then assess transient tolerances separately.

5Locate a stability boundary using the actual local slope

A continuously changing reaction order can locate a transition more precisely than a limiting exponent. Álvaro calculates this for a symmetric three-repressor teaching model. Let the three scaled totals satisfy

qˉi′=α1+qˉi−1n−qˉi,i=1,2,3cyclically,α,n>0.\bar q_i'=\frac{\alpha}{1+\bar q_{i-1}^{n}}-\bar q_i, \qquad i=1,2,3\quad\text{cyclically},\qquad \alpha,n>0.

The symmetric fixed point qˉ∗>0\bar q^*>0 solves qˉ∗(1+qˉ∗n)=α\bar q^*(1+\bar q^{*n})=\alpha. Its local repression slope has magnitude

g=nqˉ∗n1+qˉ∗n,λ0=−1−g,λ±=−1+g2±i3g2.g=\frac{n\bar q^{*n}}{1+\bar q^{*n}},\qquad \lambda_0=-1-g,\qquad \lambda_\pm=-1+\frac g2\pm i\frac{\sqrt3g}{2}.

The complex pair reaches the imaginary axis at g=2g=2. Such a crossing requires n>2n>2 at finite α\alpha. Substituting qˉ∗n=2/(n−2)\bar q^{*n}=2/(n-2) into the fixed-point relation gives

αcrit=nn−2(2n−2)1/n.\alpha_\mathrm{crit}=\frac{n}{n-2}\left(\frac2{n-2}\right)^{1/n}.

At n=3n=3, the crossing occurs at α≃3.780\alpha\simeq3.780. At α=20\alpha=20, it occurs at n≃2.273n\simeq2.273. The current core's reaction-order definition already retains continuous slopes. This calculation applies that definition at the actual fixed point instead of replacing it by a limiting value.

The next experiment should test the response that the calculation actually predicts. Around one positive operating point, measure a small input step, the two pool trajectories and their relaxation times. Test whether the inferred rate slopes predict the endpoint gain and transient peak together. A mismatch can then be assigned to the rate law, the clock or the observation rule before a global behavior is inferred.

Evaluate the reaction order at a consistent fixed point. Separate the resulting local stability boundary from any further claim about nonlinear oscillation.
