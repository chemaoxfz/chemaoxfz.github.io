Protein design: from evolutionary clues to testable molecules

How historical search methods became an AI design pipeline. What each model supplies. How to decide whether a proposed molecule works.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · Lecture 15 core · 29 October 2026 · methods snapshot checked 20 October 2026

Lecture 14 treated proteins as a demand on the cell's production capacity. A new protein earns that cost only if it performs a useful task. Today we ask how to choose its amino-acid sequence, how computation narrows the possibilities, and which measurements establish success.

The lecture follows three questions. How did we learn to guide the search? How can we carry out one reproducible design workflow? How do the many current AI methods divide the remaining problems?

A design becomes useful when its proposed structure and its measured behavior support the same specification.

Part 1

Learn how protein design acquired its guidance

First distinguish the problem we want to solve from the information available to guide it.

1Specify the molecular job

A sequence, a structure and a behavior are three different objects. A design task connects them through an explicit purpose.

P1A molecular job differs from a coordinate prediction.

For a folding task, success might mean a soluble protein that adopts a chosen fold. For a binding task, success also includes recognizing the intended partner under stated conditions. For an enzyme, success includes catalysis. Each task asks for more than a visually plausible coordinate file.

We need four molecular concepts to describe these tasks. A sequence specifies the ordered amino-acid identities. A backbone specifies the protein's main-chain geometry, including the repeated nitrogen, alpha-carbon and carbonyl-carbon atoms. It leaves side-chain identities and arrangements available for design.

Atomic coordinates assign a three-dimensional position to each modeled atom. A backbone is therefore a partial coordinate description. A full atomic structure also places the side chains. An interface is the contact region between molecular partners. Designing an interface requires their relative arrangement as well as compatible local chemistry.

The size of sequence space makes guidance necessary. A length-100 chain has 20100≈1.27×1013020^{100}\approx 1.27\times10^{130} possible sequences if each position admits the 20 standard amino acids. We cannot test them all. A useful method must propose a small set enriched for the desired behavior.

The histories of design and prediction therefore develop together. Design improves the proposal. Prediction helps assess its structural consequences. Experiments determine whether the selected consequence actually occurs.

You can state a design task as a molecular behavior under specified conditions, then identify which parts a sequence or coordinate file can answer.

2Follow the sources of design guidance

The central historical change is where useful guidance comes from: an assay, a physical score, evolutionary variation, or a learned model.

P2Different historical methods supply different kinds of guidance.

Directed evolution uses the experiment to guide search. Starting with a protein, generate variants, measure a desired property and use successful variants to guide another round. Chen and Arnold's 1993 subtilisin study demonstrated this logic for activity in an altered solvent environment. The assay defines what is selected. Interactions among mutations can make a favorable change depend on the sequence already present. [Chen and Arnold, 1993]

Energy-based design replaces some experimental trials with a structural calculation. A score rewards plausible packing, interactions and solvation. Search then chooses amino-acid identities and side-chain conformations. Dahiyat and Mayo's 1997 FSD-1 design used a specified backbone as the template. Its experimental characterization tested whether the selected sequence could realize the intended fold. [Dahiyat and Mayo, 1997]

Top7 made backbone choice part of the design search. Kuhlman and colleagues iterated sequence and backbone optimization to build a protein with a new topology. The original 93-residue design was tested by crystallography. This is a useful contrast with choosing a sequence for an already fixed backbone. [Kuhlman et al., 2003]

The Top7 calculation used Rosetta for backbone search and RosettaDesign for sequence selection. Alternating these searches allowed sequence packing and backbone geometry to improve together. This establishes a computational pattern that survives into modern workflows: propose one object, evaluate its compatibility with another, and revise. FSD-1 used a separate earlier design method. [Kuhlman et al., 2003, design protocol]

Evolution supplied a different route to structural guidance. Related sequences preserve constraints that allow a protein family to function. Direct-coupling analysis used their joint variation to infer structural contacts. AlphaFold's CASP13 system then learned distance distributions and used them to guide a structural search. AlphaFold2 made coordinate prediction a jointly trained system. The next two sections explain what the alignment contributes and why the alignment alone was not the whole breakthrough. [Morcos et al., 2011] [Senior et al., 2020] [Jumper et al., 2021]

You can distinguish experimental selection, fixed-backbone sequence design, joint sequence-and-backbone design, and structure prediction by the information each uses to guide search.

3Read structural constraints from evolution

A multiple sequence alignment places corresponding positions of related proteins in columns. Variation across those columns can reveal constraints that one sequence does not show.

P3Aligned variation carries information about compatible residue combinations.

Conservation and coupled variation answer different questions. Conservation identifies a position that changes little across the alignment. Coupled variation identifies combinations whose frequencies do not follow independent variation. In the constructed alignment below, one column stays fixed while two other columns change together.

A coupling can be structurally informative because residues that interact may need compatible replacements. Yet correlation alone does not establish a contact. If position 1 influences position 2 and position 2 influences position 3, positions 1 and 3 may correlate without touching. Shared ancestry and uneven sampling also affect the alignment. Direct-coupling methods address a network of dependencies rather than equating every correlation with a contact. [Morcos et al., 2011]

What does an unchanged column tell us?

A position may have a strong structural or functional constraint. The alignment does not by itself identify which constraint applies. A conserved catalytic residue and a conserved packing residue can look similar in a column.

What does a coupled pair add?

Compatibility can belong to a combination. A substitution at one position may become acceptable after another position changes. The informative object is a joint pattern across related sequences.

When is more sequence data insufficient?

Many nearly identical sequences add less independent information than a diverse family. An alignment can also mix different functions or pairing assumptions. Depth, diversity, alignment quality and the task all matter.

The alignment gives a model evidence about compatible arrangements. It still leaves a geometric problem: many local constraints must fit into one three-dimensional molecule. AlphaFold2's architecture learns to connect these two sources of information.

You can explain what a multiple sequence alignment contributes and why coupled substitutions require interpretation before they become structural evidence.

4Combine evolution with learned geometry

Multiple sequence alignments are a major ingredient in AlphaFold2. Its performance came from a coordinated system of representations, geometry and training.

P4AlphaFold2 combines evolutionary evidence with a learned geometric construction.

IngredientWhat it contributesWhat it does not establish alone
Multiple sequence alignmentPatterns of conservation and joint variation.A unique, physically correct arrangement.
Evoformer and pair representationA learned way to integrate information across positions and residue pairs.A measured molecular mechanism.
Geometry-aware structure moduleCoordinates assembled with geometric structure built into the architecture.A thermodynamic ensemble or kinetic pathway.
Joint training and recyclingTraining signals that connect the representations to structural accuracy, plus iterative refinement.Universal accuracy outside the training and evaluation domains.

The original ablations tested several of these ingredients. They support the combined explanation rather than a single cause such as “more alignments.” The training also used self-distillation on predicted structures. [Jumper et al., 2021, Fig. 4 and Methods]

Pairwise guesses must fit one molecule

The pair representation stores learned information for each residue pair. Triangle-based updates let a pair exchange information through a third residue. This gives the network a way to coordinate relationships across the molecule. It does not impose every geometric consistency condition exactly.

A rigid rotation should not change the protein

The structure module works with residue-centered geometric frames. Its invariant point attention combines learned features with three-dimensional relationships without attaching biological meaning to the arbitrary orientation of the coordinate file.

Training and inference use different kinds of repetition

Training adjusts the network using structural and auxiliary objectives. Self-distillation expands training with predictions on additional sequences. Recycling is an inference operation: a previous prediction informs the next pass. Recycling is neither a new experimental observation nor elapsed folding time.

A useful design filter became available when structural prediction became much more informative. Given a proposed sequence, we can ask whether a predictor returns the intended fold. This is a test of computational self-consistency: two computational routes agree on a structure. The prediction still needs an experimental test, especially if the generator was optimized using a related predictor.

ColabFold makes this prediction step more accessible through fast alignment search with MMseqs2 and practical AlphaFold workflows. ColabFold is a workflow around predictors, rather than a new definition of experimental validation. [Mirdita et al., 2022]

A new designed sequence may have few natural homologues. A predictor can still use structural regularities learned during training. RFdiffusion's original computational evaluations included single-sequence AlphaFold2 prediction for designed proteins. That setting needs its own evaluation. It does not make alignment information irrelevant for natural proteins. [Watson et al., 2023, computational evaluation]

You can explain AlphaFold2 through its complementary ingredients and place it in a design workflow as a structural prediction step.

We now have informative proposals and predictions. The next task is to make one design decision reproducible.

5Fix the design specification

Before opening a model, separate what is fixed, what can change, and what evidence would count as success.

P5A design specification makes every output interpretable.

Our task is fixed-backbone redesign of Top7. We retain the experimental backbone and propose new amino-acid sequences compatible with it. This is a manageable demonstration on the lecturer's CPU. The task also exposes the boundary between generating a candidate and establishing that it folds.

The downloaded input is PDB 1QYS. The parser uses its 92 observed residues, chain A3–A94, and maps selenomethionine to methionine. The original design had 93 residues. The deposited numbering includes expression-related residues, so the observed fragment and original design length are different. Unresolved termini are not silently reconstructed. [PDB 1QYS]

This familiar structure is a teaching input. It is not a held-out generalization test, and it may overlap a model's training data. The original experimental Top7 sequence and our newly generated sequences must therefore remain distinct throughout the workflow.

6Identify what the next design step must supply

A workflow becomes easier to understand when every step names its input and the new object it produces.

P6Identify what is given and what is missing before choosing a method.

Six questions to master before choosing software

These six questions are the conceptual framework for the method survey in Part 3. For each one, you should be able to name the available input, the requested output and what remains untested. The first four concern molecular objects or arrangements. The last two concern how design choices are coupled and how experiments guide them.

Need coordinates
You have a sequence and any specified partners. Predict their three-dimensional arrangement. This is structure prediction.
Need a sequence
You have a backbone and its context. Choose amino-acid identities compatible with it. This is inverse folding, or sequence design conditional on structure.
Need a backbone
You have structural constraints, such as a motif to preserve. Propose the main-chain geometry that can satisfy them. This is backbone generation.
Need an interface
You have a molecular partner and an interaction objective. Propose a contact arrangement and a compatible designed partner. Folding the designed partner alone cannot settle this task.
Need joint generation
Several design objects remain open. Joint generation proposes or completes them together, such as sequence and structure under a shared constraint. Models differ in which objects and annotations they can couple.
Need assay feedback
You have variants and a way to measure their behavior. Assay feedback uses those observations to select or propose the next variants. The new information comes from an experiment.

Our Top7 demonstration answers Need a sequence. The backbone already exists, so we can isolate inverse folding. A different project might first answer Need a backbone, then use the resulting coordinates to ask for a sequence. This is why the two routes below join at the same step.

RFdiffusion generates backbone arrangements under structural constraints. Its original formulation learned denoising using a structure-prediction architecture. A sampled trajectory progressively creates a backbone. The constraints can describe a fold, an assembly or a structural motif. The output then needs amino-acid identities. [Watson et al., 2023]

ProteinMPNN supplies the inverse-folding step. It represents local backbone geometry with a graph and samples residue identities using the structural context and previously assigned identities. The conditioning means that changing the backbone changes the design problem. [Dauparas et al., 2022]

pθ(r∣C)⏟structural generationpθ(a∣r,C)⏟sequence design\underbrace{p_\theta(\mathbf r\mid\mathcal C)}_{\text{structural generation}} \qquad \underbrace{p_\theta(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf r,\mathcal C)}_{\text{sequence design}}

Here r\mathbf r denotes coordinates, a\mathbf a amino-acid identities, C\mathcal C the task's constraints, and θ\theta each model's learned parameters. These distributions describe learned proposals. They are not equilibrium Boltzmann distributions merely because they assign probabilities.

8Match each claim to its evidence

The next decision is which candidates deserve further work. Several useful prediction readouts answer different questions.

P8Confidence, structural agreement and function require different evidence.

The predicted local-distance difference test score, written pLDDT, estimates local structural accuracy. Predicted aligned error, written PAE, describes uncertainty in relative placement. An interface predicted template-modeling score, written ipTM, summarizes confidence across chains. These are predictor outputs with distinct definitions.

ReadoutQuestion it helps answerWhat remains open
pLDDTHow confident is the predictor in local structural accuracy?Is the intended function present? A mean also hides local weak regions.
Predicted aligned error (PAE)How uncertain is the placement of one region when another region supplies the alignment frame?Are the relative arrangements experimentally correct?
Interface confidence, such as ipTMDoes the predictor support a particular arrangement across chains?What is the affinity, specificity or biological effect?
Structural agreementDoes the predicted structure agree with the design over the chosen atoms?Are the two computations sharing a failure mode?
Expression, folding and functional assaysWhich required properties occur under the tested conditions?Do they transfer to the intended cellular or application context?

Structural agreement must state its comparison. For a monomer, report which residues and atoms enter a superposition and its root-mean-square deviation (RMSD). A small whole-chain deviation can still hide a misplaced functional loop. For a complex, aligning the full complex and aligning each chain independently ask different questions.

Check the six questions before reading the method names

For each scenario, name what is missing. Then reveal the answer. Several questions can belong to one project.

A complete sequence is available, but its three-dimensional arrangement is unknown.

Need coordinates. Structure prediction proposes coordinates for a specified sequence. Its output still needs assessment.

A scaffold has been chosen, but the amino acids that should realize it are open.

Need a sequence. Inverse folding conditions identity choices on a supplied backbone and any context.

A small structural motif must be retained, but the supporting fold has not been chosen.

Need a backbone. Backbone generation proposes geometry under that structural constraint. Sequence design follows unless the method couples both choices.

A candidate folds, but no arrangement with the intended partner has been designed.

Need an interface. The contact region and relative pose are missing. A successful binding assay remains a further requirement.

Only part of a structure is fixed, and both the remaining geometry and residue identities may change.

Need joint generation. Several objects must be proposed compatibly. A joint method or an explicitly coupled workflow can address them.

Twenty variants have been measured, and those results should determine the next batch.

Need assay feedback. The next choice should use the observations, rather than only a computed prediction score.

Part 3

Navigate the AI methods and their limits

Classify methods by their inputs, outputs and evidence. Then ask what the intended function still requires.

9Choose a method by the object it supplies

Apply the six questions from section 6. The method names now fill roles in a framework you already know.

P9A method's conditional input and output locate its role in the workflow.

Broader molecular representations have expanded what can be conditioned on. AlphaFold3 predicts assemblies that include proteins and other molecular components. LigandMPNN designs sequence using a supplied nonprotein atomic context. RFdiffusion2 builds scaffolds around catalytic functional-group geometry. RFdiffusion3 and BoltzGen extend generative modeling toward atom-level interactions. These capabilities address different missing inputs. They do not establish every downstream behavior. [Abramson et al., 2024] [Dauparas et al., 2025] [Ahern et al., 2026] [Butcher et al., inspected preprint] [Stark et al., inspected preprint]

Sequence and multimodal models offer another starting point. ProGen learns sequence generation conditioned on information such as protein-family tags. ESM3 jointly represents sequence, structure and function annotations. A prompt can constrain a proposal before a complete backbone is chosen. Function-related conditioning still needs a functional test. [Madani et al., 2023] [Hayes et al., inspected author preview] [author documentation]

A computational loop and an experimental loop receive different feedback. NISE iterates between sequence design and structure prediction to improve compatibility with a small molecule. Its reported candidates are then tested experimentally. Directed evolution instead uses measured behavior to select variants during the loop. Either can be useful, but an iteration is not evidence of experimental feedback by itself. [Fry et al., 2026]

Energy-based design / Rosetta

Historical foundation, still a component of modern workflows

Input. A backbone or structural constraints and a molecular scoring function.

Output. Sequences and conformations found by optimization or sampling.

Contribution, limits and evidence

Useful contribution. Makes packing, geometry and chosen energetic terms explicit. Top7 links Rosetta backbone search with RosettaDesign sequence selection.

Limit. Approximate energies and incomplete sampling can favor a structure that is not realized experimentally.

Evidence. Top7 includes an experimental structural test. FSD-1 is a separate earlier energy-based method, not a Rosetta result.

10Locate the constraints that function adds

Different molecular jobs fail for different reasons. The most useful limitation is the one that changes the next design decision.

P10Molecular function imposes constraints beyond one favorable structure.

Binding adds specificity and context. A plausible interface leaves affinity, off-target interactions, accessibility and conformational state unresolved. A predictor's confidence in the interface is not a calibrated binding constant. Boltz-2 adds an affinity-prediction task, but its documented protein–small-molecule setting cannot be silently transferred to arbitrary protein–protein binding. [Passaro et al., inspected preprint and official documentation]

Catalysis adds a reaction pathway. Positioning catalytic groups is an important structural task. Efficient turnover also depends on chemical barriers, protonation, substrate access, product release and competing states. RFdiffusion2 demonstrates progress in functional-group scaffolding and experimental enzyme design. Its results establish particular tested cases, not an arbitrary-reaction design guarantee. [Ahern et al., 2026]

Switching adds populations and timescales. A switch needs more than two individually plausible structures. The intended input must alter their relative populations, and interconversion must occur on a useful timescale. Multiple random-seed predictions do not automatically provide a thermodynamic ensemble or a transition rate. AlphaFold3 explicitly discusses the limits of interpreting its structural variability in this way. [Abramson et al., 2024, limitations]

Living cells add production and competition. Concentration, localization, degradation, competing partners and expression burden can change whether a molecular design works. Lecture 14's resource budget therefore returns at the end of a molecular design pipeline. A component that succeeds in a purified assay still needs a test in the intended context.

You can identify the missing constraint for a folding, binding, catalytic or switching task and select evidence that addresses that constraint.

11Close the loop with informative experiments

The next round should respond to what the current round taught us. A failed assay can be useful when it distinguishes reasons for failure.

P11Experiments guide the next proposal and supply the parameters a cell model needs.

If a candidate is insoluble, a binding assay may not diagnose the intended interface. If it is soluble and folded but does not bind, the interface and target state become more relevant. If it binds but has no cellular effect, investigate occupancy, localization and the connection between binding and the desired mechanism.

The connection to this course is quantitative. For a simple binding pair, a cell model may require an equilibrium dissociation constant, binding kinetics and concentrations. For a catalytic component, it may require turnover and substrate dependence. Coordinates can help propose such a mechanism. They do not supply every parameter.

For a reversible elementary association, let k+k_+ be the bimolecular association rate constant and k−k_- the unimolecular dissociation rate constant. Their units are inverse concentration per time and inverse time, respectively. The equilibrium dissociation constant therefore has concentration units:

Kd=k−k+K_d=\frac{k_-}{k_+}

This ratio illustrates a limitation even of a measured equilibrium constant. Different association and dissociation rates can produce the same KdK_d but different response times. A model of adaptation or switching may need those timescales separately.

To see the difference, consider a ligand maintained at a fixed free concentration ss. Let θ(t)\theta(t) be the fraction of binding sites occupied. For independent sites with elementary association and dissociation, its response from an initially unbound state is:

θ˙=k+s(1−θ)−k−θ,θ(0)=0,θ(t)=sKd+s(1−e−t/τ),τ=1k+s+k−.\begin{aligned} \dot\theta&=k_+s(1-\theta)-k_-\theta, \qquad \theta(0)=0,\\ \theta(t)&=\frac{s}{K_d+s}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau=\frac{1}{k_+s+k_-}. \end{aligned}

The example below sets s=1 μMs=1\,\mu\mathrm{M}. The fast pair has k+=1 μM−1s−1k_+=1\,\mu\mathrm{M}^{-1}\mathrm{s}^{-1} and k−=1 s−1k_-=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}. The slow pair has both constants ten times smaller. Both have Kd=1 μMK_d=1\,\mu\mathrm{M} and equilibrium occupancy one-half. Their relaxation times are 0.5 seconds and 5 seconds.

Lecture 16 receives the remaining problem: how do we assemble molecular structures, measured parameters and an explicit interaction architecture into a model that predicts cellular behavior? The designed molecule is a proposed component. Composition into a working biomachine is a further modeling and experimental task.

You can use a design result or failure to choose an informative next measurement and distinguish molecular coordinates from the parameters needed by a cellular model.

Optional transfer and scoring details

Transfer the workflow from a fold to a binder

A binder task adds a second molecule and a site-specific objective. Specify the target state, the intended binding region, tolerated changes and the off-target comparison. A backbone generator or predictor-guided design method proposes an interface. Sequence design must then respect that interface and the rest of the protein.

Evaluation separates three questions. Does the binder fold? Does it bind the intended target under the tested conditions? Does that interaction cause the intended effect? A negative control helps separate target-specific recognition from nonspecific association. This is a conceptual transfer of the pipeline. The executed classroom example remains Top7 backbone redesign.

What a sequence score and a sampling temperature calculate

An autoregressive model assigns amino-acid probabilities in a decoding order. A mean negative log conditional probability over the designed positions gives a sequence score:

s(a∣r)=−1L∑j=1Llog⁡pθ(aπj∣r,aπ<j).s(\mathbf a\mid\mathbf r)= -\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=1}^{L} \log p_\theta(a_{\pi_j}\mid\mathbf r,\mathbf a_{\pi_{<j}}).

The permutation π\pi is the decoding order, aπ<j\mathbf a_{\pi_{<j}} lists identities assigned earlier, and LL is the number of scored positions. ProteinMPNN supports different decoding orders and masks. Comparing scores therefore requires the same conditioning and scoring convention.

For logits ℓa\ell_a, a sampling temperature Ts>0T_s>0 changes the selection probabilities:

p(Ts)(a)=exp⁡(ℓa/Ts)∑bexp⁡(ℓb/Ts).p^{(T_s)}(a)= \frac{\exp(\ell_a/T_s)}{\sum_b\exp(\ell_b/T_s)}.

This rescales a model's choices. It is not the molecule's physical temperature and does not turn the sequence score into a free energy.

Read a method paper as an evidence pipeline

Record the task distribution, the training and evaluation split, the generation budget, the filtering budget, the experimental denominator and the success criterion. Check whether variants of the same target appear across training and evaluation. Separate held-out structure accuracy, designed-sequence self-consistency and prospective experimental success.

Then ask what remains unknown about a new target. Published averages can hide target-to-target variation. A useful comparison matches the target set, experimental budget and definition of success. The absence of such a comparison is a reason to keep a claim conditional.
