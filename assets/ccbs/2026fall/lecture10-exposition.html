From chemical balances to an adaptation claim

Start from the reaction list with the rate law of every composite step, the input, the measured output and the model boundary. The six steps are worked on the core's models, then run on a network the core does not treat.

CCBS · Lecture 10 · exposition · 12 October 2026

An adaptation mechanism must connect a specified function to feasible chemistry. The core chooses the adaptation biomachine, then follows binding and catalysis to the reaction orders that restore its output. This chapter teaches how to check that connection yourself. Begin with the full balances. Derive the limiting law, identify the measured quantity, and test the conditions under which the response returns.

Six questions organize the calculation. What identity constrains the output? Which observable does it constrain? Which dominance regime justifies the law? Is the proposed rest feasible? Does it attract nearby trajectories? Which changes preserve the result? The examples develop these questions in order. The final worked problem transfers them to a protein whose inactive form converts its active form.

Choose a calculation
  • For the core’s control introduction, derive the proportional and integral responses, including their units.
  • For chemical implementation, start with the resolved memory-removal mechanism. Then compare a modification switch with the two-cycle feedback circuit.
  • For chemotaxis, distinguish the exact methylation balance from the activity-only teaching law and its biological support.
  • For the feedforward reversal, derive the finite-affinity mechanism and its free and total readouts. Then test reaction orders and added loads.

The lineage explains where these questions came from. The research essay uses them to choose a perturbation that distinguishes candidate mechanisms. This chapter supplies the calculations those comparisons require.

Conventions

A bare species symbol is a free concentration: XX, A∗A^*, UU. A total, the sum over every state that contains a constituent, is qXq_X. A complex of two species is CXYC_{XY}, named by its constituents, except in the two-cycle feedback circuit, whose four complexes keep the numbers C1C_1 to C4C_4 that the core and the literature use. An elementary step is a straight arrow carrying its rate constants. A composite step, such as production, an unresolved enzymatic step or growth dilution, is a bare squiggle, and its rate law is written beside it. A dissociation constant belongs to binding equilibrium, and a Michaelis constant KMK_M to the steady state of a resolved model (§3).

Units are written with c for concentration and T for time. Every number carries one of four labels where it appears. Exact means exact within the stated model, usually a closed form. Leading order means a limit or a dominance approximation, with its condition named. Numerical means a model prediction at stated illustrative parameters. Reported means a value stated in a cited source, with its page. No number here is a fit to data.

Part 1

The audit

What an adaptation claim states, the data needed to check it, and six questions in the order in which they depend on each other.

1The audit starts from rate laws, an input, an output and a boundary

An adaptation claim says that a measured quantity returns to its old value after a persistent step of an input, for a class of inputs, and keeps doing so under a class of changes. Checking it needs more than a reaction list: the rate law of every composite step, the input, the output and the model boundary. With those written down, six questions taken in a fixed order test the claim and find its limits.

An adaptation claim names three things

An adaptation claim names an observable, a class of inputs and a class of changes. Hold the input at one value until the system rests, step it to another value, and hold it there. The claim is that a named observable then returns exactly to its old value (core §2). "Exactly" is a statement about every input in a class, not about one trace. "Robustly" is a statement about a class of changes to the network itself, such as a changed rate constant, an added load on the output or growth dilution (core A16).

A reaction list alone does not fix the dynamics, because a composite step carries no rate law. An elementary step is drawn with a straight arrow and its rate constant, and its mass-action rate law follows from the arrow. A composite step is drawn with a bare squiggle, and its rate law must be stated beside it. The core's incoherent feedforward loop shows why that statement matters. Its resolved binding-catalysis mechanism approaches a reduced model in which the degrader X2X_2 removes the output X1X_1 through a composite step. With the rate law δx1x2\delta x_1x_2 the loop adapts exactly. With δx1x2f\delta x_1x_2^f and f≠1f\neq1 it does not (§7). The composite reaction list is the same in both cases. The rate law differs. The resolved chemistry determines whether either law is a valid approximation (§4).

The audit's starting data has five parts. The reaction list, with every arrow classified as elementary or composite. The rate law of every composite step. The input: what is stepped, and in which units. The output: which species, complex or pool is measured. The model boundary: which processes the model contains, for instance whether growth dilutes its species. This page calls these five together the starting data.

Six questions in a fixed order

Six questions, asked in order, audit the claim.

  1. Identity. Find a relation among the rates, exact for the stated rate laws, whose zero at a rest constrains the output, and say what it stores, in which units (§2).
  2. Observable. Find which quantity the identity pins, and how far each measured pool follows it (§3).
  3. Regime. Decide whether the identity is exact or a limiting law, and if it is a limit, find the regime of the totals in which it holds (§4).
  4. Positivity and reachability. Check that a rest with the pinned value exists with nonnegative amounts, and that the system can reach it (§5).
  5. Stability. Test whether that rest attracts, with every conservation law kept in the linearization (§6).
  6. Class of changes. Name the changes the identity survives and those it does not (§7).

The six questions organize the audit, while the chemistry sets the derivation order. Write exact species and total balances before imposing a closure or a dominance regime. An error identity suggested in step 1 remains provisional until step 3 establishes its assumptions. Only then can the feasibility and stability tests establish a returning response. The last step asks which changes preserve that result.

The audit records outcomes. It neither ranks models nor proves that a model cannot adapt. Each of the core's models illustrates one step's question in Part 2, and §8 gives every model's outcome at every step. A model can lack one kind of identity and still be input invariant. The core's subtractive compensator returns by a matched constant and has no identity at all. Its incoherent feedforward loop returns because its reaction orders match, and its identity is weighted rather than error-only (§2, core A9 and §9). So failing to find an identity of one kind does not show that none exists, and it does not show that the output cannot return.

Write the starting data of an adaptation claim (the classified reaction list, the rate law of each composite step, the input, the output and the boundary), and plan its audit as six questions in order: identity, observable, regime, positivity and reachability, stability with conservation kept, and the class of changes.

2Rate identities reveal what can pin the output

An identity is a relation among the rates that holds exactly for the stated rate laws. Three kinds appear among the core's models: an exact mass balance, an error-only identity and a weighted error identity. Each pins the output under its own condition. Cancelling, in a combination of rate equations, every step that does not read the output is a useful way to find one, though not the only way, and what an identity stores has units of its own.

Three kinds of identity

An exact mass balance is a combination of totals whose rate reduces, by stoichiometry, to a few fluxes. In the two-cycle feedback circuit the modified-B total qB∗q_{B^*} changes only by two catalytic steps, q˙B∗=kB1C3−kB2C4\dot q_{B^*}=k_{B1}C_3-k_{B2}C_4 (§3). At a rest it pins a relation between two complexes, kB1C3=kB2C4k_{B1}C_3=k_{B2}C_4. That relation is not yet a statement about a measured output. Turning it into one needs further assumptions, which is the work of step 2.

An error-only identity is a state whose rate depends on the system only through the output. Write it as z˙=g(y)\dot z=g(y), where yy is the output and the function gg vanishes only at the set point y∗y^*. Then every rest has y=y∗y=y^*, whatever the other states and whatever the plant's constants. The core's integral loop has the simplest one, z˙=x\dot z=x. Its chemotaxis teaching model has another, m˙=VR−VBa/(KB+a)\dot m=V_R-V_Ba/(K_B+a), whose right side depends on the state only through the receptor activity aa (core §5).

A weighted error identity carries a multiplier that depends on the state. Write it as z˙=h (y∗−y)\dot z=h\,(y^*-y), where the multiplier hh depends on the state. A rest pins y=y∗y=y^* only where hh is nonzero. The core's incoherent feedforward loop has z=x1−(k1/k2)x2z=x_1-(k_1/k_2)x_2, with z˙=δx2 (x1∗−x1)\dot z=\delta x_2\,(x_1^*-x_1) (core §10). At zero input the multiplier vanishes and the rest pins nothing, which is why the core states its claim for w>0w>0.

Finding an identity in the starting data

One useful route to an identity combines rate equations so that every step that does not read the output cancels. For mass-action steps this is linear algebra on the stoichiometry: look for a combination of species whose net change is zero in every step except those whose rate laws read the output. In the running example (1), binding and unbinding move a molecule between its free and bound forms, so they leave the totals qZ1=Z1+CZ1Z2q_{Z_1}=Z_1+C_{Z_1Z_2} and qZ2=Z2+CZ1Z2q_{Z_2}=Z_2+C_{Z_1Z_2} unchanged. Removal of the complex lowers both totals by one, so it cancels in their difference. Only the reference and sensing steps survive:

ddt(qZ1−qZ2)=μ−θX.\frac{d}{dt}\big(q_{Z_1}-q_{Z_2}\big)=\mu-\theta X.(2)

This is an exact mass balance that is also error-only. Its right side depends on the state only through XX, and it vanishes only at X=μ/θX=\mu/\theta. So every rest has X=μ/θX=\mu/\theta, whatever γ\gamma, kk and the binding constants (exact).

The route is useful, not guaranteed. A linear combination of rate equations finds identities that are linear in the species. The identity of §9 is a logarithm, which no such combination produces. So this route can miss an identity in a nonlinear coordinate.

Two models with no error-integrating state

Two of the core's comparisons have no state that integrates the output's error, and each behaves for its own reason. Lecture 9's high-gain loop has the output as its only slow state, so the output itself holds the input, as an error of about 1/(1+H)1/(1+H) of a small relative change, where HH is the loop gain (core A8). 3The identity pins one quantity, and a pool adapts as far as that quantity dominates it

An identity pins something at rest, but not necessarily what an experiment measures. In the two-cycle feedback circuit the exact mass balance ties one complex to the bound enzyme, which puts it near the set point while the enzyme is saturated. Three measurable quantities contain that complex to different degrees, and an exact decomposition says how far each one follows it.

The two-cycle feedback circuit, written as starting data

The design has twelve elementary steps on ten species. An input II modifies a protein AA to A∗A^*, the modified A∗A^* modifies a protein BB to B∗B^*, the modified B∗B^* demodifies A∗A^*, and a fixed enzyme EE demodifies B∗B^* (core §7). The pinned quantity in the sequestering pair

In the sequestering pair the identity pins the free output, because the sensing step reads the free output. The rate law θX\theta X in (1) is a function of free XX, so (2) pins free XX. 9A protein whose inactive form converts its active form, audited in six steps

The audit transfers to a network the core does not treat. In Shinar and Feinberg's two-species module, an inactive form converts the active form into more of itself and recovers on its own. The six steps find an error-only identity in a logarithm, a free active form pinned at one value, no limiting law for the stated rate laws, a threshold total below which no positive rest exists, a stable rest only once the conservation law is kept, and a class of changes that depends on the model's boundary.

The starting data

A protein has an active form UU and an inactive form DD, and the inactive form converts the active one. Shinar and Feinberg introduce absolute concentration robustness with this toy mass-action system (eq. (1), p. 1389).3 In their notation AA is the active form, BB the inactive form, and α\alpha and β\beta are the rate constants. Here they are renamed, because AA, BB and β\beta already have other meanings in the core:

U+D→kconv2D,D→krecU.\begin{aligned}&U+D\xrightarrow{k_\mathrm{conv}}2D,\\ &D\xrightarrow{k_\mathrm{rec}}U.\end{aligned}(18)

Both are elementary mass-action steps, as the paper treats them, so each carries its rate constant: the conversion constant kconvk_\mathrm{conv}, second order, in 1/(c T)1/(\mathrm{c\,T}), and the recovery constant kreck_\mathrm{rec}, first order, in 1/T. The rate equations are

U˙=−kconvUD+krecD,D˙=kconvUD−krecD.\begin{aligned}\dot U&=-k_\mathrm{conv}UD+k_\mathrm{rec}D,\\ \dot D&=k_\mathrm{conv}UD-k_\mathrm{rec}D.\end{aligned}(19)

The input is the protein total qP=U+Dq_P=U+D. Shinar and Feinberg take synthesis and degradation to be slow, so the total is constant over the time the module takes to equilibrate (p. 1389). A change of total is therefore the persistent step. The output is the free active form UU. The boundary is closed: no synthesis, no degradation and no growth on that time scale. This page calls the system the closed module.

What is new here, relative to the core, is everything after the identity. The memory of this module is the core's third route, multiplicative growth, whose logarithmic identity the core states for an abstract memory in A2. That statement has no plant, no conservation law, no boundary and no stability test. This module is a fully specified reaction list from the literature, closed by a conservation law: its memory and its output are two forms of one protein, its input is their total, and its stability needs that conservation law.

Step 1: an error-only identity in a logarithm

The inactive form's rate equation is a weighted error identity, and its logarithm is error-only. From (19), D˙=D (kconvU−krec)\dot D=D\,(k_\mathrm{conv}U-k_\mathrm{rec}), a weighted error identity whose multiplier DD pins UU only where DD is positive. Fix any positive reference concentration DrefD_\mathrm{ref}, so that the logarithm is dimensionless. On D>0D>0,

ddtln⁡DDref=kconv(U−kreckconv),\frac{d}{dt}\ln\frac{D}{D_\mathrm{ref}}=k_\mathrm{conv}\Big(U-\frac{k_\mathrm{rec}}{k_\mathrm{conv}}\Big),(20)

exactly: an error-only identity with the set point krec/kconvk_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv}, a concentration. The memory ln⁡(D/Dref)\ln(D/D_\mathrm{ref}) is a pure number, and its rate is a frequency. The action is the conversion flux kconvUDk_\mathrm{conv}UD, in c/T, which at rest balances recovery. The disturbance is the added protein.

Shinar and Feinberg's theorem certifies from the network's structure that the active form takes one value across positive rests, and the rest equations give that value. Their theorem concerns a mass-action system that admits a positive steady state, whose network has deficiency one and contains two nonterminal nodes differing only in one species. It concludes that this species takes the same value in every positive steady state (pp. 1390–1391).3 The theorem does not say what that value is, whether the rest is stable, or which rest the system reaches. Step 2 gives the value, and steps 4 and 5 the rest.

The module meets the theorem's structural hypotheses. A node is a combination of species at the head or tail of an arrow. Nodes that can each be reached from the other along arrows form a strong-linkage class, and a class is terminal when no node in it reacts to a node outside it. The nodes of terminal classes are terminal, and all other nodes are nonterminal (p. 1390). The deficiency is the number of nodes, less the number of connected pieces of the reaction diagram, less the number of independent reaction vectors. Network (18) has four nodes, U+DU+D, 2D2D, DD and UU, two connected pieces and one independent reaction vector, so its deficiency is 4 − 2 − 1 = 1. Each node forms its own strong-linkage class, and those of 2D2D and UU are terminal. So U+DU+D and DD are the nonterminal nodes, and they differ only in UU (exact).

Step 2: the free active form is pinned

Every positive rest has the same active form, and the inactive form holds the rest of the total. Setting (20) to zero gives U∗=krec/kconvU^*=k_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv} and D∗=qP−krec/kconvD^*=q_P-k_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv} (exact; Shinar and Feinberg's eq. (3), p. 1389). The observable that returns is free UU. The total qPq_P is the input itself, and DD holds it. A readout of total protein would show no return at all, and only a readout specific to the active form shows the adaptation. In Figure 6 (top left) a bolus of active protein raises the total from 2 to 4: UU jumps to 3 and returns to 1, while DD rises from 1 to 3 (numerical).

The two-dimensional linearization has a zero eigenvalue, and that eigenvalue is the conservation law. Step 6: the changes the identity survives depend on the boundary

In the closed module a load or growth leaves the identity intact and removes the positive rest. A change of total above the threshold is rejected (steps 2 and 4). A change of kconvk_\mathrm{conv} or kreck_\mathrm{rec} moves the set point, as a change of a reference constant should. A load ℓU\ell U on the active form does not enter D˙\dot D, so (20) holds exactly, but the total now falls, q˙P=−ℓU\dot q_P=-\ell U. At a positive rest UU would equal krec/kconv>0k_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv}>0, so the total would keep falling, and no positive rest exists. Growth dilution keeps the identity error-only, ddtln⁡(D/Dref)=kconvU−krec−λ\tfrac{d}{dt}\ln(D/D_\mathrm{ref})=k_\mathrm{conv}U-k_\mathrm{rec}-\lambda, with a shifted target, but q˙P=−λqP\dot q_P=-\lambda q_P drains the pool (exact). In both cases the adaptation claim fails at step 4, positive feasibility, while the identity survives.

Robustness to a change of total is not rejection of a sustained inflow. A constant synthesis of UU at the rate vsynv_\mathrm{syn}, with nothing removed, gives q˙P=vsyn>0\dot q_P=v_\mathrm{syn}>0, and no rest exists at all (exact). The closed module's claim holds the total fixed after each change. It says nothing about a flow through the module.

Opening the boundary gives a different model, with its own rest. The audit's verdict

The closed module adapts exactly to a change of its total, above a threshold and from a positive memory, and its stability needs the conservation law. Its row in the audit table: an error-only identity in ln⁡(D/Dref)\ln(D/D_\mathrm{ref}); free UU pinned at krec/kconvk_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv}; no limiting law for the stated laws, and a Michaelis constant once the conversion is resolved; a positive rest exactly when qP>krec/kconvq_P>k_\mathrm{rec}/k_\mathrm{conv}, reached from every start with D(0)>0D(0)>0; stability on the line of fixed total; a change of total rejected, while a load or growth leaves the identity intact and removes the positive rest unless the boundary is opened. Shinar and Feinberg add a caution of their own: reverse reactions omitted from such models, and interactions with the rest of the cell, should leave the robustness strong but imperfect in vivo (p. 1391).3
