Binding sets state; catalysis commits change

Binding plus catalysis is proposed as a compact model grammar for cellular biomachines. It succeeds only when the translation predicts more than it hides.

CCBS · EST 5053 · Fall 2026 · lecture 8 · core page (full) · 2026-09-24 (Thu) 10:40–12:15 · students present, lecturer-backed

The first seven lectures supplied molecules, rates, dynamics, noise, reduction, and thermodynamics. This lecture tries to compress them into a modeling language. Reversible binding selects configurations. Catalytic or fuel-coupled transitions change chemical identity, location, or mechanical progress. The language is useful only if it turns unfamiliar mechanisms into reusable modules without deleting the observable that made the mechanism interesting.

Live research claim

This is a conjecture of the 2026 course, not a settled exhaustive taxonomy of cell biology. Each student team is invited to break it. A counterexample is valuable when it identifies the extra coordinate, spatial process, memory, or collective effect that the two-arrow language cannot compress.

Part 1

Propose the grammar

A useful restriction must say what its terms mean and what evidence would make us abandon it.

1Make a restriction worth attacking

Try to write the mechanism with reversible state selection and committed state change.

The proposed alphabet has two clauses. A binding clause, X+L⇌XLX+L\rightleftharpoons XL, records association, dissociation, and the occupancy of a state. A commitment clause, such as XL→X+PXL\rightarrow X+P, records a transition that changes identity or advances against a maintained reservoir.

The same protein can participate in both clauses, and neither arrow is a claim about design intent. The restriction is about how we model edges. It should expose repeated modules, reduce independent parameters, and generate new predictions. If every unexplained event becomes another unnamed “state” or every transition is simply relabeled, the grammar has no scientific content.

2Define the two verbs

Binding changes a configuration's probability and lifetime. Catalysis changes the route through state space.

Call an edge “binding” when states differ by noncovalent association or site occupancy. At equilibrium, its forward and reverse fluxes cancel and the occupancy ratio follows a free-energy difference or dissociation constant. In a driven cycle, the same physical association need not sit at equilibrium.

Call an edge “catalytic commitment” when a catalyst enables chemistry, transport, or mechanical progress without being consumed stoichiometrically. Commitment does not mean that microscopic reversal is impossible. It means that, on the chosen timescale and under maintained concentrations, this edge advances the output coordinate. If reverse flux matters, draw it.

Binding energy is not automatically catalytic power. Stabilizing the substrate ground state can deepen the wrong well; useful binding often organizes or preferentially stabilizes the transition-state route.1

The labels belong to a chosen resolution. Product release is binding in a fine-grained enzyme model but may be absorbed into a catalytic turnover constant in a coarse one. The translation must therefore name its timescale and output before classifying edges. Otherwise two teams can use the same words for incompatible models.

Room check, 90 seconds. For a kinase, label substrate association, phosphorylation, product release, and ATP/ADP exchange. Which are binding clauses? Which advance chemical identity? Which omitted reverse step could become important?
Part 2

Translate mechanisms

Three familiar systems show what the same grammar keeps, eliminates, and predicts.

3Enzyme: occupancy becomes flux

The canonical mechanism separates complex occupancy from product formation.

E+S⇌ k−1  k1 ES→kcatE+P.E+S\xrightleftharpoons[\,k_{-1}\,]{\,k_1\,}ES\xrightarrow{k_{\rm cat}}E+P. (1)

Under the standard quasi-steady-state approximation,

[ES]≈ET[S]KM+[S],v=kcat[ES],KM=k−1+kcatk1.[ES]\approx\frac{E_T[S]}{K_M+[S]},\qquad v=k_{\rm cat}[ES],\qquad K_M=\frac{k_{-1}+k_{\rm cat}}{k_1}. (2)

Occupancy depends on every route that removes the complex, while flux additionally multiplies occupancy by the catalytic rate. This is why KMK_M is not generally the equilibrium dissociation constant Kd=k−1/k1K_d=k_{-1}/k_1. Briggs and Haldane's mechanism already contains the two verbs, plus the timescale claim needed to eliminate one state.2 The 2025 Lecture 3 packet is the course source for this binding reduction and its dominance regimes.7

4Gene: occupancy licenses an event

Promoter binding selects a state. Transcription initiation advances a transcript coordinate.

A repressor can reversibly bind DNA, creating free and blocked promoter states. Polymerase and activators add further states. If those states exchange rapidly relative to productive initiation, the transcription rate can be approximated by an occupancy-weighted initiation rate.

The bridge is an assumption, not a definition. DNA binding does not itself synthesize RNA, and expression is not determined by occupancy when initiation, escape, or promoter switching is slow. A thermodynamic promoter model can fit a steady input-output curve while missing burst durations and waiting-time distributions.

A complete translation writes the measurable output explicitly, for example r=∑ipirir=\sum_i p_i r_i, where each promoter state has an initiation rate rir_i. Equilibrium predicts the pip_i. It does not determine the rir_i without a kinetic or experimental bridge.

This translation therefore comes with two measurements: state occupancy or dwell time, and productive initiation. Without both, a fitted response cannot tell whether regulation changed binding, catalysis, or an omitted step.

5Modification: opposed commitments make a switch

Two binding-and-catalysis modules can transform a graded capacity ratio into a sharp state transition.

A kinase binds XX and commits it to X∗X^*. A phosphatase binds X∗X^* and commits it back. Each arm saturates because enzyme is finite. Both arms share the conserved substrate pool.

When both enzymes are near saturation, a small change in their maximal-rate ratio can move most substrate from one form to the other. Goldbeter and Koshland derived this zero-order ultrasensitivity.3 In the generated case, both normalized Michaelis constants are 0.03 and the midpoint occurs when kinase and phosphatase capacities match.

6Hidden binding changes reaction order

Eliminating a state produces regime-dependent effective algebra.

For Michaelis–Menten flux, the local logarithmic sensitivity to substrate is

nlocal=dln⁡vdln⁡S=KMKM+S.n_{\rm local}=\frac{d\ln v}{d\ln S}=\frac{K_M}{K_M+S}. (3)

It is 0.909 at S/KM=0.1S/K_M=0.1, 0.5 at 1, and 0.0909 at 10. The same mechanism appears nearly first order when binding sites are mostly free, then nearly zero order when enzyme is saturated. A fitted reaction order is therefore a local property of an operating regime, not a count of molecular reactants.

Lecture 6 supplies the warning: a response function inherits the validity conditions of the eliminated binding layer. Fast relative to which input, on which domain, and for which observable must travel with the reduced law.

Part 3

Compose and stress-test

A model grammar earns its value by scaling across modules and revealing exactly where an extra description is needed.

7Local rules compress combinatorial state

Association and modification rules can describe many molecular configurations without naming each species.

A protein with nn sites that can be empty, bound, or modified has up to 3n3^n explicit configurations. If each site's local behavior is independent of unspecified distant sites, roughly 2n2n association and modification rules can generate them. For eight sites, the illustrative count is 6,561 states versus 16 rules.

This is real compression only while the omitted context does not alter a rate. Allostery, steric exclusion, localization, and shared enzymes can force contexts back into the rules. Rule-based modeling makes the binding-catalysis language scalable while also giving it a precise failure signal: exceptions proliferate.4

8A machine needs a cycle and a coordinate

Binding and fuel chemistry can bias circulation, but motion requires a map from state transitions to space.

A transporter binds cargo on one side, changes access, releases cargo on the other, and resets. A motor binds a track, exchanges and hydrolyzes nucleotide, changes conformation, and steps. In both, binding defines loaded states and fuel biases a closed cycle. Orientation, location, or displacement must be an explicit coordinate.

At low Reynolds number, reciprocal motion cannot generate net progress, as Purcell's scallop argument shows.5 Chemical cycling alone also does not establish motion. The coupling must say which edge advances, which can slip, and how load changes rates.

In the generated four-state ring, current rises from zero with affinity and approaches 0.167 in normalized units as another edge becomes limiting. That is directed chemical circulation. Mechanical displacement is current multiplied by the signed step associated with each transition.

9Find where the grammar breaks

Space, continuous motion, collective media, and memory can refuse compression into a small Markov state graph.

A finite-state description works when conformations form well-separated basins and transitions between them are the measured events. It fails when force, extension, or transition-path motion inside a basin matters. A well-mixed description fails when diffusion, geometry, or rapid rebinding controls encounters. A pairwise binding model fails when a condensate's collective material state changes diffusion and reaction conditions.

Coarse-graining can also create memory. Several hidden states combined into one observed label give non-exponential waiting times, so the present state alone no longer determines the future hazard. Adding hidden states may restore a Markov model, but unlimited state expansion makes the proposed grammar vacuous.

Finally, a large number of possible binding states does not prove that a cell performs a computational task. The task needs defined inputs, outputs, error, time, energy, and a comparison class. Tight binding gives a rectifier-like limit, not an exact universal ReLU at arbitrary concentration and affinity. These are corrections to the provocative 2025 computation framing.6

10Decide whether the restriction earns its keep

Translate, test, and compare. Elegance alone is not evidence.

  1. Predictive compression: does a small reusable rule set predict new interventions?
  2. Thermodynamic consistency: do cycle rate ratios match declared reservoirs?
  3. Identifiability: can binding and commitment rates be separately constrained?
  4. Observable preservation: does the reduction retain the measured flux, distribution, or path?
  5. Closure under composition: do shared pools, sequestration, and fuel leave module behavior intact?
  6. Alternative model: does this grammar outperform a serious spatial, mechanical, memory-bearing, or phenomenological rival?
Exit ticket. Pick one mechanism from your project. Draw its binding clauses in blue and commitment clauses in coral. Circle the measured output. Then name the single intervention on which this translation and its strongest alternative make different predictions.
The arrival

Binding plus catalysis is useful when it is a restriction, not a slogan. It should expose hidden assumptions, compress repeated mechanism, and generate a risky prediction. When it fails, the failure identifies the next verb the model needs.
