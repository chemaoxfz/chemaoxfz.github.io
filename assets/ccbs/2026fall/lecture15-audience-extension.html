What evidence makes a designed protein a usable component?

Separate a computational proposal from a measured function, its resource cost and its behavior after connection.

CCBS · Lecture 15 audience extension
Editorial synthesis of seven Week 7–8 student contributions · prepared with Codex assistance · 3 November 2026

A proposed protein becomes a useful component only relative to a specified task. The core follows the path from sequence and structure to function. The audience asks what evidence is still needed when a selected candidate must supply a required output inside a larger system.

We first separate candidate selection from independent evidence. We then calculate an expression requirement, identify the capacity that limits output, and ask which kinetic parameters a measurement can determine. The final calculation changes the measurement boundary: binding to a readout can alter the signal being read.

This chapter uses accepted submissions available on 3 November. Week 8 remains open. Names link to the pinned originals. The models below are abstract teaching comparisons with declared illustrative parameters.

1Separate a selected score from evidence of function

Different computational stages answer different questions. Runzi Yan distinguishes backbone generation, sequence design, structure prediction and functional measurement. For example, RFdiffusion generates structural proposals, while ProteinMPNN assigns sequences conditioned on a backbone. Success in either task does not itself determine the response of a connected biochemical system.1,2

Selecting among more candidates also changes the meaning of a passing score. Yibing Li asks how repeated sampling changes apparent success. Suppose, as a deliberately simple null model, that every candidate lacks the required function and each independently passes a noisy filter with probability pp. Then

Pr⁡(at least one pass among n)=1−(1−p)n.\Pr(\text{at least one pass among }n)=1-(1-p)^n.

At p=0.05p=0.05, this probability is about 0.642 for 20 candidates and 0.923 for 50. The calculation concerns false passes under its null model. Real candidates can differ in function, and their errors can be correlated. A count of passing candidates must therefore be interpreted with the sampling and validation rules.

Selection can also raise the winning score without changing any true function. Leo Sin motivates the question of optimizing a proxy. To isolate the statistical effect, let every candidate have the same true value μ\mu and score μ+ϵi\mu+\epsilon_i, where the errors are independent and uniform on [−1,1][-1,1]. This is an editorial comparison, not a model fitted to a design method.

The largest transformed error Umax=max⁡i(ϵi+1)/2U_\mathrm{max}=\max_i(\epsilon_i+1)/2 has cumulative distribution unu^n on [0,1][0,1]. Its mean is n/(n+1)n/(n+1). Consequently, the mean error in the selected score is

E[max⁡iϵi]=n−1n+1.\mathbb E[\max_i\epsilon_i]=\frac{n-1}{n+1}.

A fresh evaluation with an independent, zero-mean error still has mean μ\mu, even for the selected candidate. This comparison explains why the evidence used for selection and the evidence used for validation should be distinguished.

State what each computational stage predicts. Separate the evidence used to select a candidate from an independent measurement of the required function.

2Convert an affinity into the amount needed for function

A binding affinity determines occupancy only after the concentrations and required output are specified. Peng Su asks how a required complex abundance becomes a protein cost. Let free ligand concentration S>0S>0 be clamped by a reservoir. A binder has total concentration qE=E+CESq_E=E+C_{ES} and dissociation constant KdK_d. The equilibrium relation is

CES=qESKd+S.C_{ES}=q_E\frac{S}{K_d+S}.

To achieve a required complex concentration Creq>0C_\mathrm{req}>0, the binder total must satisfy

qE≥qE,min⁡:=Creq(1+KdS).q_E\geq q_{E,\min}:=C_\mathrm{req}\left(1+\frac{K_d}{S}\right).

Improved affinity lowers the expression requirement toward a stoichiometric floor. Even at vanishing Kd/SK_d/S, every required complex contains one binder, so qE,min⁡→Creqq_{E,\min}\to C_\mathrm{req}. A finite total ligand pool would introduce a separate ligand balance and a feasibility condition on that pool.

Converting concentration to a protein budget requires another declared quantity. Write the binder protein fraction as ϕD=αqE\phi_D=\alpha q_E, where α\alpha accounts for molecular mass and the reference protein concentration. For a hypothetical Creq=0.5 μMC_\mathrm{req}=0.5\,\mu\mathrm M and α=0.02 μM−1\alpha=0.02\,\mu\mathrm M^{-1}, ratios Kd/S=10,1,0.1,0.01K_d/S=10,1,0.1,0.01 require protein fractions 0.11, 0.02, 0.011 and 0.0101, respectively.

A simple growth comparison makes the remaining assumptions explicit. If the binder occupies otherwise allocatable protein and leaves both sector efficiencies unchanged, the zero-offset two-sector model gives growth relative to the no-binder optimum as 1−ϕD1-\phi_D. The four fractions above then retain 0.89, 0.98, 0.989 and 0.9899 of that reference growth. Additional synthesis costs or benefits of the bound product would change this comparison.

Specify the required complex abundance and the controlled ligand pool. Translate concentration to protein fraction before claiming a growth cost, and retain the stoichiometric floor.

3Identify which capacity an improvement can change

A better component changes usable output only while its capacity limits that output. Shunkang examines a minimum of several capacities. An abstract example with normalized units is

Jsource=min⁡(4,10E),Juse=min⁡(ηJsource,3),E≥0.J_\mathrm{source}=\min(4,10E),\qquad J_\mathrm{use}=\min(\eta J_\mathrm{source},3),\qquad E\geq0.

Here EE controls one source capacity and η\eta is a conversion efficiency. All unlisted capacities are assumed nonlimiting. At η=0.70\eta=0.70, increasing EE raises useful output with slope 7 below E=0.4E=0.4. Beyond that point, useful output remains 2.8. The left and right derivatives at the change are 7 and 0. There is no single derivative there.

Increasing the efficiency to η=0.75\eta=0.75 raises the plateau to 3, where the final capacity becomes limiting. Once that capacity is reached, further improvement in the source cannot raise the declared output. A central finite difference at a change of limiting capacity can average two slopes and conceal this distinction.

Find the active constraint before estimating the benefit of a component improvement. At a change of limiting capacity, report the behavior on each side.

4Identify kinetic rates through a calibrated observation model

A time-dependent binding signal can leave its two rate constants unidentified. Xinyu Wu asks which measurements distinguish association from dissociation. For initially unoccupied sites, clamped free ligand SS, and elementary association and dissociation rate constants k+,k−>0k_+,k_->0, let the measured signal be

y(t)=Fk+Sk+S+k−(1−e−(k+S+k−)t).y(t)=F\frac{k_+S}{k_+S+k_-}\left(1-e^{-(k_+S+k_-)t}\right).

The positive scale FF converts occupied fraction to signal units and is common across the compared measurements. One ideal curve identifies its observed amplitude and relaxation rate r=k+S+k−r=k_+S+k_-. If FF is unknown, those are only two constraints on three parameters.

For example, at S=1 μMS=1\,\mu\mathrm M, parameter triples (k+,k−,F)=(1,1,1)(k_+,k_-,F)=(1,1,1) and (0.5,1.5,2)(0.5,1.5,2) give exactly the same curve. Association rates use μM−1s−1\mu\mathrm M^{-1}\mathrm s^{-1}, dissociation rates use s−1\mathrm s^{-1}, and scale uses signal units. At S=4 μMS=4\,\mu\mathrm M, both the amplitudes and relaxation rates separate.

Two distinct known concentrations identify the rates from their relaxation constants. For S2≠S1S_2\ne S_1,

k+=r2−r1S2−S1,k−=r1−k+S1.k_+=\frac{r_2-r_1}{S_2-S_1},\qquad k_-=r_1-k_+S_1.

Calibration supplies another route. If FF is known, one ideal curve gives the occupied-fraction plateau A=y(∞)/FA=y(\infty)/F. Then k+=Ar/Sk_+=Ar/S and k−=(1−A)rk_-=(1-A)r. Thus the need for a second concentration depends on what the observation model leaves unknown.

Identifiability does not guarantee useful precision with finite noisy samples. An editorial Fisher-information calculation uses 120 total observations, divided equally between two concentrations, with 60 logarithmically spaced times from 0.01 to 10 seconds for each. Errors are independent additive Gaussian variables with standard deviation 0.02 signal units. The reference parameters are k+=k−=F=1k_+=k_-=F=1 in the units above. The common scale is estimated with the rates.

The information estimate asks how much each noisy observation changes under a small parameter change. Let θ\theta contain the logarithms of the three parameters divided by their reference values, and let Jij=∂yi/∂θjJ_{ij}=\partial y_i/\partial\theta_j. With independent error standard deviation σy=0.02\sigma_y=0.02, the local information matrix and covariance approximation are

I=JTJσy2,Cov⁡(θ^)≃I−1.\mathcal I=\frac{J^TJ}{\sigma_y^2},\qquad \operatorname{Cov}(\widehat\theta)\simeq\mathcal I^{-1}.

Square roots of the first two diagonal entries approximate the relative standard errors of the rates. At one concentration the information matrix has rank two. Two distinct concentrations give full rank for the declared design.

Holding the geometric mean concentration at 1 μM1\,\mu\mathrm M, the local predicted relative standard error of k−k_- is smallest at a concentration ratio of about 6.24. The concentrations are about 0.400 and 2.498 micromolar, and the predicted relative standard errors are about 2.51% for association and 3.69% for dissociation. These are local information estimates for this noise model and sampling schedule. They are not a universal spacing rule or measured fitting errors.

Write the signal calibration into the model. Separate uniqueness with ideal observations from precision under the actual sampling and noise assumptions.

5Include the material load imposed by a binding readout

The clamped ligand assumption removes a possible effect of the readout on its input. Zixuan Wang instead supplies a finite free signal XX that binds to readout sites PP. Let qP=P+CXPq_P=P+C_{XP} be a fixed site total. The elementary pair and its net binding flux are

X+P⇌k−k+CXP,vbind=k+X(qP−CXP)−k−CXP.X+P\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{XP},\qquad v_\mathrm{bind}=k_+X(q_P-C_{XP})-k_-C_{XP}.

The signal is supplied at rate u(t)u(t) and removed only when free, at rate δX\delta X. The balances are

X˙=u(t)−δX−vbind,C˙XP=vbind,ddt(X+CXP)=u(t)−δX.\dot X=u(t)-\delta X-v_\mathrm{bind},\qquad \dot C_{XP}=v_\mathrm{bind},\qquad \frac{d}{dt}(X+C_{XP})=u(t)-\delta X.

The total balance shows why binding can slow the free signal without changing its eventual value. At a steady state with constant supply, X∗=u/δX^*=u/\delta. During a rise, however, some supplied signal must fill the bound pool. This is a material example of retroactivity, the influence a connected downstream system exerts on its upstream signal.3

A fast-binding reduction must retain that changing bound pool. With Kd=k−/k+K_d=k_-/k_+, the equilibrium reconstruction is CXP≃qPX/(Kd+X)C_{XP}\simeq q_PX/(K_d+X). Substituting it into the total balance and differentiating gives

(1+qPKd(Kd+X)2)X˙≃u(t)−δX.\left(1+\frac{q_PK_d}{(K_d+X)^2}\right)\dot X \simeq u(t)-\delta X.

The added factor is the derivative of bound signal with respect to free signal. Setting the binding flux identically to zero in the free-signal balance would lose this factor. Near the new equilibrium, the reduced relaxation time is

trelax=1δ(1+qPKd(Kd+X∗)2).t_\mathrm{relax}=\frac1\delta\left(1+\frac{q_PK_d}{(K_d+X^*)^2}\right).

An illustrative step compares site totals 0, 20 and 80 nanomolar. Take k+=1 nM−1min−1k_+=1\,\mathrm{nM}^{-1}\mathrm{min}^{-1}, k−=10 min−1k_-=10\,\mathrm{min}^{-1} and δ=0.1 min−1\delta=0.1\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}. Supply rises from 0.2 to 1 nanomolar per minute. Each system starts at its own old equilibrium, with free signal 2 nanomolar and the corresponding occupied sites.

All three free signals approach 10 nanomolar. The full binding equations take about 23.03, 40.05 and 91.05 minutes, respectively, to complete 90% of the change. The reduced balance predicts 23.03, 40.01 and 90.98 minutes. Its local relaxation times are 10, 15 and 30 minutes. Those local times do not make the entire step response a single exponential.

The endpoint conclusion depends on the removal mechanism. If bound signal is also removed at rate δCCXP\delta_C C_{XP}, steady supply must instead balance δX+δCCXP\delta X+\delta_C C_{XP}. Connecting more sites can then change the free-signal endpoint as well. The boundary and the observable belong in the component specification.

Derive the total material balance before eliminating fast binding. Compare the connected and unconnected systems using both their endpoint and response time.

6Specify a measurement that distinguishes the remaining explanations

A useful component claim ties the proposed improvement to an observable in the connected system. The audience's questions supply five distinct checks:

ClaimNeeded specification or comparisonRemaining alternative
A selected candidate performs better.Evaluate the required function with evidence independent of selection.The selected score contains unusually favorable error.
Stronger binding reduces burden.Hold the required complex abundance and ligand boundary fixed. Measure the required protein amount.Expression approaches a stoichiometric floor.
A better component raises output.Identify the active source and downstream capacity constraints.Another capacity already limits usable output.
A time course identifies two rates.Declare calibration, concentration and sampling. Check uniqueness and precision separately.An unknown signal scale hides a rate tradeoff.
A readout leaves its input unchanged.Compare free and bound signal, endpoint and transient after connection.Site occupancy changes the material demand or removal balance.

A concrete next question follows from the last two calculations. If a binding curve changes after connecting a readout, can the change be explained by additional occupied sites while the microscopic rates remain fixed? Joint free- and bound-signal trajectories, compared under the same supply boundary, can distinguish that explanation from a change in affinity. The component specification then becomes a test of a mechanism.

Choose the next measurement by the competing explanations it can separate. Carry the same boundary, units and readout through design, characterization and connection.
