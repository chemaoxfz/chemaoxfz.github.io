When has a molecular circuit completed a decision?

A useful answer needs a readout, a threshold, a deadline and a rule for what input history counts.

CCBS · Lecture 12 audience extension
Editorial synthesis of six Week 6 student contributions · prepared with Codex assistance · 3 November 2026

A correct stationary truth table is only one part of a molecular decision. The reporter may consume the signal. Connected gates may settle at different times. A nearly tied input may take a long time to resolve. A stored first cue may remain available after that cue disappears.

The core develops molecular logic, binding comparisons and retained expression choices. The audience contributions turn those constructions into questions about when their output can be used. We follow a decision from measurement through settling, selection and memory. The exposition supplies the underlying concentration balances and state-selection model.

Names link to accepted submissions. Numerical trajectories below are independently generated teaching calculations. Published observations are identified separately.

1Include the reporter when calculating a decision threshold

A finite reporter can move the threshold it is intended to reveal. Kehan Lang quantifies this effect for a repressor SS binding a reporter site PP. Resolve the reversible elementary pair and its conserved totals:

S+P⇌k−k+CSP,Kd=k−k+,qS=S+CSP,qP=P+CSP.S+P\xrightleftharpoons[k_-]{k_+}C_{SP},\qquad K_d=\frac{k_-}{k_+},\qquad q_S=S+C_{SP},\quad q_P=P+C_{SP}.

Let the readout be the unbound reporter fraction r=P/qPr=P/q_P, for qP>0q_P>0. At equilibrium, r=Kd/(Kd+S)r=K_d/(K_d+S). To find the total repressor needed for a specified fraction 0<θ<10<\theta<1, first solve for free repressor, then restore the bound pool:

Sθ=Kd1−θθ,qS,θ=Kd1−θθ+(1−θ)qP.S_\theta=K_d\frac{1-\theta}{\theta},\qquad q_{S,\theta}=K_d\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}+(1-\theta)q_P.

At half output, the total threshold is Kd+qP/2K_d+q_P/2. Four dissociation-constant units of reporter therefore move it from one to three. Relative to the dilute-reporter threshold, the fractional shift is θqP/Kd\theta q_P/K_d. A tolerated shift of ε\varepsilon requires qP≤εKd/θq_P\leq\varepsilon K_d/\theta in this model.

The current exposition already includes finite reporter loading. Kehan's extension makes the acceptable error explicit for an arbitrary fractional threshold. Other downstream binding pools would add further terms to the total balance. Expression burden and detector calibration require additional models.

Specify whether the input is a free concentration or a total. Derive the threshold after including every modeled binding pool that consumes the signal.

2Test a connected gate during its input transition

A connected circuit can produce a transient pulse even when its old and new truth-table outputs agree. Leo Sin tests the standard four-NAND construction of XOR. For inputs a,b∈{0,1}a,b\in\{0,1\}, define intermediate outputs n1,n2,n3n_1,n_2,n_3 and final output zz. Each gate has normalized target 1−uv1-uv and one common relaxation time tgatet_\mathrm{gate}:

n1′=1−ab−n1,n2′=1−an1−n2,n3′=1−bn1−n3,z′=1−n2n3−z.n_1'=1-ab-n_1,\quad n_2'=1-an_1-n_2,\quad n_3'=1-bn_1-n_3,\quad z'=1-n_2n_3-z.

Primes denote time τ=t/tgate\tau=t/t_\mathrm{gate}. This is a continuous gate model, not a microscopic chemical reaction list. Before a simultaneous transition from 00 to 11, the settled state is (n1,n2,n3,z)=(1,1,1,0)(n_1,n_2,n_3,z)=(1,1,1,0). Both input pairs have XOR output zero. After the transition, sequential solution gives

n1=e−τ,n2=n3=1−τe−τ,z=e−τ(τ2−2)+e−2τ(τ2+2τ+2).n_1=e^{-\tau},\qquad n_2=n_3=1-\tau e^{-\tau},\qquad z=e^{-\tau}(\tau^2-2)+e^{-2\tau}(\tau^2+2\tau+2).

The final output rises to about 0.454 at τ=2.069\tau=2.069, then returns to zero. A decoder threshold at 0.5 would not classify this pulse as one. A lower threshold could. The discrepancy is therefore a finite-time analog excursion whose logical consequence depends on the declared decoder.

Propagation delays matter in measured genetic circuits too. Nielsen and colleagues reported a transient incorrect state in one four-layer Cello circuit when its inputs changed. Their Figure 4B attributes the transient to signals arriving through different numbers of layers. That experimental circuit is distinct from the equal-time NAND model above.1

Test the full input transition and the downstream decoder. A pulse amplitude becomes a logical error only relative to a specified threshold and observation time.

3Calculate how a near tie delays a molecular majority decision

A correct eventual winner need not be available by a fixed deadline. Chengwei Jin analyzes the majority mechanism from the exposition. Its assumed elementary reactions share a rate constant k>0k>0:

X+Y→k2Z,Z+X→k2X,Z+Y→k2Y.X+Y\xrightarrow{k}2Z,\qquad Z+X\xrightarrow{k}2X,\qquad Z+Y\xrightarrow{k}2Y.

The conserved concentration is qsum=X+Y+Z>0q_\mathrm{sum}=X+Y+Z>0. Define fractions x=X/qsumx=X/q_\mathrm{sum}, y=Y/qsumy=Y/q_\mathrm{sum}, z=Z/qsumz=Z/q_\mathrm{sum} and time τ=kqsumt\tau=kq_\mathrm{sum}t. The majority difference Δ=x−y\Delta=x-y then satisfies

Δ′=zΔ,z′=1−4z+3z2−Δ22.\Delta'=z\Delta,\qquad z'=\frac{1-4z+3z^2-\Delta^2}{2}.

A positive advantage remains positive. Near the equal-mixture fixed point, z≃1/3z\simeq1/3, so a small advantage initially grows approximately as eτ/3e^{\tau/3}. Reaching an order-one advantage from ϵ>0\epsilon>0 therefore takes a leading time 3log⁡(1/ϵ)3\log(1/\epsilon). The approach to and departure from that neighborhood add a finite offset.

A concrete stopping rule makes the delay testable. Start with z(0)=0z(0)=0 and x(0)−y(0)=ϵx(0)-y(0)=\epsilon. Stop when x=0.9x=0.9. Independent integration gives dimensionless times 14.160, 21.068 and 27.976 for advantages 0.02, 0.002 and 0.0002. Each tenfold reduction adds approximately 3log⁡10=6.9083\log10=6.908 time units.

An exact tie is an exceptional input. It remains on x=yx=y and approaches the equal mixture instead of either winning corner. Molecular noise could break a tie in a stochastic model, but the deterministic calculation supplies no probability that noise selects the intended winner. A deadline and a minimum input margin must therefore be part of the deterministic task.

Define a stopping threshold and a minimum input advantage. Convert the resulting delay back to physical time using the conserved concentration and rate constant.

4Distinguish pulse area from successful memory writing

A transient signal writes memory only if it leaves the system in the basin of the desired retained state. Lipei Fang tests whether equal pulse area is enough. Use the exposition's two-factor MultiFate reduction, with totals divided by the promoter response concentration KMK_M and time τ=δreft\tau=\delta_\mathrm{ref}t. For the declared Kd/KM=1K_d/K_M=1,

ϕ=21+1+8(qˉA+qˉB),CˉAA=ϕ2qˉA2,CˉBB=ϕ2qˉB2,h(c)=c1.51+c1.5.\phi=\frac2{1+\sqrt{1+8(\bar q_A+\bar q_B)}},\qquad \bar C_{AA}=\phi^2\bar q_A^2,\quad\bar C_{BB}=\phi^2\bar q_B^2,\qquad h(c)=\frac{c^{1.5}}{1+c^{1.5}}.

The common free fraction follows from homodimer and heterodimer conservation, using the exposition's microscopic convention. Homodimers activate their own expression and heterodimers are inactive. With bounded induction 0≤sB≤10\leq s_B\leq1, the total balances are

qˉA′=0.8+20h(CˉAA)−qˉA,qˉB′=0.8+20[h(CˉBB)+sB(1−h(CˉBB))]−qˉB.\bar q_A'=0.8+20h(\bar C_{AA})-\bar q_A,\qquad \bar q_B'=0.8+20[h(\bar C_{BB})+s_B(1-h(\bar C_{BB}))]-\bar q_B.

Begin at the A-high equilibrium (19.9854,0.8342)(19.9854,0.8342). Compare a pulse of amplitude one lasting 0.30 reference lifetimes with amplitude 0.08 lasting 3.75 lifetimes. Both have area 0.30. Restore sB=0s_B=0 afterward. The short, strong pulse settles to the both-high equilibrium (18.6936,18.6936)(18.6936,18.6936). The long, weak pulse returns to the original A-high equilibrium.

The calculation illustrates the mechanism of writing, not an experimentally calibrated dose or duration. Zhu and colleagues demonstrated controllable, retained expression states in the MultiFate system. Their molecular interventions and measured time units must be calibrated before a dimensionless pulse comparison can predict an experimental protocol.2

Compare complete pulse trajectories from the same initial state. Score the retained state after restoring the same environment, rather than using integrated input as a substitute.

5Specify whether two cues must overlap or may arrive in sequence

Stored molecular activity can change the meaning of an AND requirement. Xiaomeng Zhu asks what happens when a first cue produces a receptor that later detects a second cue. The first cue can disappear while its induced receptor remains. The output then depends on recent input history, rather than only on simultaneous cue values.

A first-order loss model turns that history into a time window. Let qR(t)q_R(t) be induced receptor abundance after the first cue ends. Assume no further production and a constant removal rate δR>0\delta_R>0:

q˙R=−δRqR,qR(t)qR(0)=e−δRt,twindow=log⁡[qR(0)/qR,threshold]δR.\dot q_R=-\delta_R q_R,\qquad \frac{q_R(t)}{q_R(0)}=e^{-\delta_Rt},\qquad t_\mathrm{window}=\frac{\log[q_R(0)/q_{R,\mathrm{threshold}}]}{\delta_R}.

The last expression requires a specified positive abundance threshold below the initial level. It is a receptor-abundance window. Converting that window into an activation probability or a cellular action requires another response model.

Roybal and colleagues measured roughly eight-hour decay of induced receptor expression after priming was removed in their Jurkat-cell assay. That reported timescale motivates an illustration: an exponential with eight-hour half-life retains 70.7% after four hours and 12.5% after 24 hours. The fitted assay scale is not a universal primary-T-cell half-life, and the abundance fractions do not predict killing or clinical safety.3

State whether the task requires simultaneous cues or a permitted sequence. Measure the persistence of the intermediate that carries the first cue's history.

6Assign computational meaning through a specified input-output task

A physical process becomes evidence for a computational claim only after the task and encoding are stated. Runzi Yan raises this question through light-harvesting energy transport. Fast transport, coherence and a high destination population describe different physical properties. None by itself specifies which alternative inputs are being distinguished or which readout counts as the correct answer.

The quantities in a transport model must also retain their definitions. In the FMO study discussed by Runzi, the quantity labeled a decoherence half-time is operationally defined through the loss of half the initially excited pigment population. The reported transport efficiency is the asymptotic sum of populations on two exit-associated pigments. Those definitions do not make the quantities a direct half-life of the full coherence measure or an independently counted reaction-center trapping yield. The distinction is visible in the paper's section 4.4, equation 37 and Table 2.4

Computation does not require quantum coherence, and a fixed circuit can compute without being reprogrammable. The majority circuit above demonstrates the relevant standard. Its input is an initial concentration ordering, its state evolution preserves that ordering, and its output is an attracting pool allocation. Its delay and tie case are explicit. A transport-based computing proposal needs an equally explicit account of its input alternatives and decoded outputs.

The next test can combine the chapter's first three questions. Add a finite reporter to a molecular majority circuit. Vary both the initial majority margin and reporter abundance. Determine whether a single calibrated threshold and deadline still classify the intended winner. That experiment tests the decision after measurement has become part of its mechanism.

Define the input alternatives, their physical encoding, the output decoder and the deadline. Then test the mechanism against that task, including loading and exceptional inputs.
